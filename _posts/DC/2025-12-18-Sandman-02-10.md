Love the short moment between Cluracan and Nuala. For once we finally see Cluracan say something true and genuine and see how vulnerable and lonely and scared he is. He's Nuala's brother. (How did he forget that when he gave Nuala away as a gift to Morpheus?)

The death scene of Abel and Merv, the shot with his pumpkin head smashed, the somber music that goes with it... The pain and hurt is building and building and building.

<br>

> Morpheus: The Eagle Stone will protect all of you while I'm away.
>
> Corinthian: So you will come back?
>
> Morpheus: I must prepare for all eventualities.
>
> Lucienne: Then this is goodbye.
>
> Morpheus: No. This is an opportunity for me to say... thank you. For everything. Always. I will not forget you. But forgive me, for I must send you all away now.

This is goodbye. Just don't say goodbye.

They're crying. First time seeing Lucienne crying, first time seeing Morpheus crying. The death is not the hardest part, the parting and never seeing again is.

At least, he had his proper goodbye (and thank you) with the most important friends: Lucienne, Matthew, even Corinthian, and Cain. But not with all his brothers and sisters, he did meet with Destiny, Delirium, Death, Despair and Destruction. So the only he didn't see is just Desire.

Love Matthew so much for insisting to go with him, to die with him. Dying isn't the scary part, he'd do anything to make Morpheus not die alone, with the enemies. At least, to the very least comfort, we know he has at least a friend with him as he goes.

<br>

> Lucienne: *He* missed you.
>
> Corinthian: Did he?
>
> Lucienne: I think you broke his heart when you ran off to the waking world.
>
> Corinthian: I think I probably meant to.

Thank you, that's the split second of Morpheus/Corinthian feelings I can use.

<br>

His last words, so beautiful, especially when they're layered with the muted music from the song of Orpheus. It almost sounds like Orpheus is crying for him from the underworld, singing the song that makes Hades cry, singing the song to let the music accompany his father as he prepares his own death, because of helping him.

> Death: You know I've been worried about you.
>
> Morpheus: The last time we had this conversation, you threw a loaf of bread at me.
>
> Death: I remember.
>
> Morpheus: Are you going to shout at me?
>
> Death: It's a bit late for that, little brother. I will say though, I've known you longer than anybody. And you've been down much father than this in the past, and you've come back. Why not come back?
>
> Morpheus: I killed my son. Twice. Once when I would not help him, and once when I did. I knew what I was doing. I knew what the cost would be, and... The Dreaming is no longer tha same. I am no longer the same. I still have my obligations, but even the freedom of the Dreaming can be a cage of a kind.
>
> Death: You could have left. Destruction left. You could have done that.
>
> Morpheus: No. I could not.
>
> Death: No. You couldn't, could you?

Morpheus loves the Dreaming too much to leave it. He loves his son too much to see him suffer more from the immortality. Even if the cost is his own death, he couldn't leave them, he couldn't not do the right thing.

Destruction said, everything he chooses, he should do it out of love. And Morpheus is doing exactly that, all the time, for all his existence. He doesn't change, he's always been like that, don't you see? He refused to help his son back then because of love, because he can't lose him; he helped him then, because of love, because he can't see him suffer. He left the Dreaming the first time when he was captured because he needs to hunt down Corinthian; and he brings Corinthian back even after the first version of him disappointed him, but he still thinks him as his finest creation. He allows Matthew to come with him, because he knows that's what Matthew wanted; and he sends Matthew away to bring Death to him, because he can't let him get harmed. He sends everyone away, to where he thinks is the safest, and heads to his death alone. He lived his whole existence out of love, even though he never get any from his mother and father.

The story of Endless, is the story of Love. Because only Love is endless.

<br>

I don't know if it's fortunate or disfortunate to have Death as your sibling. At least, the last comfort of dying, is to know it's just to leave with your sister.

I can't imagine what's it like for Death, knowing one day, sooner or later, she'll take everyone away, all of her siblings, she'll be there for them, and then, at the end of the universe when everyone has left and the light has gone out, it will just be her alone. Did anyone ask her how lonely she'd be?

<br>

The song at the end of this episode, it's probably the most powerful song among all the episodes.

We saw the new Dream now. Daniel grew to be 20s from an eight-month-old baby just the moment Morpheus is gone (just like Jack did in spn). Too much of a surprise to digest, too depressed to think that means Morpheus is really gone. Too much information and emotion all at once. Really didn't expect the new Dream would actually be a (metauniversely) familiar face from *Interviews with Vampire* (even if I haven't watched it yet). His charm and loving longing eyes from those other places have already interfered with my impression of him. I don't know what to expect of him, I don't know if I want to like him or not, like anyone else survived in the Dreaming. He's not Morpheus, but he's the Dream. How would Dreaming ever be same again without Morpheus? Even I want to cry, I can't imagine how much pain it would be for all those who know Morpheus, his friends and family, or even acquitance.
