Love Morpheus's performance whenever Nada is involved, even though the emotional connection between him and Nada is still a little out of place, can't really feel the love or chemistry between them, not as much as Morpheus and Calliope in Season 1's bonus episodes. Morpheus's displayed feelings and care for her feel like more for the plot than for the emotion. But every time Nada in involved, Morpheus performed so shaky and shocked and can barely hold himself together, so vulnerable and on edge again. Especially obvious when Azazel threatened Morpheus with Nada's life and soul, and Morpheus is literally shaking on his throne, but he's still not giving in to Azazel, that performance is just amazing to see. So shaky, so vulnerable, so broken, so perfect.

Same for every time he eagerly asked Lucienne if he can visit Nada (after Nada is saved from Azazel, and by the way the way Morpheus escape and combine Azazel, that scene of him being inside Azazel and the next shot is him so much bigger from the outside, is a Deja Vu from season 1 when he was fighting against John for his ruby, it's an amazing shot transition), he's so eager and worried and vulnerable, just like he's nothing more than a young boy worried for his lover, these moments can convince me he really cared for Nada. Or rather, at least he believe he loved her.

He said "I'm sorry" to Nada. Something the old Morpheus wouldn't have said. But after season 1, he's indeed changed.

<br>

The feast scene and plot officially wrapped up in this episode, with all the guests leaving. A really nice and enjoyable plot to see, even though I don't think this is the best writing to have such a plot for this story. So here's the conflict feeling about this season (which remains for many more episodes, and ultimately extend to the whole season): it doesn't feel like a very good show, not the best storytelling or the best writing, but I love it, and I enjoyed it a lot.

Love the part with Shakespeare's *Midsummer night's dream* show and the faeries and the first appearance of Puck (aka Robin Goodfellow), a very memorable character with very few shots and lines, a very successful depiction. And a very familiar face. Took me a while to remember where I've seen him before, turns out to be Joffrey from Game of Thrones. Look like Sandman love Game of Thrones actors.

But really can't appreciate Morpheus's long hair, it just looks so weird.

<br>

Love the fighting scene near the end between Morpheus and Nada, with Nada gives him a strike on the face:

> Morpheus: I love you.
>
> Nada: No, you do not. I know you *believe* you do. But I wonder if your kind is even capable of love.
>
> Morpheus: How can you say that?
>
> Nada: How could anyone who truly loved me do what you've done to me?
>
> Morpheus: I have changed.
>
> Nada: It is the same as the last time I refused you. I think perhaps you merely want what you cannot have. That is not love. That is desire.
>
> Morpheus: Desire?
>
> Nada: There is no shame in it.
>
> Morpheus: No. You cannot tell me what it is in my heart. I love you.
>
> Nada: But I do not love you.

"I wonder if your kind is even capable of love", this one strikes me hard, this is just another lonely unloved nonhuman character, so much older and bigger than human, can't understand love, accused by human of not even capable of loving, just like destiel (can't remember if it's canon or fic where Dean wonders aloud about angels: wondering if they're even capable of love, it's like every time they try to love, they make things even worse).

The unhuman aspect of the story, the older and more powerful character that is too far away from human life that can't understand human emotions, can't understand *love*, I feel so resonated with them. As if I'm one of them. Or just that I can't understand human just like they do, even if I'm one. It's difficult to understand and to love, sometimes I wonder the same, if I'm even capable of loving, or maybe I believe I do, but what I'm feeling is not really love.

"You merely want what you cannot have, that is not love, that is desire." That is such a nice quote to have, I'm gonna steal it and maybe use it somewhere in my story. The longing and wanting part may or may not be a part of love, or maybe that's just desire, but it's so difficult to tell the difference, and it hurts so much to hear from the person you love that they don't even think what you are feeling is love at all. And even more heartbroken, that they don't forgive you for the harm you did to them, and they declare they do not love you. I'm a whore for hurt/comfort and whump, and I get them all in Sandman.
