Love the relationship between Orpheus and Johanna Constantine. Love the lines Constantine tells Morpheus about Orpheus:

> Morpheus: How is he?
>
> Constantine: He asked me the same about you.
>
> Morpheus: And you said nothing, of course.
>
> Constantine: I didn't answer, of course.
>
> Constantine: He says he wishes to die.
>
> Morpheus: He once said the same to me.
>
> Constantine: I suppose I can't blame him. After all he's suffered. But even in his current state, he is... He's more alive than anyone I've ever met. He jokes at his own expense. He's curious. He asks questions, and more remarkably, he listens to the answers. He even offers to sing for me.

I think, hearing what Johanna says about Orpheus, even if Morpheus still haven't made up his mind, now he's even closer to the decision of agreeing to giving Orpheus what he wants. Hearing how alive he is and how suffered he is, it touches me, I don't believe it didn't touch Morpheus just as strongly.

<br>

The moment Morpheus and Orpheus meet and talk and apologize, I'm starting to cry all over again, no matter how many times I have watched. I cry for them, I cry for myself, I cry. Love, and family, and hurt and pain, and worthiness and deserve, and forgiveness, it's too much, I can't figure them out, I can't understand, I don't know what's the right thing to feel or to think, I feel for them but mostly I think I'm feeling for myself. And I think and think and think, and I still can't figure it out. It's the most powerful and mysterious thing I can't figure out, love and family and pain and past and time and future. I'm afraid to be too late, and I'm afraid to be hurt again. I'm afraid, I'm always so afraid, I'm afraid I did the wrong thing, I'm still doing the wrong, I'm afraid I can never forgive myself or walk out of my past, I'm afraid guilt will haunt me forever, so is self-hatred. Maybe it's just me trying to stay feeling sorry for myself, just like Morpheus was, for a long time, before he gets slapped awake to make changes. Who is there to wake me up?

>
> Orpheus: Hello, Father.
>
> Morpheus just stares at him.
>
> Orpheus: You look... just like yourself. You needn't describe what I look like.
>
> Morpheus: You look like my son.
>
> Orpheus: Father, I am so sorry.
>
> Morpheus: Mm. It is *I* who am sorry.
>
> Orpheus: No. You were trying to protect me. I know that now.
>
> Morpheus: I abandoned you.
>
> Orpheus: You never abandoned me. You sent Andros, and his father, and his father's father, to look after me. You sent Johanna Constantine. And you're here with me now.
>
> Morpheus: I'm not worthy of you, my son.
>
> Orpheus: Why would you say that? What's happened? When last we saw one another, you said we would never meet again. So something must have happened.
>
> Morpheus: Many things. Where even to begin?
>
> Orpheus: Why not the beginning? I have nowhere to go. (chuckle) Do you?

"I'm not worthy of you, my son." The whisper. It tears my heart. My tears can't stop.

It seems, "worthy" and "deserve" are my two greatest tear triggers now.

I cry whenever someone doesn't think they deserve or worthy of something, especially love, especially someone else that they love so much about. I can see so clearly they are all worthy, they all deserve, but it's so impossible to see it in myself, to be convinced. It pains me to think like them, to feel like them, to feel their pain and guilt and the feeling of unworthiness and unloved, to feel them like feel myself until I can't tell what's mine and what's theirs.

<br>

The conversation when Morpheus and Delirium find Destruction seem to emphasize very heavily on the key word "change", it's repeated a little too many times than one would like, certainly not very easy to ignore now. Especially not when the very memorable destiel line also has "you changed me", "I cared about the world because you care", and with the Endless, they also have something very similar:

> Morpheus: How did you know we were coming?
>
> Destruction: I set up certain... mechanisms when I left. I mean, nothing fancy. There's a scrying pool in the shed out back. It lets me know if people have been looking.
>
> Morpheus: These *mechanisms*... might they have harmed those who helped us to find you?
>
> Destruction: I'm afraid so. There was nothing I could do once you started looking for me. Not without resuming my function once more.
>
> Morpheus: But the people who died... some of them were your friends.
>
> Destruction: One of them was once my lover.
>
> Morpheus: Ishtar.
>
> Destruction: You never cared for her. You seem to care now.
>
> Morpheus: I care that you continue to hurt those you claim to love.
>
> Destruction: Who among us has not done that?
>
> Morpheus: I care that innocent people have lost their lives simply because we were looking for you. It was not just that they should die.
>
> Destruction: (scoffs) Three hundred years ago, you would have told me that they were simply mortal. And would have died later had they not died then. You've changed, Dream.
>
> Morpheus: I doubt that. I still perform my responsibilities, after all.
>
> Destruction: I don't. But you know that.
>
> Morpheus: And how do you justify that? Leaving us? Killing your friends? Abandoning your realm? For this? (scoffs)

> Morpheus: Will you return with us?
>
> Destruction: No. Of course not. (to a very upset Delirium) I'm sorry, lad.

> Morpheus: And what of your realm?
>
> Destruction: I'm sure it's still there. People and things are still created, still exist, they're still destroyed. Things still change. The only difference is that no one's running it anymore. (sighs) They can make their own destruction. It's not my responsibility. And it's not my fault. You know, I think I knew that it was time to leave this place. Even before you started looking for me. Life was... comfortable. Unchanging. And you know what they say -- You change or you die. Plus, can't have Mom and Dad and the rest of the family turning up, guilting me into going back, can I? Not that I haven't loved seeing you both.

"change", now suddenly everyone starts talking about change. Of course season 1 also appeared a few times, a few people telling Morpheus he's changed, who wouldn't, after being held captive for a hundred years? He's be in the waking world too long, being among humans too long, and the pursuits afterwards are just making him see more about human, feel human, be sentimental like human.

Interesting how this is the very first time we learn that Endless also has Mom and Dad (who we'll see later, we have to, otherwise, don't show the gun in Act 1 if you don't plan to use it in Act 4).

Still think Destruction looks way too alike Hemsworth brother, it's making it difficult to focus on what he's performing. He looks more like a Thor or just a wandering lover than Destruction. His Australian accent certainly helps (or counter-helps) that.

<br>

Now that I think about it, even before this episode, maybe near the end of last episode, when Morpheus agrees to go with Delirium to continue searching for Destruction, back when they're still in Destiny's garden, he already makes his decision. He knows what Orpheus will ask of him, and if he wants to get anything from him, he'll have to grant his wish, and by doing so, he'll doom his own death. But even knowing those, he still goes in that direction with Delirium, because it's what Delirium wants.

There's a line there: "Those who seek Destruction often find it."

He deliberately seeks Destruction, and he can already see his own unavoidable destruction in the very near future. Yet he still makes the decision.

Love how the character and personification and abstract concept all weave together and fit into it perfectly again. Love the quiet determination of going toward his own death, because he thinks it's the right thing to do, just like in the beginning of this season, he believes it's the right thing to do to free Nada, so no matter how scared he is, no matter how likely he thinks he would die in Hell, he still goes to Lucifer; and how, he believes it's the right thing to do to find Destruction for Delirium, right thing to let Orpheus out of his misery and gets the death he wanted for so long to reunite with his wife, so he doesn't mind his own death, doesn't mind the consequence, he just goes toward it. He looks so light of his own life, yet so heavy of his responsibility. He cares about so many others, the justice of the things he do, but so little about himself. I pain for him. Is it because he's never so deeply loved by someone the same way he loves others and the world, even if he doesn't realize it's his way of loving, even if he can't recognize it when others are also loving him just maybe not the way he expected? He's so much bigger than human, yet also so so very human. Love him even more by every step he takes toward his own destruction. Love him more after losing him.

<br>

"You change or you die" is also so foreshadowing. But for Morpheus, even with so much change after so many millions of years, he still had to die. Why? Why does it have to be him?

<br>

The scene with blood dripping down his arm, remember it so clearly from the comic. It's so much more vivid in my mind's eyes when I read the comic, yet it's still a powerful enough scene to feel shocking and shaky and all the shuddering emotions.

Morpheus's sobbing and shaking, maybe a tiny little bit of difficult to relate because we're not the ones who have to kill their son, but I still pain for him. "I was scared to live in a world without you," I guess that the words of any parent in the world (who loved their child), but I still wouldn't expect to hear that from Morpheus. I guess I never realized how human he is, how much emotion he's able to feel, and how truly deeply he loves his son.

And then the inevitable. We know it will happen, Morpheus knows it will happen. The prophecy starts fulfilling, the Fates start chasing him. When Morpheus said "the journey is at end", he already knows he signed his own death certificate, this is the beginning of the end for him.

Six episodes, that's as far as we got for the good half, and the remaining half, is all going to be the heartbroken foreshadowing death-chasing ones. I'm scared, even to continue. I know how much it's going to hurt. Moving from here, there's only dread and heartbroken left.
