### S02E01

The beginning of the show, the starting point of this season, feels a little enforced. Even though it's exactly how the comic starts the second book too. Destiny receives message from the Fate Sisters, so that everything is following what Destiny's book and the Fate's prophecy say, thus the family gathering, thus the going to Hell to save Nada, thus the key, and everything else in the chain reaction. All very destiny and fate, too destined, too passive, almost like *Merlin*, no one is really making their own choices, or maybe they think they are, but everything else is just pushing them forward, toward their death, like a big vortex swallowing them in. No free will freedom at all, that's just not my favorite type of story.

First time seeing Delirium, love how young and cute she is. Her two eyes have different colors! One blue and one green, so very comic, so pretty. Love how delirious she is. Wondering why delirium is also one of the Endless, I can understand Dream, Death, Despair, Desire, Destiny, which are all very straightforwardly essential to human life, Delirium just doesn't seem very straightforward. It's like saying everyone is bound to have a crazy, chaotic side -- which, come to think of it, is actually quite true and accurate. Who doesn't have a chaotic impulsive moment? But she's definitely not among the top five in my mind when thinking about what's most common things that all humans have (that also starts with "D", maybe that's the major constraints here).

Love the cinematography, the first episode has some very deliberate lighting and coloring of the image to give a half-half lighting and shadowing on Morpheus's face. Think season 1 already did that a few times, like when he was held captive, the framing and lighting puts his face entirely in shadow and only show the light from his eyes, very deliberately recreate the famous image from the comic. And now, the frame when Morpheus was rebuilding his palace and castle, when he stood against the window, half his face is completely black, half in light. It's just so astonishingly beautiful and mysterious, and a little depressing (just like his whole character always is), and so comic-feeling. So well-done that the filming can replicate comic in such detailed level. The visual is amazing to see. And if I remember correctly, this happens many other places too in this season.

<br>

When Morpheus made his mind to go to Hell and knew it's likely a death trip, he might not come back, so he went on a suicidal farewell trip (just like Dean every time before he started a suicidal mission; so very neutral about this whole death thing, is it just like going to stay with your sister forever, Morpheus? What's it like for an Endless to die forever?). He visited Daniel first, his successor, the one that's almost like his son but not quite (and Lyta doesn't welcome him, still thinking he'd kill them).

The very second one he visited is Hob Gadling. I totally didn't realize how many people ship Morpheus/Hob until I check AO3 stats, I'd thought it might be similar to Morpheus/Corinthian (which I prefer more, personally, and unsurprisingly another tragic story in many ways, but fascinating), but turns out to be about ten times more. And after rewatching Season 1 just recently, I can see where it comes from. It's so much more in TV than in comic, and it's all contribute to the actor's eyes. Those longing eyes and looks Hob gave Morpheus, the heartbreak after 1889 when Morpheus stormed out and Hob thinking maybe he made the mistake, and the miserable 1989 when Morpheus didn't show up, and the joy in 2020 when Hob waited in the pub for 31 years and Morpheus finally showed up, and those smiles between them, the "friend", it's so easy to ship them, at least from Hob to Morpheus, the longing looks like so much more and has so much potentials. It looks like Morpheus sees him mostly just a friend, which is also something rare and probably never happen before, but Hob sees him in so many different ways, he doesn't dare to reach out or remotely hope for anything more, since 1889 just merely mentioning the possibility that Morpheus might see him as a friend made him storm out, how could he even hope for anything more than friend. But maybe, possibly, for the TV version at least, from the way he acts and looks and stares and longs, it does look like he wants something more but doesn't dare ask or think.

Comes back to Season 2, when Morpheus comes to say goodbye, and brings him a good old dream-wine, and I think Hob also knows it's Morpheus's farewell, since he did mention "away for a very long time", and Hob understands "a very long time" for an Endless does literally mean a *very* long time, which is essentially eternal, forever, so this is their farewell. Love Hob's toast so much, he said it's going to be a good one, and it indeed is: "To absent friends, lost loves, old gods, and the season of mists. And may each and every one of us always give the devil his due." The final very long stare between them, especially Morpheus's stare, it has so many things in their eyes, it looks almost as if Morpheus longs to stay and be there with Hob just as much as Hob wants him to stay. But Morpheus, as he is, just always put his duty above everything, he doesn't allow himself to live for himself, always has to live for the world and humanity, as his duty requests, so he has to go. Maybe knowing Hob, allowing Hob to live forever is the only selfish (or rather, just self-facing) thing he's ever done in his life. They're really indeed really really good friends, even if it comes into shipping, the platonic nature of the friendship could be so so beautiful and profound. I totally see where the shipping comes from now.

<br>

*I could turn back. Abandon my quest. Live with the knowledge that I have hurt those who I professed to love. No. We do as we must do. "Welcome to Hell," I tell myself. And I'm afraid.*

Love the opening of the episode as much as the closing, start and end with Morpheus's monologue. Love his voice, his sound. Love this is how TV is cheating, since screenplay shouldn't have V.O. monologues about thoughts but novels/visual novels can, and this is the cheating way that's doing it on TV, but doing it really well.

It gives me chills to see Morpheus standing on the hill outside Hell, looking down, wearing his helmet (that helmet still looks so freaky but cool at the same time, so very fitting to be a symbol for both dream and nightmare), and speaking his thoughts. Hearing him admit he's afraid, he's scared. The invincible Endless is afraid, knowing that he might not come back, he already arranged for his realm to survive without him, he already told Lucienne and everyone "Dream will survive even if he doesn't", he's prepared to not survive this, he's looking at his own perish in the eyes, and he realizes he's scared, just like how humans are scared of death. He's getting more human every day. He's so vulnerable, so easy to break, so broken as he is. It's probably exactly the brokenness that makes him so easy to love, since right from the beginning. Season 1 also started with his vulnerable moments, his possessions taken away, even his clothing taken away, captive inside the glass globe, can barely stand, without clothing, only his pale body against the dark background. Can't get away, can't do anything, so very very vulnerable, so helpless. But yet so otherworldly superior, still so proud, so remote, as if this vulnerable version of him has nothing to do with him, he's still so far away, like he's not the one being hurt by the world and other humans here, but meanwhile it's also completely him, he *is* here feeling every bit of harm. The contrast is just so fascinating and intoxicating, making his story, making *him*, so unbelievably attractive, addictive, easy to love.

It takes him a whole season 1 to be strong and powerful again, getting all his belongings back, handling the vortex, handling the escaping dream arcanas, creating new dreams and nightmares, and at the beginning of S02E01, rebuilding his castle. But right at that moment, he's made vulnerable again. Even with all his current power, when facing Hell, when facing his own potential possible death, he's still scared.

He is indeed, so much more human now.

### S02E02

Lucifer giving up Hell and give Morpheus the key: also something already know from the comic but watching the twist on TV, it still feels so sudden and counter-climax. The ending of Season 1 has Lucifer gathering the demons, announcing the revenge against Morpheus; S02E01 when Cain went to Hell to announce Morpheus's arrival, Lucifer and the demons also made quite a show about how they'd be a menace to Morpheus. Even Morpheus's own scare and preparation becomes subjective foreshadowing of how terrifying Hell will be and how much of a fight there will be. But when Morpheus actually arrives Hell, this "I quit" show is so far from expectation. Can't say it's not a disappointment to see this twist and turn going downwards, even though understanding (they want to make the most surprising twist, and downplay is one way).

But still, one minute all menacing, the next all tired and resigned and "I'm done with it" and go to retirement, close Hell and give away key without foreshadowing, too sudden, too out of no where, no foreshadowing, purely surprise, purely from writer's thought of "what's the least expected thing to come next" and give out the twist, twist only for the twist's sake, this is a failure in writing (as much as I enjoyed the feast that comes next).

Love the scene where Lucifer kisses Mazikeen on the cheek, asks her to give her blade to her, and refuses her to follow and let her go. So very gentle and caring, no doubt Mazikeen loves and adores Lucifer so much, always willing to give away her everything for her, and is the last demon to leave the realm. And this farewell scene here, I can almost be convinced Lucifer cares her back just as much. Wonder if there's any Lucifer/Mazikeen fic on AO3 (bet there is). Got familiar with Mazikeen from *Lucifer* show, a little surprised to see her (first time is in season 1), a nice familiar face, even the half human face half demon face/skull doesn't look terrifying at all; the good thing about seeing same character in multiple shows: already knew the setting, expect nothing less. Of course Mazikeen looks like that, and of course even look like that she's very lovable. Don't think we see Mazikeen ever again, wonder where she'd go, maybe she'd still try to follow Lucifer around? Just like the *Lucifer* version did, no doubt she'd try to do that here too.

Love the scene where Lucifer asks Morpheus to cut off her wings, just like in the *Lucifer* show. Love the cutting wound is all flesh and blood, love the sound when the knife cut through the flesh, love Lucifer's panting and shaking when her wings are gone. Love the design of her gown on the back, having the buttons and opening, so perfect to fit the wings, still elegant with the backshot after her wings are cut and she walks slowly away. It's the perfect gown design for winged creatures, I'm definitely thinking about using the same design for Cas with wing fics.

PS: no wonder I thought why Lucifer looks so familiar, or other way round, why Wednesday season 1 the headmistress look so familiar. Both are played by the same actress, who I should have remembered meeting the first time in Game of Throne.

<br>

The feast scene is very much exactly duplicate of the comic, trying to replicate but doesn't look as good as the comic look. Especially the Order and Chaos part, the comic did fabulously with the squares and colorful bubbles, and the TV version with the straight dull shirts all business-looking men and circus-looking girls, the impact just doesn't feel very impressive. A little curious why, both as visual media, intuitively might think TV with its moving frames should have offer more chances of making it more visual and impressive, but instead achieve less than comic book. One answer might be, TV is limited when it has to be all real human figure, and there's only so much one can do with a human figure, and comic uses the bubble shapes and fonts and colors to express the ideas, and *ideas* can be so much more abundant and unexpected than human performance. Even the story pace feels better in comic than TV.

The guests are mostly following the comic: Order, Chaos, Odin (with Thor and Loki), Sunano (Japanese God of Thunder), demon, angel, faeries. Once again show the fabulous ability of depicting each character with only a few lines, and making each one different and memorable, and has their own motivation and display.

Saw so many familiar names as in SPN, demon Azazel (though the name pronounces differently here), angel Remiel (though it's Ramiel in SPN, and it's a demon there), Duma angel of silence (also angel in SPN); and bonus, Lucifer's angel name Samiel (something popular in many literatures but not in SPN canon). Love the graphic design of Azazel, the whole universe of demons live inside him, and all teethy and smoky and shadowy, the CGI is quite impressive, hope SPN could have such impressive CGI (but then it wouldn't be SPN).

Meet Loki for the first time here. Nice hair style design. Very charming, and great that the character performance and design is not affected or constrained by MCU Loki, it's a very different character and performance here. But also convey the right amount of charm and trick, very likable, even if already know he would do no good (he's Loki, of course he's supposed to do no good). Has so much potential for good story around him.

### S02E03

Love Morpheus's performance whenever Nada is involved, even though the emotional connection between him and Nada is still a little out of place, can't really feel the love or chemistry between them, not as much as Morpheus and Calliope in Season 1's bonus episodes. Morpheus's displayed feelings and care for her feel like more for the plot than for the emotion. But every time Nada in involved, Morpheus performed so shaky and shocked and can barely hold himself together, so vulnerable and on edge again. Especially obvious when Azazel threatened Morpheus with Nada's life and soul, and Morpheus is literally shaking on his throne, but he's still not giving in to Azazel, that performance is just amazing to see. So shaky, so vulnerable, so broken, so perfect.

Same for every time he eagerly asked Lucienne if he can visit Nada (after Nada is saved from Azazel, and by the way the way Morpheus escape and combine Azazel, that scene of him being inside Azazel and the next shot is him so much bigger from the outside, is a Deja Vu from season 1 when he was fighting against John for his ruby, it's an amazing shot transition), he's so eager and worried and vulnerable, just like he's nothing more than a young boy worried for his lover, these moments can convince me he really cared for Nada. Or rather, at least he believe he loved her.

He said "I'm sorry" to Nada. Something the old Morpheus wouldn't have said. But after season 1, he's indeed changed.

<br>

The feast scene and plot officially wrapped up in this episode, with all the guests leaving. A really nice and enjoyable plot to see, even though I don't think this is the best writing to have such a plot for this story. So here's the conflict feeling about this season (which remains for many more episodes, and ultimately extend to the whole season): it doesn't feel like a very good show, not the best storytelling or the best writing, but I love it, and I enjoyed it a lot.

Love the part with Shakespeare's *Midsummer night's dream* show and the faeries and the first appearance of Puck (aka Robin Goodfellow), a very memorable character with very few shots and lines, a very successful depiction. And a very familiar face. Took me a while to remember where I've seen him before, turns out to be Joffrey from Game of Thrones. Look like Sandman love Game of Thrones actors.

But really can't appreciate Morpheus's long hair, it just looks so weird.

<br>

Love the fighting scene near the end between Morpheus and Nada, with Nada gives him a strike on the face:

> Morpheus: I love you.
>
> Nada: No, you do not. I know you *believe* you do. But I wonder if your kind is even capable of love.
>
> Morpheus: How can you say that?
>
> Nada: How could anyone who truly loved me do what you've done to me?
>
> Morpheus: I have changed.
>
> Nada: It is the same as the last time I refused you. I think perhaps you merely want what you cannot have. That is not love. That is desire.
>
> Morpheus: Desire?
>
> Nada: There is no shame in it.
>
> Morpheus: No. You cannot tell me what it is in my heart. I love you.
>
> Nada: But I do not love you.

"I wonder if your kind is even capable of love", this one strikes me hard, this is just another lonely unloved nonhuman character, so much older and bigger than human, can't understand love, accused by human of not even capable of loving, just like destiel (can't remember if it's canon or fic where Dean wonders aloud about angels: wondering if they're even capable of love, it's like every time they try to love, they make things even worse).

The unhuman aspect of the story, the older and more powerful character that is too far away from human life that can't understand human emotions, can't understand *love*, I feel so resonated with them. As if I'm one of them. Or just that I can't understand human just like they do, even if I'm one. It's difficult to understand and to love, sometimes I wonder the same, if I'm even capable of loving, or maybe I believe I do, but what I'm feeling is not really love.

"You merely want what you cannot have, that is not love, that is desire." That is such a nice quote to have, I'm gonna steal it and maybe use it somewhere in my story. The longing and wanting part may or may not be a part of love, or maybe that's just desire, but it's so difficult to tell the difference, and it hurts so much to hear from the person you love that they don't even think what you are feeling is love at all. And even more heartbroken, that they don't forgive you for the harm you did to them, and they declare they do not love you. I'm a whore for hurt/comfort and whump, and I get them all in Sandman.

The new quest begins, Delirium drags Morpheus to look for Destruction. The organization of this season is quite clear, with one main plot point following another (just like season 1: get out of cage, after that find the three items, after that find the three escaping dream arcanas, after that handle the vortex), and for this season, it's to free Nada (side quest: handle Hell's key), after that go find Destruction with Delirium (premise: find the three friends of Destruction's), after that handle the situation with Orpheus and the Kindly One. (Why it's always "three"? Three items, three arcanas, three friends.)

Meet Destruction for the first time. Is it just me or he really does look like a young Hemsworth brother; got so distracted so many times just thinking how much alike he is to one of them.

Love Destruction's talking dog. TV rule: get a lovable pet in the show, it's the easiest way for people to love the show.
