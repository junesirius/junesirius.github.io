It's painful, to go into a mourning scene. Everyone is mourning, missing Morpheus so much.

As do I.

The hurt in both their eyes. The New Dream facing everything that is too new and too sudden for him, he really is still an eight-month-old infant, just with all the Endless power now. And the one person he thought he can rely on, the one person he witnessed Morpheus trusted and asked for advice, he'd hoped Lucienne would be the same for him as she was for Morpheus, but she refused. It's too much pain to continue counseling the new Dream Lord when it's a different face and a different being. If it's not Morpheus, then there's no reason to stay. The connection and relationship Daniel wanted to build, and the pain and close-off of Lucienne, the desperation and pain from both sides are too much to bear or witness. The real pain only starts after Morpheus is really gone, the pain and missing is something that happened to others. Death is something that happened to everyone else.

I can feel the pain in Lucienne as she mourns Morpheus, I miss Morpheus just as much as she does (probably not as much, since she actually knows him and was close to him), but I can also feel the loneliness and isolation in Daniel. It's not his fault that Morpheus is gone, it's not his wish that he becomes the new Dream, and he's not even a human anymore (not like he ever really was, since not even his birth a normal one), but now he has to shoulder all those burden and responsibility, be the being Morpheus and the Dreaming needs him to be. It's not the life he'd choose for himself, and he doesn't have a choice at all, just like Morpheus doesn't have a choice either. No one is really free, and everyone just takes what's given. That's the miserable and tragedy of this story.

Daniel wearing all white, is also too much of a contrast to Morpheus's all black, one can't even pretend to imagine he's just an reincarnation of Morpheus. He's his own self.

"You loved him, didn't you?" and Corinthian didn't answer that. And we already know Morpheus loved him, because Lucienne has said that, how much he missed him and his heart broke when he had to uncreate him.

That's why there're so many Morpheus/Corinthian.

And for the snippet above, that's why there're so many Morpheus/Lucienne, or Morpheus/Matthew, or Morpheus/Hob, or Morpheus/Nuala. They are there, because there are feelings, emotions, bonds, relationships, importance between them. Because Morpheus is important to these people, changed their lives one way or another, "made an echo" in their lives, and they did the same to him too, changed him, made him something different, made him more human.

He has so many friends and family and important beings in his life, and he changed so many people. That's why so many has come to his funeral. So many would grieve and mourn the memory of him. Just wish he could see that, know his life is not for nothing, know he's loved and remembered by so many. Just wish he know. Hope he know.

It's so touching and philosophical to see Fiddler's Green refuses to be resurrected. It could have been a happy ending, with everyone else being brought back. The New Dream is trying so hard to remedy the losses, but Fiddler's Green *refused*.

He's born from an idea, grow as an idea in the Dreaming. He's not a "who", he's a "where". And this "where", chooses not to continue, in order to give his life and death meaning. The meaning of an idea comes from the birth as well as its death. And bringing him back, wiping away the death and sacrifices, ruins the meaning of this story, takes away the meaning and importance of the life.

It's so beautiful, and for sure difficult to understand. I'm pretty sure many people just can't not understand why he wouldn't want to be alive again, but those who don't afraid to die, the suicidal ones, would understand. There's the beauty in the death, just as much as the beauty in the life. He gets to let the story of sacrifice be the final memory of him.

But that can only apply to Fiddler's Green, as an idea, but not as a person, isn't it? Did Daniel ever try to tell him how much his friend would miss him and pain to his loss. Lucienne and Matthew and Merv, all of them, they have enough pain mourning the loss of Morpheus, does he have to make them mourn the loss of him too? It feels like a cruel thing to do.

I understand his choice, and I may approve his choice if I were him, but I also would hesitate, if I have to think about his friends, think about everyone important in his life.

Maybe, he's just trying to be with Morpheus, wherever he may be, in the underworld or with Death, wherever Endlesses go after they're gone. If Morpheus can't be brought back, then he shouldn't be, either. That, can finally be a strong enough argument to persuade me.

<br>

It's such a complete structure now, to see this season begin with all these guests coming and we know them for the first time (God of Order, Chaos, Odin, Japanese God of Thunder, Angels, Demons, Faries, humans, everyone) for the party and drinking and fun; and by the end of the season, we see them all gather together again for the same man, for his funeral.

The foreshadowing and introduction of all those characters, and the exiting for all of them, together with the farewell of Morpheus. It's the completion of the story, a full circle, no wonder it's okay to kill the series now. It's a pity to not see more stories for Daniel, the new Dream King, but it's okay. We come to see Lord Morpheus, and we stop when he's no longer here. The show lives and dies with him. Even metauniversely it feels like fate: The Sandman can only be him, without Morpheus, there's just no more story to see.

I'm surprised at the almost hopeful ending for Lyta, for all that she's done, that it's her that pushed Morpheus to death, but in the end, she's not punished, because she's also the victim. It looks like, when all of them are helpless, the only thing for sure is the fate and destiny.

It's a surprise that it takes only one episode for Lucienne to accept Daniel and willing to stay for him. But granted, Daniel did a lot, the first and second and third thing he did, he saved Abel and Merv and Fiddler's Green. He's nervous and vulnerable about meeting the rest of the Endless, he wants advice from Lucienne and Destruction, he forgives Lyta and offers her the best comfort he can offer. He's good, and he's human. For that, Lucienne is willing to take a chance, to guide him, to support him, to see what big great thing he can achieve as the first human that's also an Endless. After all, Dream is hope. And she must have seen so much hope in the new Dream King.

And it's still so very confusing what exactly is the new Dream King. He's not Daniel but he sorta also is; he's not Morpheus, but he sorta also has his memory and power, and he recognizes his friends. He does things differently, but also not so different. He's himself, but how much of Morpheus is still in him? Corinthian said maybe he's like him, just like he still has the memory of the first Corinthian, but he's not the same (which we also don't know for sure how much of him is still like the first Corinthian). It's all so very confusing and not reassuring, still so much to figure out.

So, this is the ending of Lord Morpheus. Another lonely unloved celestial being that "can't understand love, not capable of loving" but died because of love kind of story, just like Cas and the whole destiel story. End up dying to save the world and everyone he loved, trying so hard to make everything right, to save everyone, to correct all the wrongs, but at the end, he still can't outrun fate, the only one he can't save is himself, died not knowing how much he's loved and missed by all his family and friends. Almost practically just exactly another version of Cas for me, it's killing me. But comparatively slightly more hopeful. The hopeful ending makes the pain and mourning from the funeral scene a little better.

The new King of Dream is great, but I still miss Morpheus so much. Still love him so much. Still love dreaming so much. I'm pretty sure I'd still dream of Morpheus sometimes, and so I can pretend I visited his palace and actually met him in his realm. As long as I still remember him and still see him in my dream, that has to mean he's still alive and there, right?
