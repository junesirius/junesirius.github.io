As much as I love the story, as much as I know how Gaiman's brain works, so fast and brilliant that there are too many subplot lines and irrelevant characters that he'd like to make vivid, this is not the best thing to be adapted to movie or show.

Ever since S02E06, the second half has turning messy. It could (probably) have found an easier way to make Dream's ominous coming death more pressing and deadly, instead of being so forced. The in-world rule of triggering the Kindly Ones law: you have to have someone starts a vengeance on you, is just becoming inconvenient.

If it's not because of trying to self-safe, Morpheus wouldn't have initiated the Loki storyline, then Loki wouldn't cause all the trouble with Lyta and Daniel, then Lyta wouldn't go to the Kindly Ones to revenge on Morpheus, then nothing would really happen here. I can see the reason why they made the design, it's the "Death in Samarra" story all over again, but it doesn't feel very powerful with so many extra characters taking away the attention. It might be easier to simply set Loki genuinely (without the push) to start revenge on Morpheus, or simply Lyta starts doing it by herself. Otherwise, it's almost like every single one of them is just manipulated by someone else and no one is really making their own choice. (Not even the Fates sisters, they're following the old rules and can't do anything about it, one of the three sisters actually initially had the line about wanting to spare Morpheus, but that's beyond her power too.)

I like it but I also don't quite like it, or rather, I can see why people wouldn't like it. It's not really a good enough show or writing or adaptation, but it's just enjoyable to see and pick Gaiman's brain. (And trying very very hard to forget about the taint to not ruin what I would have liked.)

Love the very philosophical dialogue between Morpheus and Delirium. Always love how delirious Delirium's words are. It feels easy (and most difficult) to write chaotic yet deep words like those:

> Morpheus: I cannot leaving Dreaming at this time. I have responsibilities.
>
> Delirium: You use that word too much. Do you think about what it means in your head?
>
> Morpheus: I use it to refer to that area of existence over which I have a modicum of influence and control. Namely, the realm and action of dreaming.
>
> Delirium: It's much more than that. The things we do make echoes. Suppose, for instance, you and I stopped on a street corner to admire a bolt of lightning in the sky. Well, for ages after that, people and things would stop on the very same corner and look up. They wouldn't even know what they were looking for. Some people might see a ghost of lightning. Love how Delirium knows so many things and always have to use analogy that grownups can't understand. She's the kind of genius kid that no grownup takes seriously and no grownup really try to understand. But at least after the traveling, Morpheus is *trying* to see it now.

Love the way Delirium describes their responsibility. "Their existence creates echoes", they affect the universe, "deform" it (even if it's not a word Morpheus would use).

She really does know a lot about real serious things, it's just her mind not necessarily always on these things. Important things, small things, they are all the same priority to her. She's probably the textbook example of an ADHD.

And right about time we saw Destiny splits into so many different versions of himself, that's why I'm thinking of Destiny as multi-personality disorder.

The very sudden twist and capture of Loki also feels... too sudden. He's doing all the tricky works to trigger Lyta's vengeance journey, and now when it's almost time, he's not there to see it. And all it takes is just for Constantine to summon Odin to take Loki away. And there's no grudge between Odin and Morpheus, just because of Odin being nice.

Now I realize why the second half of the story feels so weak: There's no strong villain to hold the story together. Loki isn't a strong villain and he's taken offline now; Lyta isn't a strong villain because she's simply misguided and the misinformation is just so easy to fix; The Kindly Ones are probably the closest to being the villains, but they're also simply the helper hands, and not being in the direct conflict hurts the tension. The story is just too loose to be tied together.

Come to think about that, that's exactly how *Stardust* is like too, too many storylines, but then in the end, they're tied together. But that "tie", how good the tie is, how tight it is, it all affects the quality of the story and the show (or movie). *Stardust* just happened to make it not bad (by "not bad" I mean really good), and here the second half is just not doing good enough. There's always risk to have too many subplots and make the motivations too complicated (or not strong enough).

But, on the bright side, thinking back on that Puck/Loki/Corinthian storyline, right after Puck breaks up with Loki and plans to go back to Faeries, Loki gets captured (after fighting and losing to Corinthian), and Puck doesn't know any of that. I wonder what Puck would think about if he's just trying to be pissed for a bit but come back only to find Loki is gone and forever imprisoned and they'd never get any chance to fix it again? Sweet sweet hurt/comfort whump angst and very likely not happy ending for them, my favs.
