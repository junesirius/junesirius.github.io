My uttermost favorite episode of the season, "The Song of Orpheus". There is no adaptation of Orpheus that can ever really go wrong. The story is too beautiful to be done bad.

The wedding scene is so beautiful. All the Endless come to the wedding. It feels so otherworldly to see Orpheus calling Death or Destiny or any of the other Endless siblings uncles and aunts. And even more so, it's the first time ever that we realize they too also have a name, just like Dream has his name Morpheus, Destiny is Potmos, Death is Teleute, Destruction is Olethros, Desire is Epithumia, Despair is Aponoia, and Delirium is Mania. Though, to be fair, most of the names are just a lazy design, a Greek translation, but those are already so much better than just calling them "Death" or "Destiny", this is the first time we see them really as a human-adjacent character, not just a simple personification of a concept, when they're someone's uncles and aunts. They too have feelings and attachments. It is by having a child that Morpheus makes his whole family of Endless member humans. It is Orpheus that makes them the most human, and makes his father utterly vulnerable.

Being son of Endless and Calliope, no wonder he's a oracle and the best singer in the whole world that even Hades is touched. The song in the show is no disappointment. I don't need to know Greek to feel the feelings in the song, so beautiful, so light, yet so deep, the voice is so clear, yet so painful, so much longing, so much love, it's flying yet it's drowning, it's pleading yet it has pride. It's love, it's life, it's Orpheus sending the power of his soul in the song, and the song conveys everything. No wonder Hades gets touched, no wonder I gets goosebumps. I cry in the song without knowing why, tears falling down on their own as I dive into the music. It's the song of Orpheus, that's the only explanation why it can be so beautiful.

<br>

Love the underworld scene, though a little too dark and actually not too much design, but feels fitting, and best of all, not taking away the attention on Orpheus by being too grand. The scale of Orpheus versus Hades and Persephone, it looks so very intimidating, so perfect to show the smallness one would feel in the underworld.

The leaving scene, the turning back, the regret and pain, the love that wouldn't possibly be able to resist turning back, it's all done so beautifully by the music and sound effect. The absolute quietness with only his footsteps alone leaving the underworld, the color scheme around him, all dark, with the only light coming from front, no shadow to show she's behind him. Even the camera is helping the trick, filming from behind him, looking in the same way as Orpheus looks, so not even audience knows if Eurydice is behind him. Anyone who doesn't know the original story must have the same doubt as Orpheus, "Is Hades trustworthy?" "Is Eurydice really behind him?" "Could this possibly just be a trick to lure him out of the underworld with empty hands?" He just needs one look to be sure, he just has to look back. So he does. The love that almost saves his wife is the same as the love that pushes her back into the underworld. The last whisper of "Orpheus" before she falls back, it's performed so extraordinarily. The trembling lips, the teary eyes, the hands that are so close but so far away that he can't hold on to. The pain and agony. It's the most beautiful tragedy in all the Greek lore that I've ever known about. Love it so much.

<br>

Very surprised to see Orpheus even declares he doesn't want anything to do with his mother, just like he disowns his father. "If you don't help me, then I'm no longer your son (or, you're no longer my father/mother)." It pains Morpheus so much. This is probably the only part that I can't quite agree with Orpheus. Morpheus refuses to help him to go to Hades because he doesn't want to see him harm; very similarly, Calliope also refuses to help him die because she doesn't want to see him harm. Both of them love him very much (in their own way), but both rejections actually cause him more harm and pain, and not by his choice. Love could create so much pain. It almost feels like karma.

But still, breaking his relationship with Morpheus and Calliope, seeking his own death, trying to die but can't, trying to save his love but can't, oh Orpheus, how can your story possibly be more tragic or more beautiful?

In those long years after you break with Morpheus, would you regret doing so, breaking his heart, as well as breaking yours? Do you wish to do something different, or still resent him for not helping you save the love of your life? Who comes more important for you, the love of your life, or your family? Though, admittedly, Eurydice is your family too, it's an impossible choice to make. There is no doubt you still love your father and mother so very much, asking of them when you're drifting in the world miserably. When Constantine saves you, you know immediately it must be your father who comes to help you, to save you, but he can't come himself, and you know that too. So clever as you are, you figured that out, you miss your father so much, yet all you can do is to ask Constantine if he is alright, if he looks okay. But Constantine is too clever to give anything away. The family feelings and emotions and bond, it's so tied and thick no matter what, whatever you say or do, whether you are human or god or Endless, it's always so complicated, always so impossible to break, so everywhere to have nowhere to run, even if you do, you're still tied in the heart because deep down you know it's still there. Am I projecting again, or am I feeling for Orpheus too much? I'm going to cry again just by thinking back those beautiful shots and feelings and dialogues and tensions between them. It's written and shot too beautifully, too emotionally powerfully.

<br>

Almost forget about the beginning part of the plot around Wanda. It's so sad, but so real too. Funeral is not about her, it's about the rest of the family, about those other people, about the living. They see her as who she is not, because they need to. They're still mourning, they're still feeling for her, but their feelings are for themselves, which have nothing to do with her.

It's so real for those who can't get accepted when they are alive, who still can't after they are gone. The trans people, the minority groups, those who are invisible in the world, this is happening everyday. And for once, there is a show revealing their reality, their pain and the pain even after their death, the dilemma, the conservative parents who can't understand; for once, it's some media that tries hard enough for people to see, but it's just so happened to become a tainted media that people don't want to approach because of Neil Gaiman. I can't imagine how powerful and influential this sequence might get in the Internet if it's any other modern popular shows, I can't say enough that it's those times that I can't forgive Neil Gaiman for ruining this chance for so many others. Even if I can forgive him for what he did, I can't forgive him for what he's done to others.

<br>

Love Death's home and her music taste and her interior design. So very casual when she's not working, she looks like the most easygoing Endless of all the Endless (besides Morpheus, especially in the later time). She's just a cool, gothic girl having a rock taste and keeping a goldfish in her apartment, very comfortable, very casual, she even commented herself: she might tidy it up a little if she'd know she'd have guests, just like every single messy easygoing comfortable girl hanging around her own room. She's so cool.

Yes she can get so serious too. Of course she can, she's still Death, the second oldest sibling only younger than Destiny. When she asks Orpheus if this is really what he wants, being immortal, going to underworld to save his wife, the pain in her eyes (and the pain and tear and determination in his!), she knows so much about practically everything in the whole world, and she knows what a curse that would be, but unlike Morpheus, she gives Orpheus free will, it's Orpheus's decision and his alone. If it's something in her power to give, and if she can be sure this really is what he wants, then unlike Morpheus, she'd give it to him. She disapproves, but she lets him make the decision. It's exactly what a cool aunt is expected to be. But also, just like she expected, this immortality is the exact source of pain for Orpheus (and Morpheus) for so many centuries to come, and ultimately, leading to Morpheus's own death. If Death has known that, if Destiny has known that, would they still make the choices they make back then? Would Death think it's her fault that leads to Morpheus's ending, when she's in his funeral? Would Desire? Would Destiny? I wonder what they're thinking about, in the funeral. I'll have to watch the sequence very closely next.

<br>

Love Delirium's realm. I wondered how one could show a realm of madness, and they did it beautifully. The train sequence, transitioning between quiet and noise, dark and light, empty and crowd, so very fitting for Delirium. There's a circus shot too, I think, and also a beautiful garden, a deserted building, a broken sundial. So many colors, so many different changing scenes, I'd guess anyone would have get lost if it's not Morpheus stepping into the realm. Actually, is this the second realm we've ever scene? Or third, if counting Destiny's? For some reason, I haven't thought about that "Destiny's Garden" to be actually Destiny's realm, more like Death's home, it's some place Destiny hangs around and holds family meeting, but not really his realm where he works and lives day to day (or maybe he is, maybe he can spend eternity in his garden).

Surprised to hear Delirium used to be Delight and then she changes, and not even Destiny knows why. She suddenly looks so grown up for a second there, so serious, for Morpheus's sake, trying to help him out by confronting Destiny, their oldest brother. She doesn't like seeing Morpheus distressed, just as Morpheus doesn't want to see her hurt.

Love the scene when Delirium suddenly rises her head and asks if Morpheus likes her, and that small smile on Morpheus's face. Morpheus doesn't smile much, but every time he does, when he smiles at Hob Gadling, when he smiles at Delirium, and at Lucienne too at one point I think, it's always so heartwarming and beautiful to see. Smile looks so good on him. And even better to hear he apologized to Delirium, and even admits he must like her. And Delirium likes him too.

Love how Morpheus gets along so well with Delirium and Death, probably his two favorite siblings. He needs people, needs friends, needs family. And he has them.

Love the part when Delirium tries to stay sane for Morpheus, she makes her two eyes the same color, and that underlying disharmonious high-pitched hidden in the background sound effect, unnoticeable at first but already there, then as she says "She can hold herself together, but it hurts", the sounds get louder and louder, more unbearable. Only after Morpheus tells her she doesn't have to, she changes back to heterochromia and a little delirious, but no longer painful to bear. It feels so so real that it touches me. I've long suspected they have the metaphor of some mental illness or minority group for every one of the Endless, maybe it's not the most politically correct statement to say, but I do see them, and so many things can get better explained that way, e.g.:

- Destiny: control freak, multi-personality
- Destruction: anger control issue
- Death: ADHD, gothic (doesn't count) (probably most socially acceptably normal and healthy among all)
- Despair: depression
- Desire: sex addict, non-bi, trans
- Dream: autistic
- Delirium: ADHD, bipolar

Basically they're writing all of them as some outcast freaks (not negatively, as many other shows would comment, like *Wednesday*, *Wicked*, *Stranger Things*, etc. I've seen so many outcast shows lately (or not so lately) and love them), and giving them powers. But those "problems" are still there, maybe transforming into "qualities" or more neutral "personalities" or "attributes", but deep down, those are where these come from.

**PS:** Though, probably the only noticeably bad thing in the visual of this show: They are really bad at making special effect for fire. The fire that burns the dancing woman's place that killed Wanda, the fire and explosion that Destruction created for Orpheus to go to Death's home, all those fire looks so fake and very much digital effect, and not the good kind. One shot it's the beautiful realistic scene with Destruction and Orpheus standing by the sea, the next is the very fake and digital effect creating an explosion in the middle of some random mountain and cliff and tries to impress the audience that the door to Death's home is in the middle of those funny-looking flames and smokes, that's probably a budget problem right there, they might want to avoid using CGI fire or might want to bump the budget to get a real better-looking version of that.

But also to the credit of CGI, last episode, in the same sequence right around the fire scene, the dancing woman scene is so so beautiful. The dreamy white dress, the overlapping films, all the audience in the story get dizzy and just stare at the stage, probably most of the audience outside the screen is doing just the same. It's like the dance of Death in *Mozart L'Opera Rock*, in Mozart's dying song, like the Death's in *Romeo et Juliette*. It's always Death or woman in white dress that dancing the most dreamy and dizzy dance, taking away people's breath, so beautiful that human stop thinking, stop existing, just watching, forgetting, even not minding death itself. Maybe the real Death should try that too, luring people away with that frantic dance, it's going to be worth it to die for.
