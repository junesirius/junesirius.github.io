Holy moly, the first time we see Endless's parent, Time and Night, and we see Time is just a dick. If that's the upbringing they got, no wonder all of them are broken.

"Children are an inconvenient consequence of love", damn, how can you even say that? How can you say that while not expecting to be hated as a parent? How can you say that without caring about any consequence and hurt? Oh right, he has no compassion, he called Dream weak and sentimental for can't bear letting his son suffer more.

Time just goes straight with "I do not love you, Dream", *time* doesn't love anyone, time is unbiased. It actually makes sense. How would *Time*, as eternal and unchanging as it is, know what love is? No wonder it has also become such a struggle for Morpheus to understand love for the thousands of years of his life.

And also it looks like, for the second half of this season, the keyword in script shifted from "change" to "love". The advice Destruction gave Morpheus is to trust love, he gave up his role and realm out of love, he's asking Morpheus not to forget love and to make choices out of love. And now here when Morpheus visits his father for help, Time knows he's trying to ask him to help out of love, and the rejection is just as simple and cruel as the love is difficult: *Time* doesn't love, Time can't love. He just doesn't have the capability to, just like Nada once accused Morpheus of.

(But also inevitably, by doing so, emphasizing the lines with so many repetition of "love", the script looks enforced and unnatural. It's not a very good writing, especially after the half-point, after the blood is spilled. (Or maybe even before that, but before, it feels easier to forgive, when "change" has been repeated too many times too.))

<br>

Very interesting to see Puck and Loki are really seriously officially together together now, with kisses and all that. Two tricksters, should work out but they do. But also inevitably they'd fight and trick and fight and hurt, just wait for it, that's the best part.

<br>

It's announced here already that Daniel Hall will be the new Dream of the Endless, but he wouldn't be the new Morpheus. Probably it's because of the spoiler (in the sense that it's a rewatch), now that we already know how Daniel look like when he's twenty, suddenly in those shots when he's still a baby of eight months old, he does look very much like his grown self. The cheek, the forehead, the nose, the eyes, the skin color, everything in the grown man can be found in the baby, it might as well really be the animated CGI version of him (Jacob Anderson) from his childhood baby photos. It's just either a really coincident or a really good baby casting that shouldn't have been possible.

<br>

When Morpheus goes to Destiny for help, he said: "I deserve to die. But I have my duty. And I have no wish to die." It's very interesting to see the conflict here, "deserve" and "wish" having conflict. I don't see Morpheus as a self-preserving character. Having known too many suicidal characters, feeling themselves deserve to die and having no wish to live are always the same thing. But apparently, either to make the plot interesting or to make his character even more complicated, he has both sides: He knows he deserves to die based on the oldest rule and law, for killing his son; he knows his death would endanger his realm and everyone this realm serve, so his duty requires him to make better arrangement; and he knows his own survival instinct, which is as rare and surprising as his sentiment and love and feelings and changes.

I'd imagine he wouldn't have that survival instinct, just see how he just curl up and not say anything and get captured for eighty years! And that's the very first time we got introduced to him! But now he's scared, he's scared he'd lose his son, he's scared he'd die, and he doesn't want to die. He changed so much that I can barely recognize him.

<br>

> Time: But I do not love you, Dream.
>
> Morpheus: No. I know that.
>
> Time: Do you? Tell me something, Dream. Do you love me?
>
> Morpheus: A child cannot help but love his father. No matter who he is or what he does... or doesn't do. I learned that from my own son, and I abused him. And yet he loved me.
>
> Time: Did I abuse you? And yet you love me. I think that makes you rather foolish, don't you?
>
> Morpheus: I think I knew you wouldn't help me. I think the reason I came here was to thank you. For everything you have given me, for my... existence. Every moment of it. Even this one. And I think I came here to say goodbye. I don't believe we will see each other again.

"A child cannot help but love their parent", being treated unwell will not make them stop loving their parents, it will only make them stop loving themselves.

It's not just Morpheus's looking for help, going to his Father and Mother, this is his journey of farewell. So it looks like, maybe he's really almost giving up, knowing too well there's no way to change it or escape it, knowing too well his death and destruction is inevitable. He's not fully made peace with it yet, but he's not too far from it either.

> Time: If I were inclined to help you, Dream, how would you propose I do that?
>
> Shall I send you back in time? So you can go to Hades on your son's behalf?
>
> I could do that for you, if you'd like. But Hades will refuse your request. And your son will find his way to the Underworld, no matter what you do.
>
> That is the only help I can give you. The peace that comes from knowing it could not be other than it was. There is nothing you could have done then to save your son. Just as you cannot save yourself now.

But that knowledge and peace, that even if he could use his father's help, even if he went back in time to change what has troubled him and became his regret for thousands of years, there will be no change. What happened would always happen, and there's nothing he can do. The peace of that, does feel slightly comforting.

BTW, the transition scene from Dream's realm to Time's, then to Night's, are both so cool.

<br>

Probably unsurprisingly, the conversation between Morpheus and his mother Night, the dialogue also has to mention "love".

"What does she (Delirium) want with me?" "Your attention, your love." It's so clear that even Morpheus knows better about Delirium than the mother. Even as the mother *acts* to be loving and friendly and compassionate, warmer than the father, she's no better in feelings and compassion. Actually even worse.

> Night: I'll tell you what. Why not let Mother make it all better?
>
> Morpheus: How?
>
> Night: What if I were to make you a perfect little dream world... here. With a partner, a relationship, you always wanted one of those. And you'd be safe here and close to me. You could see me whenever you like, when I'm not working. There. That's my offer, darling. It's not like you've left yourself a way out.
>
> Morpheus: I must, with respect, decline.
>
> Night: You would rather be hounded to death by the Kindly Ones than live here with me?
>
> Morpheus: No. But I have realm and my responsibilities.
>
> Night: You've always been selfish, even when you were a child. Everything had to be your way or no way at all. The rest of us didn't exist to you, except as a means to an end. Well, now you have your end, and I leave you to it.
>
> Morpheus: Forgive me. I meant no disrespect. I was merely trying to be the person I thought you wanted me to be.
>
> Night: Of course. It's always the parents' fault. Well, if it gives you comfort to blame me for the way you are, or the way you turned out, you are welcome to it, my love. It will be my farewell gift to you.
>
> Morpheus: I don't blame you, Mother.
>
> Night: Oh, I'm glad, darling. It's much nicer that we end as friends.

It hurts to hear Night as a mother to say words like "If it gives you comfort to blame me for the way you are, you are welcome to it". It might mean well (I doubt it, for Night's sake), but it hurts so much to hear. It almost feels like she doesn't care at all (which she probably doesn't). The pretense of warmth just makes everything so much worse to bear.

<br>

And in the counselling between Morpheus and Lucienne, when Morpheus humbles himself and finally reaches the stage that he's seeing Lucienne as a friend, not just a subordinate, this is what he confesses about his takeaways from seeing his parents for the last time:

> Lucienne: Nuala said you'd gone to see your parents. Were they at all... helpful?
>
> Morpheus: No.
>
> Lucienne: I'm so sorry.
>
> Morpheus: Between them, they have everything. All of time and space. And yet, they want only what they cannot have. Which is to be left alone. So I have given them what they want.
>
> Lucienne: And did they give you anything?
>
> Morpheus: They gave me life. It is a child's vanity to believe his parents will give him anything more.
>
> Lucienne: There are parents who... actually love their children, you know? Who take pleasure in their company and... would do anything for them.
>
> Morpheus: Not mine.
>
> Lucienne: No. But you did... for your son.
>
> Morpheus: As you have done... [whisper] for me.

That whispered "for me", the pain and appreciation in his eyes when he said those two last words, the glaring teary look, it breaks me. The way he knows how much he owes his friends. I think in a way he does know he's loved, like Lucienne already told him, there are parents who don't love their child, but there are those who do, and Morpheus is one of them. And immediately, Morpheus realizes, Lucienne (and others) love him and would have done so much for him, would do anything for him, just like how he'd do anything for Orpheus. He must know that's love and he's loved, he must.
