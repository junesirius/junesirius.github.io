O. M. G.

It's been a long while since last time I saw such a good movie on the big screen. Too long for Hollywood to cook up something this good. It's probably the least Hollywood story in a long while too, and it should probably all thanks to the original story. Hollywood wouldn't be able to come up with such a good ending if it's not for the novel it adapted from.

Love it so much. Want to rewatch it so many times. (And just did it again.)

<br>

### Oscar prediction

So, here's the summary of my thoughts for this movie. And my prediction for *Project Hail Mary*'s awards on Oscar 2027:

- Best movie: nominee, the winner is some diversity-focused movie
- Best director: win (I know it doesn't make sense to predict winning a best director but not best movie since those two only make sense when they're awarded to the same movie (quote from Rich Speight Jr.), but weirdly I think taking into account how political Oscar is, this would be how it turns out.)
- Best visual effect: at least nominee, maybe win, but small chance. It's a brutal fight against Dune 3, Avengers 5, new Spider-man movie, Odyssey, and probably even new Hunger Game and new Jumanji movie (why are there always so many sequels and so few originals)
- Best main actor: at least nominee. A high chance, but the winner is probably some diversity actor. (That would be his fourth nomination, he'd be so close to getting one, just give a few more years, but maybe if there's no other diversity movie out there, then he'd have to win it now.)
- Best sound effect: win (There should be no doubt. It's just too good.)
- Best original score: nominee. (Really want to say it has high chance to win since it's so so beautiful, but fighting with Dune 3, it's gonna be a cruel fight.)
- Best adapted script: win (No way there's a second movie as good as this coming from adapted script this year, just no way.)
- Best cinematography: at least nominee, maybe win (brutal fight with all the other big budget movies from big studios, but it's still good enough to put up a fierce fight, and maybe even win.)
- Best production design: nominee (more like honorary mention for this one, it's a good production design for Rocky's part, and the costume and set designs are good, but probably not good enough to win it. But it might still make its way to a nomination.)

### Music

The music is so phenomenal, exactly why I predicted it would get some awards (winning or nomination) from Oscar. The sound effect is impressive, the scoring is absolutely infectious. It's so powerful in hinting the emotion and plot tensions:

- The very deliberately misleading chasing music when Rocky's ship is following Grace's. Without the background context information (aka spoiler) from the novel, I got no idea if Rocky is going to be friend or foe, I was nervous and worried like Grace, scared of the unknown. The music is so deliberate in causing suspension and tension, leading to the same tension when Grace (and the audience) wonders if the first contact is message or bomb. Holding breath and scared of an explosion just like Grace did, bracing himself (myself), expecting the worst. (only to turn into a comedic moment: "Why is the second one so much slower? Oh they think I'm dumb." lol)
- The beautiful beautiful tango music when the two ships are spinning together in space, as if they are dancing (or dating, in a sense). It's so beautiful. And mirrors the previous lonely scene when Grace danced with a gown-doll to pretend he's not alone. He's so so alone, and lonely. And then not anymore, there's Rocky for him.
- Also almost like joyful dancing music when Grace remembers to use centrifuge to create gravity on ship. I'm surprised how easy it is to convey joy (and thrill, more like) through music. It's a powerful tool to manipulate (in a good way) human's emotion.
- Not just music and the sound, even the lack of it is also really really well-done. The really good use of silence in the most intense moments, suddenly cut off the sound when Grace's ship gets out of control and he hit his head and went into coma, and Rocky breaks the protection to save him. The sudden silence made me completely not dare to breath, the tension is exploding. Grace is dying, Rocky is dying too, willing to sacrifice himself for Grace. No sound would be good enough to convey the feelings, but the lack of it does the work. Also like when the camera shot zooms out to show a lonely little ship floating in the big vast dark empty space, the broad shot just to show how small Grace's spaceship is against the starry space, and how quiet and empty the space really is. (And really good visual too for that shot) It's extremely powerful and emotional. It's as well-done as the script and story-telling.
- And also shouldn't miss the unbelievably beautiful music when Grace is bathed in the infrared view of the Petrova Line of Planet Adrian, the moment of wonder and truth, the solution to save all the population on Earth and Erid. And he just closes his eyes, soaked in the moment, being in this moment. The astonishing view, the epic music, I could cry. (I probably did.) (And even more astonishingly, as I learned later, this is all practical shot, the production team actually created this whole real view, and it took the whole crew's breath away even while they were shooting, but the only one who couldn't see it is Gosling who's right in the middle of it, but the performance is incredibly convincing and powerful.)

### Plot

I only got very little complaint about the plot, but it doesn't mean I have none. There were confusion in the first time watching: when it's the first time audience got introduced to Grace, I didn't realize he doesn't remember anything. There were dropped lines here and there, and the white board full of questions like "Who am I" also indicated a little, and lines like "I must have know you, Commander Yao, but I don't remember." The amnesia is so useful in revealing the past and playing in parallel with the present, but it can get a bit confusing too. Only after the first watch did I understand the fully, and the second watch becomes a lot more enjoyable. So the confusion here didn't really trigger curiosity, just plain confusion, and that's not my favorite part when watching. (On top of all those other scientific/technical terms and setups the story needs to introduce and audience needs to remember, it's slightly demanding in the intelligence to keep up with everything. Must admit, I didn't do the full score in that. Might be better after reading the original book.)

<br>

Love the mutual learning the language part of the plot, still feel it's maybe a little rushed in the film. Bet there are more details in the books. How come they get to figure out so many words so quickly? How come it looks almost as if Rocky figured out human language faster than Grace and teaching the words one-sidedly? I need to read the book.

The learning language part also reminded me so much of *Arrival* (still love that movie so much too). And it's so obvious that music is the universal language.

<br>

I can never say enough how much I love the ending. Almost all the space movie ends with the protagonists going back home, going back Earth, saving the world. But not this one. This time, we don't go back to the place that disappointed us and failed us, that is willing to sacrifice you to save everyone else as if you don't belong to everyone else that deserves to be saved. The Earth (and people there) has to make the difficult choice, sure, and an understandable one, but it doesn't mean you have to forgive them, or give them hugs, or go back to them. You get to choose your own home, and this new home is a place where people genuinely appreciate you and love you, what's better than that?

About the several plot twists in the film, that when Rocky is dying or Grace is dying, or ship is exploding, troubles here and there, somehow I'm totally not worried for their lives. I was holding my breath while watching, sure, but I'm not worried. As soon as I learned it's a *good* movie that's not the cliche Hollywood industry flavored shit, I know there's no way this story will end in a bad ending, no way Grace or Rocky will die, because if they did, then there's the cliche Hollywood story once again. So they have to be okay, and unsurprisingly and satisfyingly, they are. And even better than I could ever expect them to be. Which genius comes up with the idea of that Earth environment firmament? That's really the genius of a genius (It's Andy, apparently.) It's indeed proved again that the characters can't be smarter than the writer, and this time, the writer is indeed very very smart.

### Interaction with other characters

No doubt all the focus lands on the friendship between Grace and Rocky, but there's some other minor beautiful interactions too. And all those other characters, however small the roles are, however short their screen time is, the depiction is concise and beautiful and very easily emotional too. (And tragic, for the most part.)

Like the beautiful little depiction of friendship between Grace and Carl, and when Grace asks for some funding that he'd need for experiments, Carl answered "We don't. But I do." And the playful scene that's no more than two minutes when they were buying the things from the shop, even including the "totally not needed but emotionally they do" sunglasses. And the payment is "From government. Which government? All of them." Which, all things considered, surprisingly isn't a lie. (Which makes the "betrayal" later even more tragic and tough, when Grace begged for Carl's help when they were forcing him to the spaceship, but Carl can't do anything but stand there, watching him, until the view gets tilted, and eventually darkened.)

The singing scene with everyone in the room, almost like a celebration, but everyone knows it can be the "last fun time before the end" kind of situation, but no one is sad. Strat even sings. It's almost as if Strat loves him too, the way she looks at Grace through the crowd, her eyes land on him, and he's surprised just by how beautiful her voice is, learning that she can be gentle and soft too when she chooses to, but she just can't afford so. A very very good touch to show she's also human and have so much emotions. We can't ever villainize her, or even blame her for making the difficult choice. It is indeed an impossible and difficult choice, someone has to do the wrong thing for the greater good, and she knew it, and she's brave enough to be the one to make the bad call, to be hated, if it means to save more people, to save the human species. It's the Trolly Problem, and she has the gut to pull the trigger, whatever it takes.

### Grace

I cried when Grace took his helmet off for the first time. IT's a huge leap of faith. He barely know Rocky at that time. He can choose to trust Rocky, or he might be wrong; and if he's wrong, he's dead. So this is the very first moment of trust, he's trusting from basically just guessing, assuming the kindness of a stranger (an alien), that neither of them have any ill intent, and based on how Rocky designed the tunnel to be the right pressure and temperature for him, he's trusting Rocky to do this atmosphere part right too. And the leap is a right one. From this point on, the rest goes easier.

Love how the writing depicts Grace to be a normal easy-to-get scared human, like just any everyday people, get scared when things just suddenly move, very jumpy, scared with height, scared when Rocky first time made a yell. Very easy to relate and love. He's not any high-above Hollywood hero, he's among us, he's just like one of us.

Love how quickly Grace figures out Rocky doesn't use vision, but see by echolocation. He's so quick and smart and observant, noticed Rocky doesn't need light to see, and it's dark on his ship, so he must not use visual, so he needs surface to see, and therefore can't see printed things or screen. That's the intelligence of a scientist's brain.

Love the visual affect showing from Rocky's POV too. The majority of the story is told from Grace's POV, but for movie, we get a glimpse how the world look like in Rocky's eyes. The wavy echolocation visual without color. It's such a beautiful POV and important one to show we're different, but both Grace and Rocky are so clever to not make any assumptions. If they're different, they make accommodations. And both of them are so clever and funny and thoughtful for each other, and care so much for each other, no wonder they should be the best friends.

### Grace and Rocky

"He's so excited about us both getting home. There's no harm in pretending for a little while." When Grace accepted the fate he'd be sacrificed, I'm crying. So much pain, and then there's the resignation. He really doesn't want to die, but Earth has abandoned him, for a greater good that he isn't a part of. He understands, and he doesn't even want Rocky to be upset about it, so he shoulders all of it, his own coming death, hiding all of it to himself.

"I've made peace it." Did you? Did you really, Grace?

"I thought you made peace." "I didn't mean any of that. That's just something you say." Of course he didn't really mean it. Of course he wouldn't have wanted to die if he can have a chance to live. Earth took that chance away from him, making the choice for him; but Rocky gives the hope of living and life back. Rocky saves his life.

Love it so much that the music and song when Grace made up his mind to go save Rocky one last time, risking himself to run out of food: the music is singing "Run away home". And exactly when the lyrics is "we are going home", Grace is the farthest away from Earth, knowing full well he'd never make it back, but closest to Rocky. Earth is not home, Rocky is. Home is not a place that made choice for him and cast him out, but the people that chose him and saved him no matter the cost.

### Filming and visual

The film did so well with foreshadowing and mirroring. It begins with flashing red and blue lights when Grace first woke up alone on the ship, and it's the same flashing lights when Rocky risked his own life to save Grace.

There're the same mirroring AI lines of "Eye movement detected." being the first thing Grace heard when he's waking up from the drug-induced coma and when he's waking up from that head injury.

And needless to say, there are too many parallels and mirroring in their mutual saving. Rocky watched Grace sleep, and then Grace watched Rocky sleep when Rocky is hurt so bad and not waking up for a long time.

And the hugs. The hugs when Rocky just woke up from the hurt, the hugs when they're celebrating, when Grace learned he didn't have to die, when they're finally on the Erid and he learned the sun is saved. Love hugs so much, need hugs so much. Loving watching the hugs so much. I'm skin starved for hugs, so if I can't have enough, the least I can do is to watch someone else have it and get emerged in the love of it.

The music is retro and classic, the film color palette is retro, even the filming methods are retro, not much CGI, just mostly practical effect, and Rocky being the real puppeteers' job like the old way Star Wars and Lord of the Rings did the things. Love that retro style so much. Retro is the right path, 80s is still the golden age in everyone's memory.

### Writing and original publishing

Just learned that from wiki, that Andy initially self-published *Project Hail Mary*, and only gets public and officially published (by publishers) after the self-publish version gets enough attention. And it's something happened just recent few years. He's also used to be a software engineer, and then moving to become a full-time sci-fi writer. He's like a model, walking the path I'm longing to walk. Looks like so much things I can copy from his success. So much hope for me.

### Translated titles

Probably the very only thing I'm really unhappy about this film, is not the film's fault at all -- its Chinese title translation. But knowing that translated title first before knowing its original title, almost ruined it for me. The very unappealing translation made it sound so boring and old weary Hollywood movie, which it's totally the opposite way. They could have done so much better.

It would be so easy to just make a direct translation, but they (the distributors) just disappointingly have to choose the most unattractive and cliche and copied-from-old-success-examples one. That's definitely from someone in the business side, instead of creative side. The entertainment industry needs both sides, but it's almost always the business side endangering the creative works. Always.
