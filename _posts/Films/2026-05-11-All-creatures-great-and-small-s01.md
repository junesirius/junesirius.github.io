Funny story how I ran into this show: Since I enjoyed *Clarkson's Farm* so much, I'm starting to look for similar shows, farming ideally, documentary ideally, British/English ideally. And from the recommendation, somehow I was convinced this show is just the same kind but with the veterinarian. Same in the sense that it's a documentary (which it's not), and farming (which it's not), and English (close enough). And it's totally not set in the modern time, but what's better than current England? 1930-40s England! (okay was going to say Victorian era, but historian 30s is also good enough) With every gentleman wears like gentleman and ladies wear nice beautiful dresses, but also because it's in the countryside, not everyone can get fancy, then the local girls just wear the farm girl dresses, which are just a different kinds of charm. (So are the accents, I do get a little worried though if my accent might get carried away to the Scottish or old strong grumpy English ones if I spent too long watching this show.)

Anyway, I was just there, waiting to see some rooky vet treating animals, showing me all those cute (or scary) moments with animals, I found myself knee-deep in a drama show with all kinds of romance triangle and family drama stuffs going in all directions. Every single character have some complicated romance journey and family things going on in the background. But it's not saying this isn't an entertaining show.

I definitely start falling for all these characters all at once. Maybe take slightly longer for Tristan, but it's so apparent that he's also just another good lad, but also having a self-protective mask just like his brother does. Siegfried's mask is grumpiness and anger, and Tristan's is alcohol and girls (but he's not even really into being a womanizer, he has his heart somewhere/on someone too, just like James, but being too afraid to be rejected, he rejected her first, which is a whole classic nice little angsty subplot).

At the end of the day, vet and animals are actually just a little part of the story. The majority becomes the romance. For whatever reason (mostly it's misreading), I thought the initial novel the series adapted from is a real-deal journal from a real-deal vet back in the age, which makes everything happening real (sorta). But looking closer, it turns out just a novelist using the protagonist's name as pen name and writing in first person view, making it *look like* a real journal. Which totally explains all the drama and that *everyone* has their own problem. But also proves they really did a good job, they fooled me once at least.

Accidentally got spoiled by wiki when looking it up, so now I knew the author writes his real-life wife into the story and even use the same name. Which makes it no doubt James and Helen would end up together, which does take out quite a lot thrill, but it's good to know.

But still, it can get angsty to see how they try to suppress their mutual attraction and attention and love to each other, all the expression and eyes and gestures are so subtle but expressive, especially the Christmas episode when Helen is about to get married, and the looks on James's face and eyes, and those unfinished sentences of what he would have told her, but in the end he still couldn't. And Helen had so much hope, waiting for him to say it out, but she didn't ask either. They both have too many burdens on their shoulders, what they *should* do. But eventually still can't fight the love, James turning the car around and Helen running away from the wedding, it's so classic a troubled love story would be. It's almost unsurprising, but it's still so touching to see those beautiful suppressed emotions. That's why British/English shows are so good, at least so much more easily attract me than American shows, I think. English people is more introverted and subtle and suppressed, while Americans being too loud. And the suppressed pain and love and longing, those are the best part (just like destiel).

Tristan being so good with doggies and kids, that's almost a surprise but actually not really. He's the most kid-like one in the family and he's so playful and gentle and kind, he's like a kid sometimes but not all the times, but he speaks kids, he knows how to cheer them up and invite them to talk. Seeing him being so good with the kids must also make Siegfried proud.

The Christmas special episode is so good. What's it with English people and their obsession with Christmas? It's like so much deeper obsession than Americans. They really respect the festival like a huge thing, in show or out of show, and totally not just like a commercialized shopping festival. The most apparent sign: This show has a Christmas special episode every single year since aired first season, just like Doctor Who's obsession of Christmas, just like Merlin's finale. Won't they just run out of Christmas story to tell?

But come back to it, Christmas episode is so good, it's like the whole season's feelings and emotions and relationships all come back together, converged at the end of the year. (And funny how I'm rewatching *Clarkson's Farm* at the same time as watching this show, and notice the ending of each season of *Clarkson's Farm* is usually the harvest season in Autumn, and ending of each season of this show (too long the title so I don't want to type it out) is always the Christmas).

It surprised me so much that Siegfried lied about Tristan's exam grades and burned the letter, telling him he passed and he's proud even though he didn't. Maybe it's finally the time he realized he can't use exam grade to evaluate how good Tristan is. He's been personally training Tristan for the year and Tristan has also really demonstrated good ability when treating animals, and he's responsible and gentle to them, and Christmas eve he's willing to spent with kids and a sick donkey, it all says so much more than a grade paper. Tristan doesn't do well with exams and papers, but it doesn't mean he's not a good vet. And finally Siegfried starts to learn to trust his little brother and believe he's good enough (and believe himself in his ability of teaching Tristan good enough) that they don't proof from school.

On a similar note, in maybe episode 3 or 4 (or 5), the show does such a job when it hits the point to let Siegfried realizes he's just doing exactly same as the wealthy lady and her spoiled sick dog, and that's when he tore the check and decided to teach Tristan himself. "You can't expect to have a different result when you do the same thing over and over again." He's not talking about Tristan failing over and over again (and so worried about letting him down), he's talking about himself signing checks and sending Tristan to school. Tristan is like that sick spoiled dog, so the problem is not the dog, it's the owner. That moment of realization when he looks at the dog and looks at his brother, that's also the audience's sudden realization as he realizes, so the audience's expectation was initially debating between whether or not he'd give the check, and suddenly gets disturbed and surprised (just as surprised as Tristan right at that moment), but fully understood. It's such a nice moment of realization and parallel storytelling. Nice writing and editing and performing!

Cried when Mrs. Hall cried during the church chorus. Especially when my own family relationship is just as strained as Mrs. Hall's, foreshadowed for a whole season and finally building up to enough of emotional stress that it erupted through her tears. All those hope and pain about forgiveness, it's so much pain. She did nothing wrong, but she couldn't get the forgiveness from her son. There were so many times in this season for a whole year she tried to reach out but couldn't get through, she hurt him even if she was doing the right thing, and she cried and pained and mourned for the coldness in that relationship. I feel it all. *I feel it all.* I'm at both ends, on her ends and on her son's. I feel the resentment and I feel the fear and hope and disappointment and pain and looking for forgiveness and trying to give forgiveness. Her tears fall silently, it's another so beautifully subtle suppressed emotion in the most English way (and love that Siegfried was there for her).

They form an odd family, the Farnons and James and Mrs. Hall, but that's a beautiful found family. So very heartwarming and funny and deep and emotional. It's a beautiful show. Would recommend.
