It's so cute and healthy!! Haven't see such *healthy* relationship in a long while in any film/series, usually every character in those situations with those bottled up issues they *know* they have would need tons of therapy before they'd even come close to what Judy and Nick would say, and these two little one is just like, actually use words and actually talk, and actually say what they have in mind, like, not even hiding their issues in a box and keep it buried like anyone in their position would have done and lead to two more hours of screen time just for miscommunication, and these two just: "I don't care about the world, I care about you", "I can't lose you", and *apologized*. Apology and confession in the same sentence, basically, and recognize what they'd done wrong and how they want to do right, and right there, misunderstanding got solved and friendship/relationship/whatever-ship got resumed. It's so heartwarming. No wonder it's healthy for kids all age to watch. Kids need to know the right way to solve these things, and this right there is the perfect example.

Love their respective *unhealthy* issues so much too. It's just so destiel right there, but they don't even have to define their relationship, all along it's just "partner", they have pretended to be married couples for some case, but at the end of the day, they're not really in any relationship, they're friends for sure, but maybe more than friends, they're "partners", there is no better term than "partner" to define what they are. Unless, maybe platonic relationship? But that's a little too complicated to explain to kids. So, yeah, "partner" is fine, partner is good. More than good. Really good for them. (Dean, you hear me, this could work for you too. Borrow a page from their book, will ya?)

Love that Judy's issue has always been feeling that she has to be useful, she *needs* to be useful, needs to do something, to save the world, to do the right thing, to do whatever it takes even if it means it could cost her life, because, in her heart, she must have always felt herself dispensable, because she has so many brothers and sisters, so many bunnies that she can't be the only unique special one that matters, so it must not matter that if by doing the right thing it costs her life, there must not be anyone care this much about her, caring about her life more than herself. She's loved, by her bunny parents, but she's still neglected, because her parents' love are also among four hundred other siblings. Underneath her heroism and Savior Complex, that's her low self-esteem and self-value, even with all those training in the school and growth in the first movie, even when she gets to prove herself to the world and everyone else, deep down, she still doesn't see herself important enough that worth her to make enough effort to take herself into consideration when she goes out saving the world. She's exactly like Cas, like the angel who has thousands of siblings that he can't begin to understand himself as a being is also important, to himself, to someone else personally.

Love that Nick's issue comes from the childhood trauma of being bullied and mocked, always feeling insecure, always being abandoned, always alone, no one stayed for him, no one cared enough for him to feel loved, no one takes care of him but himself, doesn't think he deserves the good things, doesn't think he deserves to be loved, but always so protective of things that he chooses to protect, the ones he loves. When Judy is willing to sacrifice her life saving the world, Nick is willing to sacrifice his life saving her. The world didn't treat him well enough for him to be willing to sacrifice his life to save it, but for those he loves, he'd give anything. So right there, he's exactly like Dean with all the insecurity and protectiveness.

Is it really that all the stories can be seen as some variation of destiel in other universes, or that my brain has been rotten by destiel so much that I can't see anything else?

In any case, love the movie so tremendously, even if not for the story it's trying to tell, for the destiel undertone that I know I'd always enjoy so very much.
