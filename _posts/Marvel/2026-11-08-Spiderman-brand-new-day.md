so very annoyed that I came back to watch Marvel again after I said I stopped liking them, but apparently I still couldn't really let them go and not watching them once the new ones came out, even though it's been a few months late, I still wouldn't be able to resist watching it (I blame it on Vision this time, if it's not for watching VisionQuest for the past two days, I wouldn't have been back on this trend watching another MCU movie). And it's even more annoying how much I still love-hate them.

As much as I stopped liking Tom Holland, the story has a good background context: the amazingly angsty amnesia trope. Which means a lot of grief about loss that's not actually loss but still feel like loss, and repression, and pining, oh my god the amount of pining and yearning and longing that's just too delicious to savor.

And that "I love you" but "I don't love you, I don't even know you." the rejection from the love of your life, the heartbroken, the pain and torture and tears and shakiness, the little hope that gets snuffed out, but still, the little moments that the three of them get to spend together in the apartment, just chatting, like it's the same old time, the little hope that just has to stay there wishing that maybe they could still be what they used to be, what's lost might can be found again. The friendship in all this, it's very pleasant to watch and gives a little smile while watching.

Doesn't like how the plot depicts Frank Castle. Maybe because I loved the old Netflix version of Frank too much. Same with how I stopped liking the new MCU movie version of Matt Murdock, it's only the old Netflix version that I'm so in love with (okay DDBA is sorta alright, at some places, when I can forget it's related to MCU). But the movie version of Frank in this one, it's almost like they only invited him back to be a part of this movie because they (the studio) wanted some familiar faces to be responsible for attracting old fans but they also don't want to pay any big money on any of the real big names, so they got Frank and Banner and White Widow. Like, they want us to still remember Peter is connected to all the big events of MCU, but still not big/important enough to really get any of the big names in here, so they get the "somewhat related but not as expensive" people in there, dangling carrot in front of a donkey (the candies for the audience, that is). It's just kinda dick move. They don't really care about Frank or Banner or White Widow here, they just want their faces to show and wish that's what fans would pay for (which, actually works, because I'd a lot less likely watching this without knowing Frank's in there; which is even more annoying, because now that they know they can do that to get fans pay for the box tickets, they'd do that again, and again, and again).

Frank's characterization isn't good. He's flattened, kinda like Matt's in the one-time cameo in another Spider-man movie (can't remember which one). He's just like the heavy-machinery veteran maniac who only knows how to kill. All his solution to problems are big guns. Which, admittedly, is kinda the cliffsnotes of his usual action, but not *all* what he'd do. Simplifying him like this is just going to so easily attract a group of new fans who don't know much else about him other than just big guns and snarky funny. That's the other thing that bothered me so much. I get it that Frank can be funny at times and ease the story sometimes, he did that in original DD and DDBA sometimes, but he shouldn't be the one that's responsible for the whole movie's comedic tone. But here, he certainly becomes that role. Was about to say there are no comedic roles in this movie but quickly realize Banner and White Widow too, setting Yelena's office in the hot bath is definitely on purpose to make audience laugh in a very blunt clumsy way.

Frank's function in this one is basically: making snarky comments and quarrels with Peter (that's supposed to be funny and slowing down the intense pace a little), carry big guns and add enough action to this supposedly action-heavy movie (which it does serve the purpose and get some really good shots, especially in the fight with Hulk, but I don't see the point why Hulk has to be in this story, it feels so very... stretched, like, it's definitely not in the first natural draft of the story, it's after so many nudges from the studio comments and they have to figure out a way to squeeze Banner in and this is what they bake to the best they can without making it look like it's forced -- it totally still looks like it's forced in there), make the final blast that almost kill Peter (totally can't see the point of why it has to be there, just to show how brainless and careless Frank is? We've spent decades establishing how soft Frank can get with teenager girls, how much kids would remind him of his own lost kids, how his vigilante justice is only towards gangsters and criminals, but never kids, certainly not superpower kids, it's just not something Frank would do, let alone doing it in a way that would almost kill Peter, it just shows how much of big-muscle-without-brain he is, but that's totally not him, and it's not fair to leave that impression for anyone who might not know him before this movie and only get this about him out of this movie, it's not fair for his image -- it's just bad characterization and depiction).

There are indeed some small moments around Frank that's not as bad, like the part he'd promise to keep MJ safe when Peter asks that of him, then he'd go help Peter out when MJ asks that of him. Don't quite like the cabinet with all the photos setting, feel like it's a bit too straightforward. Kinda like his new home, it looks good and suits him. Like that he asks if the two kids like chili because he cooked chili. Like that he calls them "kid", kinda remind me the way he'd always call Matt "Red", he just doesn't like calling people their names (make me want to re-re-read "It takes a village" again).

About Jean Grey. Holy shit, didn't realize they'd re-introduce Jean Grey to MCU like this. And, can't describe how happy I was when I realized that's Max -- oh hello again lonely creepy child with stubborn and strong mind who'd love and hate so strongly, but also still just a lonely traumatized little girl who'd long some love and company. It's so interesting to see Max -- I mean Jean Grey, but I can't stop seeing it's Max right there, red hair, green eyes, I still see Max -- to have some psychic superpower. Can't help wondering how it feels to act that kind of role, almost like Eleven. We got so many superpower kids now, Eleven can use the brain to kill, Will can, and now Max can too, in a different universe. She fits this kind of role too well. Look forward to seeing more of her in the other movies down the road.

The plot twists of the story: not very unexpected. Like, it's just so obvious the initial fake-villain wouldn't be the true villain, but the friendly one actually is. The director of the Damage Control doesn't look like an innocent guy since the beginning, wonder why Peter doesn't realize he's sketchy from the beginning. The clue of "V-max" is set to be a suspension but solved in a way a little anticlimax, but also sorta served its purpose, so I'll let this one pass. The part MJ and Peter kiss, it's so obvious it can't be real, I expected it to be a dream or brain-induced illusion, but turns out to be possession, but also very obviously an audience-baiting moment, like, it's almost feel like a hard requirement in the script-writing that the writers have to figure out a way to insert the kiss in there whatever way they can, and this ends up to be the way they find least abrupt and still fits into the story good enough -- it's still a bit too abrupt and uncomfortable. Especially from earlier we're already shown MJ has a boyfriend (which, funny enough, even until the credit the guy still doesn't have a name, it's just "the boyfriend"). Thus why I call it audience-baiting.

Kinda like the ending, not a happy ending, not MJ remember him again and everyone reunite, just a kinda hopeful ending: everyone is lonely together. Jean Grey is still out there alone (even after Peter promised he'd be there for her, she's still alone); Peter is still alone on top of the roof; MJ alone on the roof trying to remember but can't; Ned alone in the shop (but the muscle memory remembering Peter is giving so much hope). So, a hopeful ending, good enough as a preparation for a next movie to come, just more of commercial movie, more spiderman movies, more money for the studio to earn using and reusing the character, the same old trick (that annoyingly does still work). That's why it's so unsurprising that the plot of "spiderman almost died" just wouldn't work -- unless the studio is willing to give Tom Holland up, they'd never kill him off and we know that, so spiderman is definitely not in any real danger and he'd just get better, so none of the threat would pose a real threat, which makes the story emotionally way less connected than it could have been. Audience are getting familiar and tired of the studio trick, they'd better come up with something real good and new, instead of just keeping feeding us industrial level plot.

The spiderman "turning dark" part of the plot is, kinda interesting, but once it's shown once, it's also not new anymore. It's unsurprising the dark side would take over when the normal version couldn't handle -- because, of course they'd take over, that's like the standard procedure, and of course it would malfunction when he tries to suppress his feelings, of course he'd lose control when he most wants to -- all standard procedure. They (the studio) did find a rather clever way to change the spiderman setting, turning him from the mechanic web setting to organic web setting seamlessly (is it really seamless? still a bit forced, it seems, like, it's also one of the hard requirements that we can just see as if seeing a bullet point sheet for the writers to accommodate for the story, like the recipe is cooked with too much force and not enough organic, the downside of working with a big studio as a writer to sacrifice the self-expression and creativity). And now they (the studio) get what they're after, from this point on, they can start using the organic web setting for Tom Holland version just like they had for Andrew Garfield version, to get more retro, to get more old fans.

Speaking of old fans and nostalgia scenes, the scene with MJ falling and Peter jumping down with her, yeah that trick never gets old, all spiderman movie should have a falling scene like that to scare the shit out of you, isn't it? We know Tom Holland is safe with the contract so we know spiderman is, but can't say the same for MJ, so yeah that part is still scary.

Speaking of that, now that to think about the MJ's role, knowing it's actually his wife he's kissing now, wonder if it'd feel different during acting for Tom Holland and Zendaya. Feel like it's definitely very easy for Tom Holland to act all longing and loving now, for MJ or for Zendaya, all the love are right there in his eyes. Wonder how long that charm/curse could last, the charm/curse of love between spiderman and the love of their life for all the past generations of actors. Just wait and see this time. Maybe they'd have it work out, maybe third time is the charm.

Guess overall would have to say this one isn't too bad. Still a commercial studio popcorn movie, but worth watching when you have a free afternoon. As much as I don't want to admit that I might still care for these characters in this universe, I'd still have to watch Avenger 5 when it comes out next month, and I'd be so annoyed that I still wouldn't be able to give it up and completely stop caring for it. Talking about an annoying ex that you still might hope it could work and things would get better. Don't keep the hope high. Expect it to disappoint. I'm warning myself.
