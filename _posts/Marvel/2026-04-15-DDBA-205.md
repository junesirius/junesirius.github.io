oh wow, the parallels in the storytelling, three storylines weaving together, past and present, cause and consequence, it's not the grand design of fate or destiny, it's the grand design of the writers and creators, and all come out really well.

Fisk and Matt are always the biggest contrast of this show, and because Fisk has Vanessa, Matt has Foggy, it naturally makes Vanessa and Foggy also a parallel. That is also how Dex measures the scale: Vanessa orders him to kill Foggy, so it's only just if he kills Vanessa, so if he did this, his score would be even.

And how Matt measures his scale: he tried to kill Dex, and so tonight, saving Dex is also his penance to make it right again.

<br>

Vanessa has the brain, Foggy has the heart. So there's beauty in how they die: Vanessa is killed by a fatal wound to her brain, and Foggy is killed by a bullet to the heart.

Foggy's heart taught Matt how much they should care about people, have mercy, that almost sounds like Matt as Daredevil's origin story, why he put on the mask. And Vanessa's first encounter with Fisk, making the arrangement of putting that painting out there, knowing the one who walks in will see the value of it, see the loneliness in themselves, therefore also plays a critical part in the origin story of Fisk turning into Kingpin.

Even as a Matt/Frank shipper, I can't argue anymore that Foggy plays a larger part in Matt's life. (But shipping is shipping, as much as I enjoy the nice warm healthy friendship between Matt and Foggy, it's the pain and tension and inevitable tension between Matt and Frank that I crave most. And I can't say enough how much I love *It Takes a Village*. There is just no better work than this I've ever seen anywhere.)

The hiding and escaping scene when Matt tries to save Dex while Dex has almost given up, stumbling across the tunnels and basement of the church, I got reminded too vividly that's exactly what *It Takes a Village* described Matt and Frank did. I longed to see some more Matt and Frank's interaction, and all I got is Matt and Dex, and I'm not complaining. What's with Matt keeping getting himself involved with murderers like Frank and Dex? There's a very certain beauty in their contrast and tension, a danger of it, a suppressed instinct of killing and saving among all three of them. Is it too good a dream of hope to see all three of them teaming up together?

All the time Matt is saving Dex, he's thinking about Foggy, thinking about the man Dex took away from him, but it's also memory of Foggy that made him save Dex, the irony and full-circle of their story, it's still so messed up but also so beautiful. Dex would never have known it's because of Foggy that Matt saved him.

All the time Matt is struggling and it's so close both him and Dex would nearly get found out by the taskforce, I prayed Frank to jump into the situation to save both of them and start the threesome bickering and disgusting and swearing. *That* would be a quite good thing to see. (I feel no guilt of already trying to look up Matt/Frank/Dex on AO3, and the only thing I found is a very well-written threesome porn, and I adore the writer who wrote it and very apparently partial to Matt/Frank just like I do. Great minds think alike.)

But honestly, it would be such a good thing to see the three of them team up together. It would be a pain to try to stop the other two killers from random killing, and admittedly both of them are (adorably) psychopath, the setup scene with Dex the previous episode when he claimed to have seen Frank to draw out the taskforce and kill all of them with forks and diner knives, it's just a really nice setup scene to show the psychopath side but also so easy to love. That's a successful anti-hero to build up to. Almost forget Marvel is actually quite good at writing anti-heroes, so much better than writing actual heroes. (Or actually, isn't it just the irony of hero stories? People expect so much from heroes, they have to be perfect or they're hated, but for anti-heroes, they can be loved for doing one right thing, same for villains.)

In any case, I miss *It Takes a Village* so much, it's been a while since I last read it, maybe it's time to re-re-read it? Can't see another one as good as it coming out any time soon anyway.

<br>

Can't say it's a surprise that Vanessa ends up really dead. We don't know that for sure last episode. I kinda expected that to happen, because how else can you have a big enough twist to tear apart the story to make some more impulsive changes, big enough to keep the show being entertaining after the sensory insensibility of already seeing blood and violence every single episode.

But it's a really nice touch to give Fisk (and audience) some hope mid-way that maybe she can make it this time. The surgery is successful, and she's sober for a short while, as if all go back to normal again. If she made it and Dex made it, then there's just gonna be more of the same thing that we've seen for five and a half seasons. But no, the backlash after the brief sober is so harsh and strong and powerful, that's the best kind of emotional surprise.

We can almost already see it coming during that brief sober moments, she doesn't look much like herself anyway, not that canny woman that always calculates everything, she looks tired and simple. The last thing she asked for is a pineapple juice with ice that she doesn't use to like. The last words she said are to ask Fisk to retell their encounter story. And the editing is just so good that the overlapping of the first word "Hello" with her last word. It's going to destroy Fisk and he's gonna tear open the whole city without thinking about any consequence. He's lost all the things he really cared about, there's no one here to bring out his best side, so now the city has to witness his worst side. (unless, just like Matt got reminded of the memory of Foggy and that makes him save Dex, hopefully by some miracle, at some ultimate critical moment, Fisk gets reminded of the memory of Vanessa and that makes him spare Matt or Karen or someone we really cared about.)

(But meta-story wise, we already know there'd be a sequel, so we already know Matt would be fine, Dex is also gonna be fine, so it's only Karen and BB we really really worry about. And among the two, BB is the more dangerous "sacrifice-able" one from the storytelling point of view. BB isn't really protected by anyone, Karen has Frank on her back. And Daniel is already freaking out, and killing her would have the least real consequence to the whole MCU universe. And to be blunt, she's the most replaceable for now (telling story using short videos is a good way of showing and making it following the modern media trend, but there're other ways of doing it). She's really really in the most danger here.)

<br>

Buck's back story and a freaked-out Daniel, the C-story is overshadowed by the A- and B-storyline, but still a charming one. It's good to see Wesley again, and it's rare to see Buck without fancy suits. Buck really is a good successor to Wesley. Both are so polite and gentleman. It's probably Fisk's best decision to hire them to be his righthand man. Without them, cool gentle (but also killers), Fisk would lose too much charm to stand a chance.

And it's really interesting to see Buck's first case from Fisk, the target is exactly the one saved by Foggy, even before they have their law firm. And also the day Fisk hired Buck is also the day he met Vanessa, two most important people in his life now (and he's losing them). The grand design and their interaction started before any of them even realized.
