Been a while following and chasing a current show as it airs, instead of waiting for all to finish release and binging it. DDBA S02 is one, The Boys S05 is another, and funny how their release schedule almost completely overlap, one in Tuesday and one in Wednesday and the Tuesday one (DDBA) almost always need to wait one extra day for the pirating sites to get a good enough source and subtitle, ending up to watch both of them on the same day, every day on Wednesday, making Wednesday one of the most expected day of the week for more than one reasons.

Anyway, but now DDBA S02 finished releasing its finale. And the finale is surprisingly well-written. Dare to say it might be the finest finale in quite many years in Marvel's recent TV shows and movies (and as disappointing as they are, yes I'm still following all of them, call it guilty pleasure).

But somehow, almost like the original Netflix versions of DD three seasons, this season managed to land on a solid ending. It's not a happy ending, with DD being in jail, Kingpin exiled, Foggy still dead, Karen still... being Karen, alone. But also so many new things, new hope, new turns, Bullseye is on loose, becoming the killing machine he's always been and happily so; we still have no idea who Mr. Charles is and what he's been doing; DD would be so easily escaping the jail if he really wants to; Kingpin would end his own self-exile if he so wishes to, and without Venessa, there's no one keeping him on his better side now; BB follows her uncle's path and starts the proper career in the newspaper; Heather Glenn is going to become the new Muse and it's been foreshadowing for the whole season; the new kid Angela is going to be the new White Tiger; still wishing to see more of Punisher, and Jessica Jones, and looks like Season 3 is promising those for us, though I completely forgot Luke Cage and Jessica Jones are together together, and wasn't as interested in the billionaire monk Iron Fist, so there's still a tad bit of chance that Season 3 is going to be shitty just because the show might want to bring too many people together. But with the good footing of DDBA 2's ending, there's still hope that the next season might keep the good rating, keeping DD a good-quality sub-brand under Marvel just like Netflix version used to do, and not ruin it.

About the plot: it's quite satisfying to finally see something comes out of the fight between DD and Fisk. They've been fighting since the beginning, so that's five seasons by now, and over and over and over again seeing them trying to kill each other, then don't want to kill each other, then save each other, then try not to let others kill each other, honestly it's a bit exhausting. Just like Karen and Bullseye suggested, it would be so much easier to just let others do the dirty work and get over with it. Almost like Batman and Joker. But no, they just have to keep chasing in circles and keep the show running forever. But if that's really the forever plot, doubt many people would want to stay for too long (some still might).

But the best job this season did, it's not to really end it (because neither of them are dead, so it's obviously still not over), but to still have a sense of ending there, to have Matt say the words: "It's over." *It's over for you, and it's over for me too.* Fisk can't be the major, and Matt can't go back to be the vigilante, so in a sense (in many senses), it's almost like Matt sacrificed himself to get rid of Fisk, but without either of them die (let's pretend all those other protests' death aren't the cost the both of them paid, including Foggy and Venessa's). Old three seasons it's the revelation for Fisk to realize Matt is DD, and now after two new seasons, it's Matt willingly expose himself to let the public know he is DD. And for a masked vigilante, this very much is a suicide. (But not necessarily.)

Love the use of white and black and red in all the final shots, love the parallel editing between Matt (and Angela and Jessica and Karen) fighting their way through the corridor to get to Fisk, and Fisk killing his way out, all brutal and bloody. And the contrast that this could have ended a whole different way, the public might have killed Fisk with their vast amount of population, but Matt stopped them (to let the series keep going).

Almost know for sure there will be a court scene somewhere, just didn't know where, and turns out the climax is the court. Matt has been Daredevil the whole season, almost as if he has forgotten his other half of identity as the lawyer, and finally, *finally* in the finale, he's back in the court, as the lawyer. Everyone's been waiting for that, and the writers/directors/producers know that, they keep our appetite, and finally at the very end when everyone's killing to see it, they give us what we wanted. Can't say it's a really brilliant court scene though, not enough argument, not enough truly legal fancy terms, but good enough tension for the plot, serves its purpose, pushes for the ending. So all things considered, it's a reasonably decent court scene and very suitable for the plot.

Still been thinking about the whole renewed series' title "Born Again", who's born again? Matt isn't reborn, Foggy isn't reborn, Fisk isn't reborn. No one really is reborn, then where does the "Born again" comes from? (okay, fair enough, it's the show reborn and comes back, but it can't just be that, right?)

Always love the animation around the title before the names come to the screen: everything crushes to dust, the plate of "Nelson, Murdock & Page" crushes, the city crumbles, everything and everyone suffers in pain, and then in the ash and dust, the statue of Daredevil stands up. He's born from the pain and ash and dust, but he still forms himself and stands tall for the city. I think the point and idea and the execution are all quite perfect, except just that little bit about the reborn thing still bothers me a little bit. Maybe we'll see more about reborn in Season 3?

And still, the whole Matt and Karen being together thing also still bothers me. They're sweet, they were friends for ages, they were bonded by the mutual friend's death, they were attracted by each other since the very beginning ten years ago -- sure, all these are true, but it *still* doesn't look right to me. Forget about my shipping brain for a sec (or not, maybe that's the root cause, I don't know), I enjoy so much to see them as friends, support each other as friends, the pure beautiful beautiful friendship, it's so valuable and amazing and pure and golden. All get explained away shallowly as their romantic attraction. Almost feel offended for losing that pure friendship. (pretty sure though the comic must have them as a couple somewhere, so them being together has quite many supporters) still, as much as I appreciate them together, it doesn't feel *quite* right. Like, not too wrong, certainly better than seeing Matt with Heather, but still a bit off. With Electra worked for me though, so maybe it's because my subconsciousness doesn't think Karen fits with Matt? Matt would be so easily attracted with dangerous opponents who could kill him and enjoys fighting him, the sick unhealthy relationship, therefore with all the whump and angsty ship with Electra and Frank and Dex, but with Karen, it's just too healthy, too sweet, too gentle, so it's not violent and sick and dark enough to feel right. But maybe, by being with Karen, by him and Karen accepting each other, it's their way of mutual healing and self-accepting, and finally feeling they deserve some healthy warm sweet relationship, finally allowing themselves to enjoy the love they can feel from each other. I should feel happy for them. (Except wholesome characters aren't so easy for fanfic writing, I'm so much more easily attracted by broken ones.)

In any case, a really well-done ending, a pretty solid season. Not saying it's perfect, and definitely could do better in some places. But compared all the other recent jobs with other Marvel TV shows and movies, already feel very grateful that they didn't ruin DD for us. Job well done, Marvel. Don't disappoint us in next season, I'll be following it in real time (I think).

PS: Near the finale, the subplot with Daniel and BB and Buck, those are some really nice subplots. Daniel's very very difficult choice between saving the girl he likes and doing the right thing versus saving himself, at the last moment of struggle, he finally chooses the right thing, with full knowledge that he'd be killed by Buck and there is no other way (if he runs, he'll get his mom killed). With a whole season and a half of being just an adorable/comedic character, he finally ends up becoming one worth remembering. Nice character arc, nice character building. Nice script writing.

PPS: The part with Angela turning into the new White Tiger, where that super power comes from actually bummed me a little bit. Didn't realize the power has been in the necklace all this time. And don't think anyone else really know (why else no one else would chase after this thing?). So all this time has been thinking it's just some sentimental symbol, but then all of sudden in the last episode, the girl has some fancy special effect or stunt shots, running on the wall and all that superhero shit. It's amazing and cool to see, sure, but just, still finds it too convenient and it bummed me.

P3S: Heather turning into the new Muse is fully expected, ever since she starts having those creepy look in her eyes and hallucinations and tear marks that are just like the mask, and the fake professional testimonies, and helping Fisk. Still wondering what her super villain power is gonna be.

By now it almost starts feeling like *The Boys* now, everyone somehow just can grow some super hero or villain power just so conveniently. Isn't it a little too easy to have powers?

P4S: Dex oddly still manages to be adorable and innocent-looking and likable. How did he manage that?

The redemption curve maybe contributes a little. And the pretty face too. But the rest, why on earth is he able to be so irresistably charming being a villain as he is?

P5S: A very surprise fall-for character: Swordsman. He's just all charming and elegant and good-looking. Totally didn't get to know him well enough, didn't even remember the character real name or the actor name, but already (accidentally but unsurprisingly) dreamed about him once after the show finished. And very glad to actually see him using the sword and doing the Swordsman stuff at least once on the show, instead of only being a symbol and locked up. That's quite satisfying to see.

btw, the character name is Jack Duquesne, was also in *Hawkeye* series apparently but totally forgot. And the actor is Tony Dalton, who, as wiki told me, is also in *The Last of Us* but also have no memory at all.
