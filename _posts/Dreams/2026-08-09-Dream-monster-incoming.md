In a room, small, crowded, so full of people that every human in the space is crowded against one another, shoulders to shoulders, back to back. Every available space is occupied by people. It's end of world setting, this room (or maybe a little like a wide corridor), longer than width, rectangular shape. Too many people escaping here, can't estimate how many people are here. I'm among them. This is like the last known safe place, but even this place isn't safe anymore. Monster is coming, but we don't know from where. People are panicking. I'm a little worried, but not super scared or panicked. Like I'm kind of confident I'll survive the monsters or the end of the world somehow, like I know I have some special ability or privilege or too important to die with everyone else.

The floor of the room starts shaking, like waves. Every human in the room shake with the floor. People are shouting "Monsters incoming!" And they are getting more and more panicked.

Most of them aren't really moving, just staying in this over-crowded room, but I'm actually trying to squeeze through the crowd, moving toward one end of the room where there is a window. The window looks like my childhood bedroom window, but the whole room looks noting like my childhood bedroom, way bigger and plain and no design in any of the walls. The window is actually wider, but somehow the feeling around it just reminds me of the childhood bedroom window.

Mother and Father are with me, a few steps ahead of me. When they're closer to the window, they move on top of the crowd, moving by stepping on people's shoulders. This also helps them move onto the window more easily because the lower edge of the window is higher or around average human's shoulder. The floor is still shaking, there's still the sense of dread that monsters are coming and emerging from under the floor, like Hell breaking, but no actual monster are really seen.

Mother and Father move over to the window and start climb down the building along its outer wall. When I get to the window and look down, it's really high, like at least 10 floors high and there is no steady structures on the wall to allow climbing down. There is only one soft water pipe linking from under the window to the ground, they just slip all the way down using that water pipe. I got scared for the first time because of the height. I tried to ask Mother if this is the only way (Father is already reached the ground, Mother is behind him, but way ahead of me), Mother says there is no other way, if I want to live than I need to do this, just don't look down. So I climb onto the pipe, the pipe is very very thin, barely thicker than a string, white, but looks steady enough that it won't break. I'm not afraid of it broke, I'm more worried I'd loose my grasp or the slipping down would abrase my palm skin and it's gonna hurt. But like Mom said there is no other way, so I hold the pipe with two hands, legs crossed it like one would when climbing a tree (even though the me in reality never know how to climb a tree), and just slip down like that, not looking down. Still very worried and nervous the whole time. But then I get to the ground safely.

This is the outside of the building where that "last safety room" is, but the building doesn't look very special, just like a normal resident tall building. No significant decoration or landmark or anything, easily blend in with other buildings around it. We are now in a narrow back alley, like thin small alleyway in traditional Jiangnan town would have. And I follow Mother and Father to somewhere else that I know they know are safe and only available to us. It feels like they're veterans to survive end of the world setting  like they've already spent a lot of time figuring everything out.

The next scene, I'm in a big grand hall, all white tiles and walls, clean and reflective, like a fantasy school building and the biggest conference hall. I move from the hall to the hall's linked restroom. The space is mostly empty, there might be people in further distance that I didn't pay much attention, but there isn't anyone in my vicinity.

The restroom is equally big and grand and white and clean, look more like a conference room than a restroom. I go into one of the stalls, and then almost like out of nowhere there are other girls in the restroom too, heading to me, surround me, at least five six of them, higher grader looking, as if I might be a lower grader highschooler and they're the senior year highschooler. Mostly blonde, the stereotypical high school bully group kind of vibe. They corner me, the leading girl ask me difficult questions. Can't remember the exact phrasing of the question, but I think her point is to demand an answer why I didn't try to save other people when I had the chance. And I don't remember what my answer was, just remember thinking this is a really difficult question and I don't have a good answer. But I'm also not feeling guilty exactly, like I didn't think it's something I should do but didn't or I could do better but didn't, I genuinely didn't think there is a chance for me to do that or I couldn't have saved them even if I tried, or something along the line. I didn't do anything wrong.

And then next scene, I'm in my childhood home. We're packing and leaving, or rather, I have the awareness that Father is packing and leaving, and he's going to somewhere I wouldn't see him for a long time, and it's going to be a high-stake place, we don't even know if he'd be okay or if/when he'd be back. I'm in my bedroom doing some mental preparation because I'm still processing that, but then I hear the sound of the door, and when I go out of my bedroom, the home is empty, Mother and Father already left the home. I knew there weas packed suitcase on the floor near the front door, but now they were gone. I know they just left. I debated for a second whether or not I'd like to chase down the stairs to try to catch them before they leave (my awareness is it's Father leaving, but I don't remember what it says about Mother; the feeling is like as if it's the end of the world wartime, so all available fighters need to go to some place to help defense the world, and Father is very capable or even the best, so of course he has to go). I am also aware that I'm still angry about him (because of the reality me is still feeling the anger constantly after the hurtful even three months ago and the relationship never improved), so I ask myself, do I want to keep feeling angry or do I want to see him again, which is likely my last time to see him, I wouldn't know if he'd come back or not. And it's an easy but very emotional choice. I rush down the stairs outside the home door, wishing they haven't left yet. And the first thing I see outside the building gate is the two suitcases (blue, one big 28 size, one small 24 size, carry-on suitcase, both are my commonly used suitcases if I need to go somewhere long term), so that means they haven't left yet, I'm a little more relieved, and searching for Father.

Father is on a very cool looking motorcycle, red, Harley, old-style, very beautiful, very muscle looking. (Pretty sure this is because of my imagined "my dream house" I wrote yesterday where I wrote the dream house's garage also need to have a motorcycle just like that and imagine the visual), Father is on it, very athletic and powerful and battle-ready looking, talking with Mother about something, as if arguing. I just stand there taking them in, like visually trying to memorize this image, without too much interaction, just savoring the moment as is, that's enough for my own "see him for the last time" memory. And then Father calls out to me and talks to me. I don't remember what he says. One of the things is he demands me to move the two suitcases closer to him, help him attach them to his motorcycle behind him. It looks like very unstable when they're attached. I'm a little worried if the motorcycle would hold, but Father doesn't seem to worry. And then we exchanges some more words, mostly clam an plain and mundane. And at the end of the conversation, I tell him I cherish this last moment we talk, but even so, I'd still like to point out the thing I still didn't like in the way he speaks to me, the way he still demands me to do something (move the suitcase over), and that's why I still need my boundary and I'm still angry with him. He doesn't answer before I wake up naturally. Alarm isn't triggered yet, but the music is playing in the background. (Think I really miss them after so long of no contact, but it's more missing an imagination that's not really them. And now that I face the question in the dream, whether I'd see them for the last time, whether I maybe already had, it's swaying, I don't have a definitive answer. Time will tell. I'll make the decision when I have to. For now, as hurt as I still am, I'll keep the reality as is, and let me brain do the mourning and missing by itself and conjure dreams for me as it pleases, outside my conscious control.)
