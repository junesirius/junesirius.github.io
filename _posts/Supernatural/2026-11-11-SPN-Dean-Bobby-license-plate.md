Dean gives Bobby a license plate, with Bobby's name on it. As his birthday gift.

Bobby isn't impressed.

"How on earth did you get that idea and your brilliant brain thinks that's a good idea?" Bobby grunts so hard when he takes a look and sees the big brand-new "BOBBY" right there in the center of the plate.

"I dunno, just thought, you know -- " Dean spreads his hands, gesturing at something in the air helplessly. "-- it's fun, or whatever."

"And for whatever reason, you got that the legit way?" Bobby flips the plate this way and that, still grumbling, but hasn't put it down somewhere yet, like leaving it on the table or shelf or anywhere, as Dean thought he would.

"Yeah, yeah --" Dean scratches the back of his neck. Uneasy. He turns around, moving a book on the table nearest to his hand, putting it on top of a different pile. Can't find the right place to put his hand. "It's not much hassle, you know. They just ask for an ID -- and we got plenty of them lying around to use -- and all you need to do is to pay a few hundred bucks and boom, there it is."

Bobby hums, walking closer, putting the book back where it was before moving away. "Well, it's gonna be a hassle for me now, you thought about that?"

Dean frowns. He hasn't. "Why? What do you mean?"

Bobby looks at him with eyes that are reserved for looking at morons. "You run around doing the job with your real name taped on your forehead every day or what?"

"Shit." Dean stares back, looking at that license plate, then looking back. Looking like a real moron now.

"Or you want everyone to remember your license in a heartbeat when you run a red light or cut someone off?"

"I didn't think about that." Dean rubs his cheeks, sighing.

"I know, you idjit." Bobby lets out another grumbling sound that's a Bobby-patented version of chuckling.

"I mean, you don't have to use it. Just, leave it somewhere. I'll -- I'll get you something else." Dean rubs his hands. Fidgeting. "You know, just to say, happy birthday."

"Nah, don't worry about that." Bobby pats his shoulder with his free hand. "Get yourself comfy, will ya? I'll be back in a minute. We'll have dinner then."

"Okay." Dean heads to the kitchen when Bobby heads to the door.

Bobby comes back with empty hands, and Dean forgets about this whole thing soon enough.

Only to be reminded when he has to call Bobby for backup a few months later.

The case is tricky, his Dad doesn't answer his phone and his brother is at school, so, naturally the only one he can call and knows would answer is Bobby.

As expected, Bobby answered and agreed to come join him.

Bobby arrived earlier than Dean thought.

Dean approaches the edge of the crime scene and Bobby is already there, next to his old beat-up truck, in his probably best suit, with his hair all groomed fancy -- the perfect image of an FBI agent.

Dean joins him, having his own FBI badge and everything ready.

Police is already there, talking with Bobby. Dean walks toward them, capturing part of their conversation.

"That's your car, Agent Willis?" Dean hears the policewoman say.

"That's what the badge says," Bobby answers smoothly.

"Or shall I call you 'Bobby'?" The lady nods at the rear of the car.

Dean slows his step for a second, wondering how come they're already on a first-name basis, or if the detective meant it in a sarcastic way to tear apart their FBI facade. Both seem equally likely and worrisome. Until he walks to where they are and sees the license plate.

"Shit," he murmurs under his breath.

Sure enough, right there in the center of the plate, are the big, bold, clear letters: "BOBBY".

"Oh, that," Bobby says, nonchalant, glancing at where the police lady was nodding at, acting as if he only just realized what's on that license plate. Dean is close enough to both of them now that he knows Bobby sees him coming. Bobby gives him a quick glance.

"I get that a lot, but no, not my name," Bobby says, without missing a beat, eyes back on the police lady. "One of my boys."

"Your son?" The lady's expression gentles visibly.

"Something like that." Bobby glances in Dean's direction again. But Dean isn't sure if it's time for him to jump into the conversation. His fingers are itching around the fake badge in his pocket.

Bobby saves him the trouble of wondering, waving him over while speaking to the police lady. "Now, Officer, if you'll excuse us, we have work to do."

"Of course. I'll be right here if you need anything." She walks away.

"I didn't know you actually put it on." Dean nods at the plate that seems to be more troublesome than anything useful.

"Gotta put it somewhere." Bobby shrugs, like it's not a big deal. "Might as well put it where it needs to be."

"Didn't mean to eavesdrop earlier, but heard something funny. So what, now *I*'m the one called Bobby now?" Dean can't help huffing a laugh.

Bobby gives him another "you aren't the brightest one, are you?" look. "Had to figure out a way to stop the police from searching for one 'Bobby Willis' in the FBI database, ain't I?"

Dean hums. "Still, kinda feel bad if it's just adding more trouble. I can always get you something else if you need."

"And I said don't worry about it." Bobby pats his shoulder, ducking his head in a way that Dean suspects is to hide his smile in his beard, before turning to walk toward the crime scene.

"Get me a bottle if you want, boy. That's some idea for what to give next time, genius." Bobby says over his shoulder.

Dean catches up to his steps. "Alright. Noted. Next time."

Then thinking it over. "I mean, tomorrow, or whatever, whenever you want."

When he's walking side by side with Bobby, the old man gives him another look, and this time, unmistakably, with a fond smile. "I'm glad you called me," Bobby says, quietly. "Always hate to think someone has to work this job alone."

"And I'm glad you came," Dean answers reflexively.

"Of course," Bobby answers easily.

Dean chuckles softly, feeling warm all over him. It's always just like that, so easy with Bobby, ever since he was the age that still calls him "Uncle Bobby". Always way, way easier than with his Dad. He swallows. He thinks he'll always remember the look on Bobby's face when he answered "Something like that" earlier. He's... something like that to Bobby, and Bobby's also... his something. Always has been, and always will be.
