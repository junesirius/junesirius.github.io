Dean doesn't celebrate his birthday, it's just another day like all the rest of the days, but at least he'd always "celebrate" Sam's birthday, because he enjoys trolling him about getting older with risks of having less hair and watching Sam turning into bitch face, that always cracks him up for a good five minutes no matter which year, especially when he got close to 30s, then 40s, then 50s -- what's siblings for, right?

Cas's birthday -- the one they just sorta all quietly agreed on the date a little arbitrarily, the day he came to Dean -- is always a blessing. Cas doesn't really see the point of birthday celebration, but whenever Dean is available, there'd always be some cake of some form: more than once, it's pie in the place of a cake, "It's round and sweet -- it's a cake, what's the problem?" Dean would say.

Sam would chuckle, and Cas would smile with eyes bright, so it's all worth it.

The three years when Bobby was still around, he'd give Cas something too; he'd grumble a little if Dean caught it and called it a birthday gift. "Just something I got around here," he'd say, "Thought it's best he take it. That's all." Cas would take it then, put it in his pocket. It was still in his pocket when Dean fished the soaked trench coat out of the water when Cas walked into the lake. Pages were all wet. Bobby had given him little pocket books. Dean made sure they were dried, later. But he hated that it got all wrinkled and he couldn't do anything to help. Like he couldn't help Cas come back -- not unless he comes back by himself.

Cas didn't mention those little books remained in his trench coat after. But Dean caught him touching and thumbing those wrinkled pages, contemplating, more than once, even when they were in Purgatory. Dean doesn't say anything. Just watches Cas stare at the book, then put them back in his pocket.

Bobby isn't around anymore.

Dean makes sure he gets Cas something every year.

The absolute hardest one is Jack's birthday. So hard that he'd almost always have to get himself half drunk on the day. The day he lost Cas. The day his world ended -- again. It had been, so many times, but his world always got pieced together, later, when Cas came back later. But that time, that day, was the day Dean fell down to the ground, knees in the soil, thinking, *This is it*. The scorched wings on the ground, Cas's wings. His heart got ripped out of his chest, his breath taken away.

That day Dean thought -- he knew, he was as well gone too.

And he lost his mom on the same day, and Jack, the kid lost his mother on the very same day.

How do you celebrate your birthday when you know that's also the day you lost your mother? That you never got the chance to even talk to her for once, when she was breathing? You can't. You just can't. But Jack deserves birthdays and cakes and gifts. Dean didn't do that too well back then, but he tries now.

"What about Winchester dinner time next week?" he'd say so casually to the kid one week before that day, as if he doesn't know what day that is, just a plain day like the others, just a good day for a Winchester's special heart-attack-inducing heavy dinner. Say it in words when the kid'd around, in prayers when he's not. And God or not, the kid loves it. And Dean makes sure there's pie after dinner, always. No candle though, and no song; he can't do that, and he doesn't think *God* actually counts the days when he'd have only five or six candles on his birthday cake. Jesus, they have a four-year-old as a God; even as each year passes by, when Dean quietly calculates his age (with a wince just thinking about how quickly time flies), he'd still chuckle and shake his head in amusement. But the smile would fade when his mind wanders back to the pair of scared golden eyes in the dark corner, and the shadows of scorched wings on the ground. He'd have to go outside, somewhere with air, breathing hard, as if this could help ease the pain in his chest. Cas would squeeze his shoulder or brush his arm or hold his hands more often than usual on that day. And Dean would squeeze or brush or hold back, and not let go. The tight chest feels more breathable that way. That's the only way that helped. Cas is still here.
