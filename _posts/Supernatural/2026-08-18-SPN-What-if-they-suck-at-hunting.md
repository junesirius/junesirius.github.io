been thinking, what is the possibility that Dean and Sam might actually suck at hunting? I mean the real normal ordinary free Dean and Sam, without Chuck interfering and making them heroes of his story. Remember the episode when Chuck gets fed up with them and gets the hell out of the world so Dean and Sam ultimately become just normal ordinary people without the hero buffs? Sam is so clumsy and gets terribly sick from a cold, and Dean has lactose intolerance and cavities all over his mouth. And they can barely open a lock to save themselves. They can't possibly fight against the monsters that really do have superhuman strength and speed in face-to-face combats, and they might not survive that case if Garth wasn't there.

So what if the real them, without Chuck's buffs, aren't actually so good at hunting as they think they are? Or, say, not necessarily suck, but definitely not "the best hunters in the world" like they always claim to be or praise each other to be. There are way too many times in the show that Sam would tell Dean "You're the best hunter in the world" or Dean saying the same thing back, as an encouragement or reasoning to ask each other to take some case or back them up or do something. But what if they really are not, when stripped of the divine intervention and protagonist plot armor and put on the same level as everyone else?

I can think of so many other normal human hunters that are actually objectively really good, that I'd think Chuck might not have that much attention or interest to give them buffs too, which means they'd be more likely the real actual "best hunters" in a fair comparison. Bobby and Rufus are normal human hunters without Chuck's buffs (probably), and they're noticeably actually really good at hunting from the few episodes we saw them going on the field trips; Samuel and those Campbell cousins too with their army-like actions and executions on the monsters; Jody and Donna and Claire too. (But one can argue that, the whole show, up until the last episode, is basically canonically all in-universe Chuck's writing, so then technically every single one of them have some writer's buff on them for the convenience of story-telling, so then no one is really "ordinary normal human without plot armor" because no one is free from Chuck. In that case, the real them, all of them, without Chuck, might also actually be bad or at least worse at hunting than they'd think they are. Sadly. Which means all the monsters can still so likely easily get to them when they take cases in the free new world. Which means the version of the future that Chuck showed Sam to break his hope in *The Trap* where "the monsters are winning" might not be too far from the truth, just the other way round; it's not monsters being too vehement and wild and winning, it's the humans hunters not being good enough and they'd just lose. Jack might be able to keep the monster number under control, but he can't always save every hunter when they run into dangers because of them not being good enough, without that being considered intervention. Which means it would be either *The Trap* version of the future happen slowly, or the free-from-Chuck world greatly relies on Jack inevitably intervening, which means taking away free will, which means Chuck round two.)

So maybe after Chuck is defeated, now that the world goes back to the normal world without the intervention and buffs, Dean and Sam's hunting abilities go back to what they really are, like maybe just ordinary mediocre reasonable human level (instead of hero level), and all those other things and traits that they never know are Chuck's buffs diminish gradually. Admittedly Jack wouldn't have deliberately done them any extra harm, but he has made up his mind to be hand-off; and the kid is only three years old and never really been much of a pure human (like, did he experience toothache or running stomach as a human apart from straight-up dying?), what's the likelihood he'd remember to keep the "not have rotten teeth however much dessert you eat" buff going on for Dean, or remove his lactose intolerance so that he can continue enjoy excessive amount of grilled cheese burgers? Or make Sam not clumsy (which definitely counts as intervention then, so if Jack doesn't do it, it would mean canonically in the last episode, in the post-Chuck era, Sam would trip over himself pretty much every other step, like maybe get a face-down on the lawn when playing throw with his son)?

Though personally I'd like to think Jack remembers and treasures the time Dean teaches him driving so fondly, he likes Baby enough that he'd subconsciously keep Baby's buff on, so that Impala -- despite our old girl is more than forty years old -- still wouldn't break down, unlike what happened in that *The Heroes' Journey* episode. But this would also turn off the alarm in Dean and Sam's minds, like if Impala break down every other mile, they might realize this (the "this is what normal people's life look like, you're having normal-people problem" thing) is happening (again) and be more careful in their daily hunting, but it doesn't, then they might think their newly developed clumsiness or easily picked cold or sickness or digestion problems are just them getting old (which is also true and not helping).

Which explains why the last case of Dean Winchester might be a case that looks so normal and easy at the first sight. They don't realize they are just normal human hunters without all the divine buff and protections, and that vampires actually have superhuman strength and speed. Other normal hunters die from vampire cases all the time. So it doesn't matter this should have been an easy case for them after so many years of hunting, they are not so good at hunting, at least not as good as they think they are. For all the cases they take after Chuck's defeat, they move slower than they think, they attack slower than they think, they dodge slower than they think, and eventually, one of these days, there would be a case caught up to them and end up with them bleeding out and gone down, and this is exactly what happened to Dean in the end. Which means this ending makes all the sense in the world.

(For the record, I hate this thread of thought so much. They are always the heroes in the story and in my mind, always so badass (once they pass the first few seasons of baby period). It almost feels like how little young Dean describes to kid Sam that John is the best hunter in the world and a hero, like how kids always think their parents are the best heroes in the world when they are young, but then one day you suddenly realize maybe your heroes aren't so badass, maybe they actually suck at their jobs and just plain ordinary normal humans and that eventually something you thought they should handle easily ends up costing their life. It breaks your heart and breaks the hero image. But my reasonable brain is so persistent and godawfully good at making connections and finding proofs that I can't even argue why it might not be the case. Which means this might very well be the case. God I hate it. Dean, my love, even if you might not be a badass hunter without the god buff, you're still the best hunter in my heart.)
