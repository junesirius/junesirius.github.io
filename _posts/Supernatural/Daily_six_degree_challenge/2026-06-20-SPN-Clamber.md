**Scene:**

Dean isn't much of a hiking person. For his defense, he got his fair share of hiking from hunting, thank you very much, so he's not particularly interested in the kind of hiking that doesn't require looking behind his back for wendigos or werewolves.

Which totally explains his grunting and grumbling on his way up the trails.

"Are you having trouble climbing this hill?" Cas looks back at him from about five steps ahead. But that five steps is already enough to put him behind a curve and half hidden by an overgrown bush.

Dean takes large steps climbing over some slightly steep rocks, keeping him in sight. "What? No!" He's slightly offended for some reason. "Don't you dare say it's last night's burger's fault or something."

Cas rolls his eyes. "I wasn't going to. But is it?"

"Absolutely not!" Dean glares at him. "My burger didn't do anything wrong." But then he has to pause to catch his breath before stepping up the next steep step. He hates to admit it, but he thinks his knees just crackled, is it really the retire age for him now, he wonders. "Whatever, just not my type of thing, I guess."

"I can carry you if you need." Cas offers, genuinely kindly.

Dean's heart flutters slightly, imagining himself on Cas's shoulder, and Cas can just move up the mountain like he's no weight, not breaking a sweat. He tries not to be too obvious but his eyes are definitely checking out those muscles on Cas's arms and shoulders. Yeah, definitely strong enough to carry him, with or without his grace.

"I'm not a baby or a princess," Dean protests. And his brain very unhelpfully lets him know, that's not exactly a no. He swallows.

"You don't need to be a female royalty or a child to be carried." Cas states simply.

Dean stifles a laugh but makes the pretense of rolling his eyes just for the sake of it, "Can we just go? I don't want to be in this wild when it's getting dark. You never know what's in those woods."

"This is a very safe area, Dean. I have checked beforehand. Many hikers have been here everyday." Cas reassures him, but he does turn back around and starts moving up the hill again. They can see the trail sign not far ahead.

"There might be snakes, or spiders," Dean grumbles, keeping up the pace so Cas is only two steps ahead of him. The thought that if he falls he bets Cas can be fast enough to reach back and keep a hold of him comforts him greatly, like what every action-rom-com movies must have, with the hero holding out a hand over the edge of a cliff to save the love of his life or some shit. It's oddly romantic and thrilling in his imagination. Not that he'd ever admit it aloud -- neither the imagination nor the fact that he even watched those shit.

"Do you know they're more scared of humans than the other way around?" Cas glances behind him, reaching out a hand to help him move up a particularly steep rock, and Dean takes it. Cas doesn't take his hand back after that, so Dean doesn't either.

"Sure," Dean answers half-heartedly, too busy making sure he doesn't step on anything slippery.

"I doesn't know you are afraid of snakes or spiders," Cas says next.

"I wouldn't call it 'afraid'," Dean protests, "they just give me creeps, you know? The way they look, I don't like things that don't have legs or things that have too many of them. Just two -- or four -- are good enough."

"Oh." Cas blinks, falling quiet.

Dean frowns, glancing at his face. He kicks himself in his mind. "Wait, no, I didn't mean..." he swallows, "how many do you have? I mean, in your true form?"

Cas's eyebrows knit together slightly. "There is no good way to map the physicality of celestial wavelength to creatural biology, but I don't have any legs in common sense, only six wings. So that is... six limbs."

"Wings are fine." Dean grins at him. "Wings are cool. And... and whatever you look like, I like it. A lot. Because... that's you. You know that, right?" His smile turns a little bashful. He still can't quite get used to saying saps like that, but he knows how bright Cas would turn into when he does, so he can't really resist.

Unsurprisingly, Cas's eyes brighten up, with almost real light glinting in them. "Are you sure?"

"Oh yeah, one hundred percent goddamn sure." Dean smiles wider, reassuring him. "Whatever you look, it'd beautiful, and perfect. 'Cause, you know, you are." He uses his available hand -- the one that Cas isn't currently holding -- to rub his cheek. It's hot there -- must be sunburn, instead of, you know.

"Even if I look like a snake or a spider?" Cas raises his eyebrows.

The muscle on Dean's face twitches. *That bastard. How can he find a loophole so quick?* He closes his eyes, lets out an amused huff, then opens them again, looking into Cas's eyes. "Yes, Cas, even if you look like a snake or a spider. I'd highly prefer not, but if you are, then yes, I'll still like it, no question asked, 'Cause it's you."

The tips of Cas's lips curl up. "Thank you," he says simply, as if Dean just did him some favor of some sort. He holds his hand tighter.

Dean beams at himself, pleased. He wonders about what Cas's true form without legs and with six wings really look like on the rest of their way up to the mountaintop.

The view is astonishing from the top. The small towns and cities scatter around at the edge of the mountain foot. Human civilization just looks so insignificant and distant from here. Dean takes a deep breath, feeling the fresh -- though a little thin -- mountain air filling his lung. He feels close to the sky.

"We should do something you enjoy next time," Cas comments. Dean opens his eyes -- not realizing when he closed them when he immerges himself with the nature -- finding Cas observing him closely.

"I am," Dean says.

"You were complaining all the way up here." Cas points out the obvious fact. "You don't like hiking. You only come because I wanted to, and you are indulging me, for which I am very grateful."

Dean edges closer to him, standing in his personal space. His hands moving up from Cas's hand to his arms, reaching his shoulders, then cradling his cheeks. "And what's wrong with me enjoying every moment of spending time with my boyfriend-husband-wife-bestfriend?" They never really talked about it, just that they moved in together however long ago; they've been living together; Dean cooks breakfast for both of them everyday, and Cas would take out the trash and wash the dishes if Dean cooked the dinner; they watch movie on the same couch, sometimes with fingers braided, other times wrestling for the popcorns from the same bowl; and they lie down on the same bed, saying goodnight in each other's hold. Dean figures they don't really need a name or definition for whatever this is between them, but that's not saying he's not pondering and ruminating the ideas of how to title Cas, and somehow, this is the moment those ideas and thoughts slip out.

"Shit," he murmurs when he catches what he just said, looking intensely at Cas's face, waiting for... something, anything -- whatever that is -- to happen, to change.

There is a faint bloom of blusher-pink creeping its way over Cas's cheeks. He blinks a few times, before answering bluntly, "May I kiss you?"

Dean chuckles, all worries thrown into the wind. He doesn't bother to answer that, just leaning in to press his lips onto his boyfriend-husband-wife-bestfriend's.

"And, we are going to the beach next time." Dean whispers at Cas's ear in between the busy business his lips got themselves into.

Cas hums happily. So that's settled.
