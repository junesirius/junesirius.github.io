> **Question:** I was wondering if not only Mary, but John had died that day as well, and Bobby had to raise both Sam and Dean. In what way, if any, do you think their lives, both Sam and Dean, and Bobby, would have changed?
>
> **Jim Beaver:** They would have been better.
>
> Bobby seemed to understand that parenting isn't just telling your children to do a good job and then leaving them to do it. He -- I think Bobby understood that people need care. They need love, they need attention. They need constant support when they're young. And, I don't think John understood that.
>
> But, and I don't know how Bobby understood it, but he did. Bobby certainly didn't get that from his father. But somehow he learned it and he was able to make good use of that understanding with Sam and Dean.
>
> And because of him, they are not complete idjits.

Jim Beaver's answer almost made me cry, I have to make everyone see his answer.

Bobby is the best father that Sam and Dean could ever have.

And Jim Beaver is the best Bobby.

<br>

John once told Sam and Dean: "It's not my job to be liked. It's my job to raise you right." (10x09)

But guess what, you *can* be liked, *and* raise them right. Bobby did it.

And John didn't really even raise them right, he just raised them to be like him. Guess what, Bobby taught them to unlearn that lesson as well.

Bobby told Dean when he became too much like his father: "You sound like your dad. You are a better man than your daddy ever was. So you do both of us a favor. Don't be him." (04x22)

And Dean listened.

It made me so sad and so happy at the same time to think what if John and Mary died at the same night, Sam and Dean lost both their parents, but then they'd have Bobby, maybe they'd even have Ellen or Jody as their surrogate mother figure, maybe, just maybe, they could have something of a childhood, they could be kids like they should be.

They would have been better.
