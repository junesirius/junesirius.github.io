> Q: 1103. What is the definition of a soul to you in a few words?

Dean has always wondered, ever since he learned that soul is a real thing, that there are demons in the world that would grant your wishes to trade for your soul, and now there are angels hoarding souls in the poor economy (he doesn't know how much he can really trust Balthazar's words, certainly not literally every single word, but that angel is also generous enough about giving away some truth and facts they've been searching for ages, when even Cas gets sketchy or hesitant about some things. That is to say, there must still be *some* truth to his words, which means souls *are* valuable to angels in one way or another).

And then there's also the fact that he just learned and firsthand witnessed Cas touching human souls when they are still very much alive. According to Cas, it's an extremely painful experience -- physically there will be no permanent harm, but to the soul, it would be a torture. Dean didn't get the chance to stop Cas from torturing that kid, and he witnessed Cas searching for Sam's soul in his chest but found nothing, and then the same process to their grandfather. Samuel Campbell is probably one of the toughest guy and hunter Dean's ever known, but even him has to grunt and grit his teeth to endure whatever that must feel when Cas poked his arm deep inside Samuel's chest. Dean watched him squeeze his eyes shut and sweat falling down his bald head, watched Cas's arm buried to the elbow inside his body where heart is. There was golden light shimmering where Cas's arm contact those men's chests. The same color and light whenever they send a spirit to Heaven peacefully, like Molly, the ghost that doesn't remember she's dead and finally let it go and moved on in the beautiful, bright, first light of the new day's sun. Dean remembered watching that golden light rise up from her ghostly form and float towards sky, towards Heaven; he remembered thinking, that must be her soul they're watching, she must be in Heaven now.

But beside that, beside the golden light and the idea that everyone has one (except for Sammy now apparently, or anyone somehow trade it to angels, demons, or anyone who'd trade for it), Dean doesn't have much idea what soul's like. It's not like you can hear it beating or feel it burning, right? Not when you are human, at least. But apparently angels can touch it. So there's someone who'd know the answer to his question.

"Can I ask you something?" Dean stops Cas one day, when they are just about to step into the car together. Dean doesn't even realize he's thinking about the question again, not until the question just burst out of his tongue and pop out in front of him like a clown pop out of the box, surprising him.

But Cas isn't surprised. "Of course, Dean. Anything."

"What's soul like?"

Cas meets his eyes and then gazes down, staring at his chest. There is a small frown between  his eyebrows, but soon it's smoothed. And it becomes a small smile at the tip of his lip. So small that it's almost unnoticed, but Dean's seen his poker face long enough to know this isn't one of his expressionless face. Cas looks like he's using his eyes to search where his soul is, Dean thinks, suddenly feeling uneasy. What would Cas see? The battered, broken thing from Hell? The ugly, bloody thing that he shouldn't forget and certainly shouldn't follow around, let alone calling a friend?

"Actually, you know what, never mind. Don't answer that, I don't know what got into me. Forget about that. Get into the car, Cas," Dean murmurs, scratching the back of his neck, turning his eyes away, ready to walk to the other side of the car, hiding himself from the driver side of the seats.

"Bright." Cas starts saying, slowly, carefully, as if handling something delicate, something like a piece of crystal art from an ancient museum. "Warm -- some of them burn so hot it can scorch me. Think of sun, the soul has as much heat and color and power as the sun, but in the ethereal plane. It's the most precious thing a human has -- it has all that makes the human the way they are. All the morality and consciousness. Most of the memories that made the human since they're born. A soulless human may still have the concept of knowledge to be intelligent and functional, but without those emotion and intimate memories, it's hard to say they're still them anymore." Cas pauses briefly, then slowly adds, "In a way, that's not too different from angels, I guess. Especially when I first came to Earth, we -- I -- was exactly like that. You may call me soulless as well, for angels having no souls, not like humans." His eyes drop. "Not like you." He finishes, voice low.

"Hey, wait, that's not like a criticize or anything, Cas." Dean stops on his way to the driver side, turning back to Cas, patting his shoulder. "You guys have something way cooler. We don't have grace or mojo or any of those friggin' awesome light show you get going in your eyes when you power up, right? You don't get to be upset about not having soul or some shit like humans." He chuckles quietly, can't even imagine why on earth would an *angel* think a soul would be anything worth mourning about not having.

"No, Dean, it's not *any* soul that I was talking about." Cas looks up again, meeting  Dean's eyes, dead serious. "Just yours. Not everyone's soul can scotch my hand, only yours when I touched you. Not everyone is as bright and beautiful as yours. Why do  you think I get so attached by you?"

It's Dean who has to look away now. His hand drops from Cas's shoulder, absent-mindedly touching his lift shoulder, where the burned handprint still can be felt under the flannel. He forces another chuckle, feeling his throat dry and his cheek burning now. Damn, when's the last time he blushed? He can't believe it's Cas's words doing it to him now. "I'd guess that's because of my pretty face," he jokes, fingers twitching, suddenly feeling his own skin is too tight for whatever that is under the skin -- for his soul, perhaps.

Cas gives him a stern look. "You joke when you're uncomfortable, Dean. I apologize if you are unused to hearing compliment, though admittedly your physical appearance is indeed appealing to look at, trust me, it's your soul that truly caught my eyes. That was also how I managed to locate you in Hell during the rescue. I wouldn't have been able to find you so quickly if it's not for the brightness of your soul. So no need to doubt it."

"Why didn't other angels see me before you did?" Dean can't help wonder aloud, a question on his mind since the day he got out and learned about angels -- Cas -- sieged the Hell and saved him.

"They didn't look hard enough. They weren't even really trying to find you or save you, I think." Cas is staring at Dean's chest again, where his heart is beating fast. Dean notices his fingers are clenching and unfolding, until he seems to be unable to stop himself and Cas puts his palm over Dean's heart. He speaks again, his voice pained. "They received the command to let you break, let you suffer and let the Apocalypse begin."

Dean can't breathe. He catches Cas's wrist. "And you?"

"I received the same order," Cas whispers, looking up. Dean sees light and tear swirling in Cas's eyes together. "We were in Hell, but we weren't allowed to save you. We were just sent there, supposed to pretend we tried and failed, just so that all the great plan could follow through. I was meant to die on that mission, getting killed by demons or burned by Hell fire like so many others; and you were meant to rot in Hell and never get back -- until Michael takes your vessel. There would be no you, and certainly no me... Not according to the plan, anyway."

"But you broke the order." Dean grasps his wrist so tight, and Cas's palm is warm against his chest, heat seeping through the fabric, becoming the only source of comfort. Dean holds onto that like the source of life.

Cas nods. "I broke the order. That's the first order I broke for you. None of the angels were pleased when I announced that 'Dean Winchester is saved'. Heaven was in such great chaos and there were so many emergency discussion in the days after your save about what to do with you and what to do with me. I was fully expecting them to smite me for my rebellion, but at least I wanted to do at least one last thing before I die, so I spent all those days putting you back together, patching your body, fixing the wounds on your soul. I still couldn't fix everything -- you see, soul is such a delicate thing, I wanted to remove your memory of Hell but I don't want to take away anything you might want to keep, so at the end, I made you  the best I could -- the way you are, the way you've always been. There is not a single thing I'd want to change about you, Dean."

"You are saying I'm perfect?" Dean tries to smirk, like the way the old, young him would flirt to a bar girl, but now he feels there is almost a tear at the corner of his eyes. It's all he can do to keep the tear from falling.

"I'm saying you're perfect as you are, Dean." Cas smiles at him, gentle, eyes bright.

Dean wipes the corner of his eyes with the heel of his hand. "Jesus." Dean sighs out, taking another difficult breath in. His throat still feels clogged. "Why didn't you ever tell me?"

"What's there to tell? That I wished things were different? That I regretted everyday that I didn't reach you sooner and save you earlier? That I wished to go back in time and save you from all the pain and --"

"No, no, Cas, stop. Not that. Jesus." Dean wipes his eyes again. "I mean, you saved me, with all the odds and so many dicks trying to kill me off and throw you into the trench. You are the only reason I get to still stand here and talk to you and breathe. I... I never even said 'Thank you'. Not even when we first met."

"You did stab me in the chest," Cas says seriously.

"Yeah, Winchester way of saying 'Thank you'." Dean chuckles. "But no, seriously, I meant it. Thank you, Cas. For everything."

"It is my greatest honor and pleasure to save you, Dean Winchester," Cas answers formally.

Dean smiles. He pats Cas on the shoulder. "Alright, time up for the chick-flick moment. Get into the car, we still have a case to work on and people to save."

"Of course, Dean."
