Love Bobby's journal so much more than John's journal, can't help comparing them all the time during the read, so much similarities yet so different -- by different I mean Bobby is so, so much better. Carried the book around the house and finished it in less a week, just says how much I love the book and Bobby.

Bobby really is trying to teach you how to hunt, how to interview the victims and survivors, what signs to watch out for, what's the meaning of them, what patterns from what monsters, how to handle them, how to figure it out based on what you can gather from the crime scene, almost like a criminal-solving class. No wonder it really is a "guide to hunting". The case fic is real and detailed and actually helpful if the reader really is an aspiring hunter-in-training. Whereas John's journal is mostly just his own thoughts and feelings and all about his dead wife. There are so hints and lessons, here and there, standalone dry information copies without much explanation or summary or lessons or anything to make anyone who read his journal in the future any easier. John's journal is for himself, Bobby's journal is for everyone else.

Bobby's journal actually has a plot, a storyline, from the first case in the beginning that starts the whole amnesia setting, till the end that wrapped the case and get everything fixed. Completed by Dean's writing in the last two chapters. The chapters in the middle are also sorta logically have their reasons of the ordering. Though the amnesia definitely makes it very convenient to jump around and be chaotic when the writer might just want to insert something or break the narrative and restart.

The foreshadowing and catching the buried hints is done really brilliantly. Especially the part about that whole chapter of confusing names, Bobby figured out after the chapter but doesn't tell us, but Dean does in the end. The revelation of those names being the kid that Bobby almost had but lost is just so incredibly sad and deep.

Learned so much more about Bobby (similar to reading John's journal to learn about John, reading Jo's blog to learn about Jo, just love those first person view writings), things I'd never thought about. Like how did Bobby get so fluent in Japanese, when did he go to Japan, how did he know Rufus, what happened in Omaha (something that's mentioned so many times but never any answer in canon), and now there's all the answers. It makes so much sense that's what Bobby went first thing after the tragedy with Karen. It's so, so important to anyone who experienced supernatural tragedy who's their guide into that horrible, scary world, the truth with all the monsters. John doesn't have anyone and he's so scared, he had to figure everything out by himself the nasty way, and according to his journal, also by the misguide and threat of so many demons around him, some even posed as friends, no wonder he'd become the way he is in the later half of his life (meaning how he turns from Matt Cohen version to JDM version). And Bobby has Rufus to give him the very initial firsthand info download -- which, admittedly still spooked him bad, but at least he gets some real honest answer to the scariest question, he's not misguided, he's just scared, and once he finally accept it, he comes right back to Rufus and become the best and decent hunter he is.

Thinking about it, talking about trauma, Bobby actually has it even worse than John. John has to watch his beloved wife burning by a demon without knowing anything about supernatural; Bobby has to *kill* his beloved wife possessed by a demon without knowing anything about supernatural. John doesn't have a father figure growing up and always think his father abandoned him (while his father actually loves him); Bobby doesn't have a good father figure growing up, instead, he has an abusive one that he ends up killing with his own hand to protect himself and his mom. Perhaps the only worst thing John has and Bobby doesn't is John's time in Vietnam. Even career-wise they're similar enough, John is a mechanic and co-owns a garage to fix cars, Bobby is a mechanic and buys a scrape yard to fix cars. Have to say, no wonder canonically there would be some version of Mary fall for some version of Bobby.

But Bobby does it right with the boys, at least he understands (after some heavy lessons) that "if you have kids, you retire." But also, to be fair, thinking it through for John (especially right now I got a fic to work on that just force me to think in his shows and take everything into consideration), it's so, so much more difficult to make the right call as a father than as a surrogate father (or uncle). He has much more things to consider than making the boys happy, because he has to be in the survival mode all this time, he is way more concerned about their safety than their happiness or well-being -- there is no well-being if they're dead. We could blame John all we want for giving them suffering childhood and all the baggage he caused them, but the fact that they even lived to be adults to *have* those baggage already means John succeeded in what he's trying to do. He'd know Sammy's demon blood at some point, he'd know something about demons after Sammy at some point, so it's not about avenging Mary's death since a long time, it's about figuring out how to save Sammy. And worst of all, he can't even tell anyone. Not even Bobby -- I wonder if Bobby ever knows about Sam's demon blood. So yeah, it's so much easier for outsiders (including Bobby) to seem to be a better father (I'm sure he still would be if he's the biological father), but for John, he already does his best.

Almost always forget about Bobby's  social connection. Being too focused on the boys and their losses, so rare to have anyone talking about Bobby's losses over the years in the series. Losing Ellen and Jo must be painful for Bobby, especially from S06 Titanic episode we just learned what could have been between Ellen and Bobby. From the canon S05 we only get a glimpse to see Bobby burns the photo and stares at the fire for a long time. Anyone asking about Bobby's grief?

And then, still the S06 Titanic episode, the whole reason we'd see that one where Bobby needs comfort from Ellen, is because he just lost Rufus. In the real world that's not timeline-altered, he's in so much grief that I think it's the first time we really see Bobby drink so much that he's passed out/blacked out/slept so deep that he doesn't notice others moving in his house. Rufus is his partner, the teacher who taught him everything about hunting when he just started. Bobby/Rufus have always been a jokingly parallel comparison to Sam/Dean. Rufus to Bobby is almost like Sam to Dean (or Dean to Sam). They quarrel like siblings, they work together like the best hunting partners, then they part their ways because of Bobby's mistake and Rufus has never forgiven him until he dies -- and it's Bobby who kills him, just like Bobby killed Karen. And we don't even have time to think about Bobby's grief, because we were too focused on the boys and their actions and their world-saving plans. While Bobby is alone, thinking over all those years he spent with Rufus, all the rights and wrongs, how many times Rufus must have covered his ass or he'd be long dead, but now he's still alive but Rufus isn't. He has to drink all the worst strongest alcohol to numb his thought or he'd kill himself. Rufus still couldn't forgive him till the end, and Bobby still believes and agrees that's his fault. So much pain, and Bobby doesn't even have anyone to tell. If it's pain from anywhere else, he'd usually tell Rufus, but now it's because of Rufus. So he just drinks and drinks until he passes out. The boys are there at the end taking care of him, covering him with blanket, but they're really *there* to share any of the pain and burden and grief. Bobby is the only one living to grieve Rufus's death the way he deserves, because he's the only one knowing him so well. Now he wonders when he dies, who'd be the one remembering everything he did -- and he'd realize there's no one. The boys would be the closest, but even they don't know him as well as Rufus, because Rufus's there with him since the beginning, knowing what's he like when he's young, at his thirties, when he's new in hunting, and in the boys' memory, he's already the old grumpy hunter always wearing a baseball hat and buried his nose in endless books and lore, the friendly uncle they can go playing catch with in a warm afternoon, but not what he's like when he's still young. He realizes no one will remember that side of him anymore. So he continues drinking. As long as the boys remember *some* part of him, that will have to do. That's all he can have, so he'll take just that, and cherish exactly that.

Love so much the relationship Bobby shows in his writing with the boys. The jokes he write about them, the unconsciously funny snarky comments about them, the familiar Bobby-way of talking and joking. Like how "if the monster wants the tall guy, you call Sam; if thee monster wants pretty girl, you call Dean" turns out making me laugh so hard. Bobby loves them both so much, it's heartwarming. It's so funny and enjoyable to read, too. Just more reasons to love Bobby.

I think, if there's one thing I can take away from reading this, I'd definitely add more of Rufus whenever I have to write Bobby. Just like how much Mary is a part of John, Cas is a part of Dean, Rufus is a part of Bobby, if I ever want to get Bobby right, I'll have to remember Rufus's position in his mind, and go from there. (And admittedly, the destiel fic I just finished reading, *Let it Be*, has quite a lot of plot with Rufus and it's getting him so good and so right, and the writer's note also explained how much the writer loves Rufus, that's a page I can also learn from.)

Enough said, just, love this book so very much and love Bobby so very much. Got the feeling I'd re-read this book many more times, either to get more monster and hunting ideas and references (which, I already got plenty and ready to insert some of them right into my next fic), or to remember more about Bobby's past, learn how to speak and think in Bobby's POV. It's the perfect material to learn how to write Bobby, and I'd dissect it and savor it so badly. Love it so much, that's all I can say.
