It's just a normal laundry day. Dean wasn't thinking much when he scans his room for dirty clothes and spots Cas's trench coat hanging in his closet. Cas is in Dean's T-shirt (the one with ACDC symbol in the middle) and a pair of soft wore-in sweatpants today, sitting on their bed reading a book. Dean realizes he can't quite remember when's the last he saw Cas in trench coat -- before the last end-of-the-world event, probably, and that's been quite some time now.

He walks toward the closet while asking mindlessly, "When's the last time you wash that?"

He's already standing before it and checking the pockets when Cas looks up, considering, "Never."

Dean nods, "About time to give it a wash then." He takes it off the hanger. "You got anything in the pockets you want to take out?" There's nothing in the coat pockets he just checked, but then he remembers something, and he goes on checking the inside of the sleeves.

"Dean, there is no pocket in the sleeves." Cas tilts his head a little, watching him, and then he realizes. "Angel blade is never *in* the sleeves. I have to manifest it, it's in the same plane where my wings are."

Dean frowns at the empty sleeve, his move slowing down. "Then why did you bring that out that time," he swallows, wanting to look up at Cas but can't quite bring himself to, he settles with still staring at the coat in his hands, "... that time, you know, when I was bad, I coulda kill you. I almost did." His throat dries fast.

"You didn't, and that's the point." Cas's voice is still warm and calm, but he puts his book down now, Dean sees it from the peripheral of his eyes. "Besides, I don't really have full control when my survival instinct tries to protect me. All my training and reflection tells me to fight back... to fight you..."

"But you couldn't." Dean's voice cracks, his hand droops. He can't help looking up now, looking at Cas. Desperate, as if he's thrown back to that bad night again. Memory starts flooding in, drowning him.

Cas slides down the bed, walking toward him in quick strides, moving closer until he's right before him. His eyes warm and careful. Blue. "I won't hurt you," Cas says simply.

"Even if it means I might kill you." Dean feels his chest tight and pain, he can't breathe.

"You wouldn't," Cas tells him, more certain than he ever feels in himself.

"You don't know that." His eyes sting. And he thinks that's the truth, he didn't even know it himself back then. He almost... he almost... oh God, what if he did? What would happen to him then? He squeezes his eyes, there's wetness gathering before his retina.

"I know, Dean. I know you were in there." Cas takes another step closer to him. Dean can feel the warmth of his body in this distance, and it calms him down -- even if just a little bit.

Dean chuckles humorlessly. "One hell of Deja Vu there." Deja Vu when their role were reversed, when Dean said the exact same things to Cas when the angel was the one who weren't himself. Why do they keep having those bad time between them? When do they had to keep hurting each other all those past years?

"Even if you did, I wouldn't regret it." Cas's hands lands on his shoulders, tugging him in gently. "There is no other way I'd rather die than in your hands."

"Don't say that," Dean croaks, can barely breathe.

Cas just smiles at him quietly, maybe a little sadly. *But that's the truth.* His eyes say.

Dean clings on to him like his life depends on it. He finds his hands fist on Cas's shirt. "What would I do if I did?" His voice breaks to pieces. "It woulda destroy me. I woulda kill myself, even that wouldn't be enough for what I did to you."

"I never blamed you," Cas tries to soothe him, his hands petting Dean like he's a startled horse, but it isn't working.

"That's not the point." Dean thinks he's at the brim of crying but there is no tears, just his eyes sting bitterly. He wants to yell at Cas, shake him, wake him up so he can see him for who he is, the bad and dangerous person that he is, push him away so he can't possibly hurt him again. But his traitorous hands only grabs Cas at his collar harder, pulling him closer.

"The point is..." He blinks hard. Cas looks at him in his eyes, attentive, patient, quiet, like he always is. There's the ghost of a frown and appreciation in his face at the same time between his brows. He looks at Dean like he's the most beautiful person in the world. He probably is thinking that -- he's definitely thinking that from the way his head tilt a small degree. Dean closes his eyes. He can't speak if he keeps looking into those eyes that's been watching him the same way for the past twelve years without him noticing a thing, the same eyes looking up at him when he pushed him to the table ready to kill him with that killer instinct tickling on his arm where the Mark of Cain was and the rage boiling in his vein. He can't.

"The point is I'm not a good person. I'm not good, I'm... I'm not good enough for you," Dean whispers, barely audible.

He feels the hands on his shoulder grips tighter. He's forced to open his eyes and meets Cas's again.

Cas looks at him like he's offended, like he can't allow anyone telling him anything bad about Dean, including Dean himself. "You see yourself the way our enemies see you, Dean."

Dean breathes hard, he knows exactly what's coming next, because he has heard those exact same words once before. He has said those exact same words before. In that most dreadful night that's forever his worst nightmare. He closes his eyes again, if he doesn't see it, then it won't happen again, right? He's shaking without knowing, only to be steadied by Cas's hands on his shoulder. Those hands slide down along his arms, holding him like a brace. This is different from the memory. So this must not be the memory.

"You're the most loving man in the world I will ever know," Cas tells him steadily, keep going.

"Don't," Dean whispers. Begging. For what, he doesn't know. *Don't say the next thing, don't leave me again, don't let me hurt you again.*

Cas doesn't stop. There is a moment of silence, filled by the sound of breathing. Then Dean feels the feathery light touch of a kiss on his forehead. "I love you, Dean."

Dean's breath hitches.

"I love you for how much you care about the world."

A kiss on the bridge of his nose.

"I love you for you always trying to do the right thing, no matter how hard they are."

A kiss on the left cheek.

"I love you for loving me back even though I didn't know before, I know now. And you don't have to say it. And thank you for letting me love you."

A kiss on the right cheek.

"I love for exactly who you are, Dean. No more, no less."

The kiss lands on his lips. Dean whimpers at the gentlest touch. It only last as long as a feather brushes his skin and it flies away, leaving the lingering phantom memory of its touch.

Cas presses his forehead against Dean's, letting out a long, slow breath. "I know you wouldn't hurt me, I just know it, Dean. Even when you couldn't believe it yourself. I know you'd never hurt me, not intentionally. That's why I have my blade manifested but I'd never use it."

"God," Dean whispers in a shallow breath, he doesn't know how he's still breathing. "I love you so much you have no idea. And I had no idea."

He shakes his head, correcting himself, "Except I did, and that must be what made me drop the blade. You have no idea how strong that Mark of Cain was, I was drowning, it was like the worst kind of drunkenness, my brain phasing in and out, I was so angry, my whole view is bloody red. But then I saw you. I saw you. You were saying my name, you touched my hand, you just looked at me. And I made myself stop. I had to. I... I can't lose you. Even the Mark couldn't do anything to change that." Dean lets out another shaky breath. "I didn't know that's love at the time. I... I didn't know what's love like because no one ever told me, and I never feel like that before with anyone else. I'm so sorry." Tears fall from the corner of his eyes.

"I love you, too," Cas whispers, their breath entangles, their lips inches away. "And never apologize for love me."

Their lips press together, neither knows who started. And Dean can taste his own tears on the tip of his tongue. It's salty.

He wonders if that's how Cas's tears taste like, in that night, when he dropped tears for the first time in his millennium of life, for him. His heart wrenches at the thought.

But then Cas kisses him harder, his hands holding him tighter. And Dean stops wondering. He was drowning, but Cas knows how to swim for both of them. So now he's floating.

He kisses back just as hard, holding him just as tight and close. They'll do the laundry later.
