You're about to leave, again. From the way you checking your phone and glancing the clock, I can tell. There's someone waiting for you at home, someone nice, someone belong to you and you belong to. Someone that isn't me. But I'm okay with it.

I know we're friends. Just friends. But I'm already feeling so lucky. Lucky to know you at all, lucky to have you around me, once in a long while, but still, in those rare occasions, I'd be able to see you for real. So so very lucky that, I think -- I know -- you care for me. When I need your help, when I *really* need you around, you'd come to me. Like now.

The case I asked for your help was probably an easy one for you, and maybe, if I looked into it a little longer, dug a little deeper, prepared a little better, I could have handled it myself too. But I didn't want to do it alone -- or rather, my intuition told me not to, and I finally learned to trust my gut and called you. That's what you told me, too, in that very first case we ran into each other, that hunters should never work alone, they should always have a backup, a hunting buddy, a partner, someone to watch your back, someone you can trust. But I didn't have a partner -- I didn't want to, either, not for hunting, not for life. I wasn't ready to have one again -- I still am not. The pain and hurt from that last one was still too deep to handle, my brain tried so hard to block those images and sound from the memory, but I could still see it, could still hear it when I close my eyes, in the quiet of the night. It still made me nauseous. There were so much mess there. I never told anyone, not really.

"I couldn't," I told you in a whisper, I remember. I could sense myself recoil, from the thought, from the memory, from even remotely thinking about opening up and touching the pained memory, or to think about the possibility of going through that again with someone, anyone, and potentially experiencing that one more time. I could sense my body tense up, in the same way that in the second before a car crash that you saw coming but couldn't do anything to stop, bracing for the impact, for the pain, for the disaster.

I couldn't trust anyone else again after what had happened back then, couldn't work with anyone, couldn't let anyone in. I couldn't even bring myself to tell another soul about those things, what happened back then. Everything was too, too much. Knowing those therapists would ask questions, thinking that I might have to tell someone about those things was already enough to make me feel sick.

But you are the exception. Somehow, in this whole world, I think you might be the only one I could do that. You would hear me, and you would understand. I don't know why; I just do.

"Just for hunting?" I already knew the answer before asking. I didn't know why I had to ask again.

"For life, too."

And that was all I needed to know.

"Okay." You patted my arm and gave me another hug, like the seal of a deal -- the good kind, not the crossroad ones. I imagined sinking into the enclosure of a sea again, except the water embracing me was warm, and I could see the sunshine and beach.

I watched you head back into your motel room, and had to drag myself away from that parking lot.

That was two years ago.
