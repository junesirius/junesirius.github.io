Suddenly starts understanding why Eleven needs the blinder and headphone (or white noise) or sinking underworld to go into her head -- I need that to go into my head too.

The voices of the world is too much, too loud, everywhere. The only way I can focus on writing or doing anything really that needs my attention, the very only thing, is to use my brilliant amazing perfect ear muff that kills 30db of any sound, which means basically any sound in the adjacent area, the buzzing of the electricity line, the lamp, the fridge, I'm not even mentioning A/C or fans because that's an absolute no for the already very scattered brain attention. The absolute quietness, the beautiful silence, the brain that doesn't have to be forced into processing any additional unintended sound info from the outside world. How do normal people live in this world without audio overload? There're so many sound, sometimes even the silence is too loud, is that just me or the world *is* being too loud all the time?

That *was* good enough for a while, until, gradually, just blocking out the sound processing procedures isn't enough, there's still the scent and taste and visual input that distract my brain, fighting for the pitifully limited attention it can offer. Used to be able to write with just the ear muff, but now, even that isn't good enough. I need the blinder to block out any visual feedbacks I'm using. I need to write, but I can only write when words and images and sounds can play in my head, and I can only do that when I don't have to process words and images and sounds *outside* of my head.

So this very recent gift that I just got myself, the perfectly amazing eye blinder (or the eyeshade, I don't know what's the proper name to call it), blocking out any visuals, leaving me only the blessed blackness. Leaving the blackness colorful and lively before my eyes. I can only see the things I need to see, when I don't have to see the things I'm forced to see, namely, every single bloody thing in this world. Too much light, always too much, too much movement, too much still things, just too many things, too many feedback, the eyes are always looking, always absorbing, always processing, always analyzing. Taking too much extra power for those background processes, getting tired too easily. How did other people handle that? All those information input from the external world, how could anyone ever really process every visual, every sound, every scent, every taste, every touch, every day, every minute, every second, and still have enough brain power to think, to go deeper, to come up with a new idea, then to create, to write, to generate new things that didn't exist before in this world, that no one has ever seen or created the exact same thing ever before. How do people do that? Or they also don't do it, but they don't feel it's wrong?

So at least for now, I solved the partial problems of the sensory overload, the ear muff fixing the audio overload, the eyeshade fixing the visual overload, I already have the citrus scent diffuser to control the scent in the house (which is probably the only sense that I notice know how to auto block or cache the already processed input and skip them to save the power, the familiar scent get dulled and muted until I can't smell anything; all the other senses just kept processing the same input over and over again until the processing power gets drained. I'm sure they already have some little amount of caching implemented, otherwise it would be even worse, but still, not good enough for the brain power), I wonder for how long I'd notice the remaining senses are also becoming a problem and need blocking. I only get taste and touch left, and if I'm not eating, the taste is the easy one blocking out, but what about touch? How do I stop all my skins from feeling everything, the way my fingers touch the keyboard, my wrists on the pads, my elbow on the desk, the clothing on my body, my pants around my thigh, the way the chair sink under my butt, my feet and toes in my slippers... It's so funny, most of the therapy method would teach you to focus on your senses to help you calm down, to be able to focus, they'd do the five-four-three-two-one practice for grounding (which is proved to be useful, for anxiety and depression), and the breathing practice. But it's not the solution here, it's turning into the problem here. I'm not *not* noticing where I am, the surroundings, I'm noticing too much, I'm noticing everything. I wonder how's it feel like sinking under the salty water like Eleven did, I wonder if that blackness and silence from water and the consistent coldness around the body and plain dull scent and taste, is as perfect and static as they showed; I wonder if that is the ultimate solution for my sensory overload problem. I'd like to try it some day -- or, actually, didn't I already? It's exactly what feel like when I was in the swimming pool in Orlando, when I just let myself float, or sink, eyes clothed, breathing closed, reduce any control over my limbs, let the water take me. And there I was, blackness before my eyes, my ears can only hear the sound of water, my body the temperature and touch of the water, and that's it. The world is so perfect, perfectly still, perfectly black, calm and peaceful. Isn't it just so nice?

I've been thinking, maybe it's a sign that some ADHD symptoms are getting worse, my attention getting scattered worse and worse everyday, so it takes more and more effort to bring them back together, like angels scooping the last remnant of their remaining grace, to do one last miracle, and that's me gathering around what little attention I can gather, and use them to read, to write, to create stories, to absorb information -- to take in, to get out. That's exactly what I figured the life's meaning is, isn't it, that sudden realization of life's meaning ten years ago -- to take in as much as possible, to get out as much as possible. But I just wouldn't have thought, "as much as possible" isn't too much, and the amount would reduce rapidly as you age. I can only take in as little as I can, so that I can get out as much as I can. They conflict, sometimes. But hey, if it works, it works.

(PS: But sadly, this pure black, pure silent environment, can only ever work on English writing (or rather, direct keyboard alphabetic-based language typing; Spanish included, for future use), but good thing is, that's the only way I'm writing right now anyway.)
