It's been four months since I lost you, five, if counting from the beginning. I couldn't write anything for you. This file was created at 23:37, 05/28/2026, the day and the time that I thought everything was over and I could finally, properly grieve you. But I couldn't. I still don't know if I could now, but I'm trying. There are so many things to grieve, you just happen to be in the middle of it, becoming the anchor of my grief, and I appreciate the world and life and destiny and fate for that. But there are still so many things around it, outside you, that takes away my attention, making it not all about you, and I'm so, so sorry about it. But there is no way I can grieve properly without also grieving those things, so, forgive me for that. I know you'll understand.

It's still so difficult to think, to feel, to recall what happened then and what's still happening now. I've been trying so hard not to feel, because if I do, it hurts too damn much. But I know it's not good for me. I can't suppress it forever. So I'm trying. I'm trying so hard in the different direction now, allowing myself to feel, not afraid of feeling too much, hurting too much. It's still scary, I'm doing it slowly. I'm hoping it will do me some good. I'm hoping.

I think, part of the reason why I didn't want to write this, this grieving letter, this obituary, this tribute, or whatever you call it, is because I don't want this to end. It's as if, if I write it, then by the time I finish writing, this is it. This would be the final thing that confirms that you're gone, and I'm wrapping up everything around you, and putting you away in my memory. As if, it's only this, the one last thing I have to check on my very long list of things I needed to do after the accident, that this, out of everything, is the closure, the final end of the story of you. It does feel like that, a closure, which is why I've been putting it off, for so long. I don't want the final ending of the story, I don't want to think this is it, for you, even though everything else about you has already been done, four months ago. As if as long as I don't write this final mourning article, then there's still a thread of hope there, a sense of it's not fully done yet, a sense that, if there is something I haven't done yet with you, then you're still there, taking up a corner of my mind, reminding me you're still there. Now I understand, it's the first phase of the grief, the denial stage. I haven't really gone through a proper five-stage grief before, but with you, I'm experiencing it. I didn't know that's what I've been doing, but I don't need to know what I'm doing to do it. I don't want to think of you as gone, even though you really are, physically, subjectively, matter-of-factly, gone.

It still hurts so much to think that, to type that last sentence. And I'm taking a moment to settle myself.

Over and over again, I've been retracing the things that happened. People always say that the point of experiencing the life is to leave memories for future to look back on, but what happens to those who blocks out the past, to just stop remembering? What happen to those who don't have the past to remember, who only live in the current moment, how to live the life then? That's a whole different long thing to write about, I'll probably leave it to another day, or later.

For now, for you, so, here's the timeline that I don't want to forget. Or when I forget, I can still check back to find. One that I want to do your justice to record, and it doesn't matter how hard it is on me (wanted to write "how hard it was", but it still is, even now). The last day I've ever heard their voices, the last day we still kept the facade that things can still be normal, we can still work it out. The last day, before it exploded. The day started as if I already knew how it would went. You saved me, and I never said "Thank you", not in the way that mattered. Never can again. What am I supposed to do after knowing that?

The event itself happened quickly. The accident on 05/02, at 14:12, six minutes away from the Foodieland that I was trying to get to. On the highway I-5 south, at the exit ramp. The second before it happened, I already knew it's going to be bad. The rush of traffic, the long line on the ramp, I should have been more careful, but I was also reckless. So what, that if I die? So what, if bad things happen to me? It's not like anyone would really care, right? I didn't slow down like I usually would. But I also had the disguise of being in a rush. Maybe it's just all the reasons altogether, I'd never be able to figure out which one caused which. The fate is just a bitch. Things just happen. Maybe there was a reason, maybe there wasn't. My mind wasn't in the right place, my mental health wasn't the best, the timing wasn't the best, the traffic wasn't the best, the road wasn't the best, the driver behind me wasn't the best. I slammed the brake when the car before me slammed it. I pride myself for my quick reaction and that I was lucky to have enough distance before to make my no more financial loss. But the car behind me became the real victim of the my messed-up mind and life. There was the impact of the hit, and that was how everything began and ended.

It wasn't really a surprise, honestly. The second before that when I slammed the brake, I think I was already bracing myself for it. Anyone who wasn't as quick-reaction as me would suffer from it. Anyone who's too close to my tail would definitely suffer from it. And he really did. And praise the law, he was the one to be blamed, and everything would be his fault, even though I know the real dark truth that no one was looking for. Like I always already knew, the best way to hide a suicidal attempt, is to disguise it as an accident. And here is the proof. Not that it really was planned out, but it was what I, at least the subconsciousness inside me, was looking for.

I was still shocked. And panicked. And my mind blanked out. I was told I was pale. I know I was shaking. The car was screwed. But the immediate two things I noticed was: my phone and water bottle flew to the right side, which means I got hit from the left; and my window wiper started wiping, but there is no rain, which means something went wrong with my Baby but I don't know what. There were also loud beeping on the dashboard, all kinds of different symbols started flashing there, red, orange, things with exclamation marks, symbols my brain couldn't process at that moment. I sit there, the safety belt hugged me, my hands trembled, and my mind supplied slowly: "Shit."

There are horns and beeping everywhere, outside me, outside the closed window, out in the world. Too much sound. Sounds so distant, so irrelevant. I stopped caring about anything outside me. I'm inside my Baby, that means I'm inside the little bubble of my world. Safe. Or as safe as I can get at this point. I reached to my phone and texted the same five sentences to two friends who were so so fortunately both only five minutes away from me, because, of course I picked the best day to have an accident, when I had both my closest friends as close to me as possible, both could reach to me within ten minutes, or just how the fate plays out for me, the best thing on the worst day. I typed to both of them: "Fuck", "Car crash", "Got hit", "Not hurt", "But scared". And I sit there, mind blank, hands trembling, staring outside the window. The window wiper was still wiping frantically on nothing.

A woman knocked on my window. I lowered it. She was yelling at someone I couldn't see. I think she was yelling at the driver that hit me. She yelled at that someone loudly, "You're not the most important thing here!" And she turned to me. My mind worked slowly, *Does that mean she thinks I'm the most important thing here?* I was still so dumbfounded that I couldn't find my voice, doesn't know what to do. Adrenaline pumping through my veins, heart beat fast and loud, that's all I can feel.

She looked at me, concerned. "Are you hurt?" I shook my head, still can't speak. I think my face must be white. And I think I got the fact wrong for a little bit here. She parked next to me first, from inside her car, gesturing me to lower my window and shouted from there, asking me if I was hurt. I would so very much like to appreciate her but I would never be able to find her ever again, just the kindness of her left with me in my most desperate, scared moment.

Coming back to the events that happened. The kind stranger parked next to me, yelled at someone else, then approached my window. She saw the frantic window wiper that was just adding to my stress and scare. I think she asked if I could turn it off. And my blanked-out brain didn't remember how to. "I think it's broken," I told her. At least that's what I remembered saying. I forgot how to turn off the window wiper. She reached over and turned it off for me. Just a twist on the handle, the standard way. It was enabled because of the impact, the hit bumped into the handle and turned it on, so all you need to do is to pull it back to turn it off. And the window wiper settled down. It seems so easy. I was so relieved. So it wasn't broken. It seems so easy to have solved one of the problems. The smallest problem out of every fucking thing. I was so appreciated.

She told me to slide slowly to the shoulder of the road. The ramp was tiny, already barely enough for a car to pass, but now I'd have to move to the side of the road. I could do that. Being told what to do and then do it, I could do it. So I did it. There were still so many things I should have done. Take down the contact information of the other driver, for example. I didn't know if they'd flee. Should have taken down their license plate, for another. I didn't even know how bad the back of the Baby look like now, and I couldn't find the strength to get out of the car. I continue to sit there, dumbfounded, frozen. You wouldn't be mine, anymore. You would be gone. Really gone. And I couldn't do anything, knowing this is the day. 05/02 was the day you were hurt, and this, 05/18, was day I finally, really lost you.

I woke myself up in the ass early hour of the day, called in for a day off, because I knew I wouldn't be able to do anything for the whole day. People took days off for losing their loved ones, and I took the day off for you, because I didn't really have many important things and beings in my life, and you were one of them. At least, you deserved this day for me to grieve you.

I didn't even know if you'd still be there before you got there. They told me this was the day, but they didn't tell me when. So I figured, maybe if I went there the first thing after they open up, maybe they wouldn't have enough time to take you away, maybe, even though I had said my "goodbye", I could say that again, because there were a few other things I forgot to say to you that I'd like you to hear.

So I went. And you were still there. Not too different from the last time I saw you, three days ago. I forced myself to not look at how battered and tired you were, how dying you looked like. But you were not gone, not yet. I could feel you were breathing, barely, but still. I put my palm on the side of the driver's seat, the place I imagine where your heart would be, feel you under my hand, touch you, connect to you. I think you could hear me better this way. That's the equivalent of holding your cheek for a loved one, in my head. I can feel my tears pouring down again and I didn't even try to hide it. I had more things I needed to tell you that I realized I forgot when I drove away three days ago, so I was back to tell you these: "Hello", "Thank you", and "I love you". I never told you those, didn't I? I had said "I'm sorry", because that's the only thing I could think of seeing you look like that. But I forgot to tell you "Thank you". You gave your life for me, how could I forget to say "Thank you"? And I love you, of course I love you, I named you after Dean's Impala, you knew how much that meant to me, the show, the story, the characters, Dean, and how much Baby (Impala) meant for Dean, then you must know that's my way of saying how much you meant for me. Impala was his home when he doesn't have one, and you are mine. Did I ever tell you how much you feel likboe home to me? How much sense of safety you've been giving me? When I went outside, there was always this sense of dread, being outside, being alone, being unprepared for the outside. That's why I always try to overpack my bag, as if I had enough things with me, back up battery of the back up battery, chargers, back up water bottle of the water bottle, food, back up food of the food, pens, back up pens, paper, umbrella, plastic bags, paper tissues... I've never really need half of these things, but I'd always take everything with me, no matter how heavy the backpack turning into, just to have a little sense of security, as if I could be less dreadful for being out there. But then, I had you. If I took you with me when I went outside, it's still so easy to get tired and worried and stressed when I was outside of you. But as soon as I get back inside you, I feel safe, I feel relaxed, I feel at home. Even just as simple as going to work, after a long day, going out of the office building and sitting inside you, that was the moment of relax, like I was home already, like I was safe, like I could finally breathe, like I could finally sink down without all the guard, because you'd be the guard for me, protecting me when I need. Let alone when I was somewhere farther away, Los Angeles, Anaheim, San Diego. Did I tell you how much I love when I was there in Anaheim, staying for a short vacation that's actually just a three-day long weekend? I could so easily imagine myself to stress out being out there, alone. But then, you were there with me, too. Every day, in the morning and at night, before I leave the hotel room or come back, the hotel was never home, but you were. I sit in you for a few minutes, I felt familiar, I felt safe, I felt home. You were all the sense of security for me. And now, I lost you. I didn't know if I could find that feeling with anyone or anything again. I was afraid I lost all that too. I was afraid that losing the real connection with the blood family, would make me lose any possibility of finding home outside home again. I was always so scared, and you made me not scared. Now, I'm alone again without you.

I sit in the parking lot for a long time after I said the final final last words to you. When I made my mind and accepted that this was the last I'd ever see you again. I sit there, just outside the repair shop, doing nothing, really. I cried a little, but not for very long. I stared outside the window, then I scrolled the phone, checking messages, purposeless, doing nothing, really. I think I was trying to wait for the tow truck to come, to see you leave, to know for sure that this was the last. But I already knew, I thought I already accepted it. I didn't really know what I was doing. Just that I needed the time. I sit there in that parking lot, for hours, I think. I was allowing myself the time to grieve, I think. It's also when I was sitting there in that parking lot, that I got the notification from the insurance company that I got all my compensation for losing you. The amount was decent. It eased the pain a little bit, but then I felt guilty again for feeling soothed, as if the money itself could measure what you meant to me, could fill in the hole in my heart for losing you. That was just another small good thing in the middle of all the pain and hurt. I'd still take this good thing. Coming to think about it, there really were many good things in the middle of all the pain, and I would forever be appreciated for them to ever show up in my life, and willing to stay even knowing what I really was like.

For the entire week following that, the third week after the accident, I threw myself into the busy shopping and test driving. Talking with the salesmen was always like a battle, so tiring, so consuming. I think I was just going through the motions. I wrote down at least a dozen final candidates on paper, and checked them out one by one. Scratched them out from the list one by one. I drove two three hours every day, as far as north of LA. I think, I was just trying to keep myself very very occupied, so I didn't have to think about the things I didn't want to think about, so I wouldn't have time for grief, or sadness, or the loss of you, or the connection to the blood family, or anything like that, anything in my head. If I exhaust myself with tasks that I have to go through, and order my body to just go and do them, and get so exhausted that I'd just pass out on bed after coming back, then the feelings would just be dumbed and it would hurt less. I think that's what happened after the loss of you, and I think that's still happening until now. I didn't give myself a moment of breath in May because of you, then in June for writing, then in July for conventions, then August for work, then September for the game, then October for both game and writing and work. I piled more and more things onto myself the second I sensed I might try to feel again, I made myself spinning on my heel so that I could stop feeling. It hurt too much to feel. It scared me to feel.

It helped to have a friend (or many friends) with me during that whole time. Check out those cars with her certainly helped, added a dash of joy and distraction to the whole dark time. By Friday afternoon, I decided on your descendent, and we were ready to sign. There were so many extra unnecessary displeasure just from that, but that was all those other shitty people's fault, and I got annoyed for weeks after that because of them, but that was really related to you, so I'd skip those.

On Saturday morning, 05/23, I drove two hours to pick up your descendent, and another two hours back. It was about a hundred miles away, in the middle of Orange County. I didn't know I'd get myself so much extra labor just to get one that's just like you, to remember you, to hurt less. If I switch that mileage display to the trip so I don't need to look at how much distance she's already driven, if I set up the maintenance page just the way I'm used to, if I set up the radio station favorite list just the same as before, then I really can pretend she is you.

I parked her where you used to sit, in the parking spot downstairs. I took photos for her. She's gorgeous, with a small imperfection because she's hurt by the shitty seller, but that's not her fault. She's just like you in every other way. I handmade her a demon-trapping sigil, putting under the trunk mat. I gave her the license plate holder that came from your corpse. It was distorted where you were hurt, as if I could still sense the blood from your wound on it. But I wanted to remember that, remember what happened to you, so I still gave it to her. How strange my mind worked, isn't it? I tried not to remember, but I tried to remember too. I hurt, and I don't want to feel hurt, but I also still want to feel the hurt. I tried so hard to pretend she's you, and then I tried equally hard to remember she's not you. I couldn't understand myself.

I still called her "Baby" out of you, even though I know I didn't mean you when I'm calling her. I tried different names for her, she's a lovely girl, a lady, a darling, a sweetheart, she's like a daughter to you, but nothing sticks, nothing felt right. At the end of the day, I still call her "Baby" and I felt guilty. I didn't know what I'm feeling guilty about. Maybe the fact that I was still trying to use her to pretend it's you, and it's just not fair to her, as if the only reason I'd love her is because she's like you. It's not fair to you too, but I also don't want to stop using the word "Baby" again. Dean fixed Impala so many times, replaced every single piece of structure more than once, was that still the same Impala anymore? The Theseus's Ship problem here. But he'd definitely say it's still his Baby. But then what makes that the same but you the different? I tried to tell myself your spirit was still here, with me, with the new girl, just on a new vessel, so it's still you, just like the angel with a different body. But it also doesn't feel right. I don't know how to do it right. I'm sorry.

I think, maybe I'm still stuck in the stage one. The denial still hasn't passed. I'm still trying to figure out different way to tell myself you weren't really gone, when your physical form was.

That's probably the reason so many people turn to spiritual or religious after the passing of a loved one, isn't it? If you could come up with a theory to help you explain the loss, or phrase it in a way that the loss stopped feeling like a loss, the pain became easier to handle, then you'd take it.

The new girl has been doing pretty well. Doing her job, being what she needs to be, taking me where I need to go. We spent five days in Anaheim too, just like you did with me and even longer, and I did still feel the sense of comfort and almost home, knowing I had her, when I was outside. But there was still something slightly off, missing, we didn't bond as well as you and me. I didn't know if it's because of me or her (certainly me). Maybe I was still withholding part of me, maybe because I was thinking, even just subconsciously, that if I stopped withholding, then you were really gone. And I still wasn't ready for that. Same with writing this memoir for you, but as long as I've been putting this off, I still ended up writing it. So maybe, just give me more time, I'd eventually get over it and accept her the way I did with you, and we'd bond, and we'd be good. And I'd still remember you, forever, because you were my first, you'd always be in a special place in my heart. I wonder if there's anything like a car Heaven. (Impala was in Heaven with Dean, so, there's that.)

I miss you.
