How long has it been? Since last time I watched Phantom of the Opera in New York, in Broadway, crying like the world was ending and there was no tomorrow and love was the most desperate thing in the world? Four years, perhaps? And here I am, pulled by the charisma of the show again, watching the Phantom of the Opera in theatre again. And this time, I'm in the very front row, right before the stage, watching their every single smallest twitch of facial expression, the change of looks in their eyes, all the glittering and flickering of their costumes, every inch of the fancy fabrics, every little details. Tearful but joyful, astounded by its beauty and grasped by its emotions again. This has been amazing. Unimaginably incredible. Even with the knowledge that it could and has startled me with its fabulousness, it still managed to take my breath away. It's just miraculous, making me run out of words to praise it or describe how incredibly beautiful and emotional it is. There are too many amazing things that I'm so afraid I'd forget some already before I can write them down.

I *have to* mention the unexpected interruption of the performance upfront as the first thing to remember it by, because I'm pretty sure no matter how many times I rewatch this show, this is pretty likely the only one I'd run into this very same incident, marking it the very unique experience as its own. Right after the show goes on to the part where the phantom gets furious and hangs the set manager and people in the show startle and scatter around, the performance in the show world is disrupted, and right at that precise moment, the performance itself, in the real world, suddenly announced that it's disrupted too. "Something unexpected happened," the narrative voice tells the audience to stay calm and seated, the show will resume shortly, but the curtain is drawn, the set has moved, and the audience lightings light up. It's almost as if the death of the person in the show is accidentally turning into a real accident and there is real unintended death happening in this very theatre that we sit in enjoying the story of the phantom, as if there is a real phantom living and existing in this very theatre, making the accidents and tragedies true, not afraid of killing, and we should be scared. And the show, as good as it is, can't afford to refund this many tickets, will have to resume the performance even with the death of a performer -- they always have understudies, don't they? So the show must go on. Seriously, I doubt they'd do anything different if that really realistically is a life-death accident, they would probably still keep the show continue -- counting each ticket price and total seat number and knowing even the cheapest ticket is more than $100, each show means more than 300 grands. So yeah, real accident or not, the show has to go on -- just like how it has to be, inside the story. So the interruption could only afford to be brief.

And that disruption only makes the whole experience surreal. For a moment, you can't tell if you're in the story or out of the story. The feeling is so unreal and immersive that I gets dazed. Am I in the reality or in the theatre in the story? Am I part of the story? Is the story really happening right around us, before our eyes? Did that unintended accident really turn into real and the tragedy happened before our eyes without us realizing?

There are so many theories during that thirty minutes of pause: the death of the actor, technical difficulties about the immersive surrounding sound effect that's supposed to happen in the next scene right after this, or maybe some issue to do with the special effect of the chandelier that's preparing to be dropping and exploding almost immediately after this scene. No one really knows what happened, not even the orchestra members, but even if it's just technical glitch, doesn't that also mean the story of there being a phantom in this very theatre might possibly be true? That it is the phantom who caused all this glitch to disrupt the show so that his demands can be met? That maybe after the show, the phantom would have a little talk with this very theatre's manager to discuss his payment that's overdue, and his help that makes this show so successful.

The sense of surreal doesn't fade, not even after the show resumed -- even then I was still out of it for a little longer before I can fully come back to the show. It really is a once in a life-time experience.

<br>

About the performance itself.

I saw so much little things that I wouldn't have noticed or been able to see, had I not sit in the very front row: I see how they frown and how they joke, how their eyes flatter and blink when they get anxious, or worried, or excited, or shy, or surprised. Or their little body languages. And all the beautiful, astonishing clothes and costumes in every single scene in all different angles. Oh, and all those little background story's performance, the ones that are not in the center focus of the stage at the time, like the first scene when the center stage has the theatre managers introducing the new patrons and having the conversation with the ballet teacher and mistress Madam Giry, the other actors and actresses and ballet dancers are just relaxing and stretching and chatting among themselves, from the corner closest to my seats, I had the huge interest watching Christine (before I even know she *is* Christine, I thought her best friend is Christine because she's so much more extroverted that she's stealing my attention) stretching and chatting with her best friend, using her body language to let us know what they're talking about, stretching and moving her body, asking her friend how she'd do those moves -- it's so hard to tell if it's part of the story or part of the real life where the lovely actors just take the moment to catch their breath and relax a little before the next scene catches on; it feels real in both worlds.

There's also the scene when all the theatre managers and Madam Giry and Raoul received notes and letters from phantom and they are reading and discussing whether to let Carlotta continue being the lead soprano in the next performance, during that long-ass debate and discussion, there in the corner (and it's the corner closest to me again) is Raoul reading and frowning at the letters, studying the strokes and signatures, thinking pensively, before he jumps back into the discussion and adding his singing into the big messy chorus. Those little frowns and thinking and lines of worries on his face, really making him look incredibly real and adorable, and I have to admit, he really does look beautiful and easy to the eyes. The beauty-biased brain of mine is so easily abducted by the aesthetics, and where there is beauty, where is my heart. I blame his beautiful boy face for stealing my heart and feelings away this time around -- same with Christine. The girl is just so vivid, so emotional, so easy to steal your heart just by the way she blinks, she raises her eyebrows, she smiles, she cries. She's a real performer. And I feel for her so easily.

Seeing the stage upfront almost always gives me the delusion that it's as if I'm back watching *Sleep No More* again, that immersive experience of following one little character and watching their role and side story in this whole big story. That's what happening here again too. Being so close to the stage, my eyes wander so many times to the background little characters, the extras and dancers, their added-in little side-plot moves and interactions, their pouts and smiles. I can see all those little characters, however small they are, they're not "tools of the story", they're real, they have their own stories, they have lives. The way they move, the way they show their emotions, it makes them all look so abundant and real, taking me away, making me almost feel drunk, floating away from the reality and really sinking into the world where the story takes place.

And all the in-between fake practicing shows inside the show, the ones that aren't exactly part of the main storyline, not directly had anything to do with the phantom, but with the fact that this is a story in the theatre and they're the performers for that theatre, so there had to be some shows that they're preparing and performing. I used to think of these as fillers, they're just something to fill in the plot gaps, something that's almost like a show-off, to let the audience see something different, to temporarily forget about the main storyline and had a moment of hallucination and confusion to think maybe they're watching a different show. Those fillers and rehearsals and shows-in-the-show really achieved everything they want to achieve, and turned out to be incredibly entertaining to watch: Like the very first scene, the first song and dance with Thailand-style curtain and elephant, the second one that looks like some royal setting, with the fancy comedic character that reminds me so many French royal family or revolution related musicals, like *Le Roi Soleil*, *1789*, *Moliere*, *Mozart*, all of those I love so much and enjoy so much, all of them have such amazing comedic fool character that's making me feel so familiar and intimate; and the last clip of *Don Juan*, finally something at least I can recognize the name of, though I'm not that familiar with the real *Don Juan* musical (I'd have to read the original story again now, and watch that musical again; I do remember I enjoyed its music), I can be convinced that clip is exactly from the real Don Juan musical. Each one of those clips shift so much from the vibe and style of the phantom-related storyline (like the scenes in the basement, the confront scene, the confession, the scary horror-vibe murder scenes), each of them so fancy and well-made, showing off more costumes than any other scenes (costing me some time and brainpower to be distracted wondering how many costumes they have in total), it's just so so entertaining to watch. The joy of shifting the mindset a little from the dark place, to be reminded they still have some different shows going on, and getting the chance to basically watch four different shows with one show ticket, I feel as if I'm taking advantage of them. I can't say enough about how amazed I got by this new finding and new passion of watching those in-between shows-in-the-show. They're just wonderous.

<br>

But if it's only that, that probably wouldn't be enough motivation for me to feel like I really *really* have to write this down *right this moment* or else I'd die or combust. -- There are too much feelings and emotions. Some bottled, some filled out. I cried. I'd cry every time I watch this show. I cried watching it the first time in Broadway, and I cried again this time round. But the funny thing is -- the point that surprised me most is -- the reason why I cry, the point that touch me the most, in these two times, is utterly and completely different, so different that I could say they're almost like opposite. And *that*, isn't something I expected. The shift and change in *me* and *my feelings* caught me off guard and get more surprised than anything I really see in the show directly.

I remember how hard I cried last time in Broadway, how much I resonated with Phantom, how much I hurt -- it's impossible to forget given the circumstance. That is a very particular point of time, very particular set of events, very specific little shift and change in the life, in the realm of feelings and emotions and relationship and friendship and love, that my heart and my feelings feel too raw, and the phantom just touched too directly. I remember thinking that's me, right there, in a story of three, being the one unselected, being the one left out, lonely, alone, unloved, unchosen, abandoned, left behind -- loving the girl so much, so desperately, but she has her love to go to; she'll always choose him over me, even though she will hug me and tell me she cares for me and sees me too, because she's so kind and maybe she feels bad, but she'd still leave, she'd still walk away with him, with the one she chose and that isn't me; she'd hold me for a brief second, and that's all the time and everything I could ever ask for, and she'd leave me, alone, leave me, and go live her own happy life, a life that wouldn't have me no matter what I do; and worst of all, I can see why, I can see she's making the right choice, she's choosing the one who really can give her a happy life, and that isn't me, because I couldn't, so she would never choose the other way round, and I have to let everything go, I have to let her go, I love her enough that I wouldn't ever think to keep her away from him because that would only hurt her, and I couldn't; so the only one I could hurt is just myself. I feel all the pain and loneliness in phantom, and I feel all of these things for myself. That's the second day after "the happy news" broke me, I believe, and I was still having a very very difficult time getting over it, getting out of it; I cried so much that whole day when I was just touring around New York, I cried so much the night before that too, I cried at the smallest things that remind me of my pain and ache, I cried when my bloody umbrella broke and small drizzle of rain comes down wetting me, I cried when I almost miss the bus to tour the city even though I've already been to this city so many times this hardly really matter, I cried at the top of the Empire State Building thinking about *Sleepless Seattle*, remembering that's a love story where loved ones almost miss each other but in the end they didn't, they find each other and get together, but I'm still alone and unloved and unchosen and would probably stay like this forever. And then there I was, sitting in the theatre alone, watching the man being rejected, being disgusted by the world, hiding from the world, hiding from the one he loved, and when he showed the love of his life his true face, she's afraid of him, she's disgusted by him, she doesn't love him back, and she flees from him. I just sit there, emerged by all the feelings of phantom, all the feelings of myself, and I cry, because I need to, because I have to let all those unspoken feelings out, because those are the feelings that I'd usually tell her about, but now that she becomes the reason I cry, I don't have anyone else to tell, so I just sit there, crying. All the feelings of abandonment, the rejection, the broken bond, the betrayal, the imagined drama that fits my narrative because I need to hear it and imagine it the way a story loves, fit the story I imagined for myself. All the feelings flood into my heart and it becomes too much to hold back -- I can't hold them back anymore and I don't want to. I cried for phantom and I cried for myself. I cried so hard that I can't even see the stage for so much part of the story, and all I can remember is I was feeling devastated, I was thinking I know how that pain feels like, I was feeling pain, so much pain, pain and loneliness and devastation that tears my chest apart, and I just can't stop crying. All the memory of the show is entangled with my feelings and emotions and love and what happened around that time and my thoughts of the life and my love at the time. Everything complicated, everything hurts. The story hurts so bad, it hurts so good. I love it for making me cry, I love it for making me seen, I love it for making me feel, even when feeling is hurting, even when I try so hard to suppress it, watching phantom hurting and in pain is letting myself hurt a little less, ache a little less. It's an outburst, it's a release. No matter how much of those feelings are unreasonable once my reasonable mind comes back online and casts them aside, shoving them in a box and decides not to look at it again, not to look at it too closely, not want to entangle the confusion and complication at all, all those feelings at that very time in that very place before that very show is very real and unforgetable.

But now, now I don't know if it's because the tour changed anything, or sitting in the front row allowed me seeing more of their facial expression and therefore more direct feelings and emotions of the characters, or I'm a different me now, this time, instead of feeling for phantom, I'm feeling so much for Christine and Raoul that the shift and change in the feeling and resonation and relate startled me. It could be the four years of growth has changed me, I'm no longer the old me anymore. I don't feel unloved and lonely anymore, because I know I'm not unloved or lonely -- I know I have friends who really do love me, I know I don't need a intimate partnership to feel loved and be a part of, I know I have a found family that cares for me like their own, I know the me now is happy (happi*er*) than the old me that I don't feel like a monster lurking in the dark anymore, I don't feel like hiding in the dark not ready to face the world anymore, the new me admittedly still has a million issues to fix, but the new me is more presentable, is more confident, is more complete -- so the broken man isn't the main center of my resonation anymore. Or, I know how politically incorrect it is to say, but it might also just be a part of the truth and reason that I can't lie to myself: my feelings shifts away from Phantom maybe because of the character not being the type that I feel naturally attracted to, aesthetically speaking, even the voice and sound is a touch too smokey for my like, but on the other hand, Raoul's actor and character's presence is just so unfairly good-looking that I can barely move my eyes away, I was so fully and easily attracted and falling in love with him the same way Christine does.

And with more attention to the story setting this time than last, I actually only now knows that Christine and Raoul had been friends and known each other since young and had been sweethearts in the past, which makes their love development so much more natural and easier than I thought it would be. The love between them comes easily.

On the other hand, Phantom's love becomes the unreasonable one in my eyes. I even start to doubt if the love they feel is really love. Phantom loves music more than Christine, and Christine for a too long time is more like just a media for his art and music, as his voice, as his way of expressing his feelings through the music, for his creation, but not for her as a real person with a soul. I believe Phantom starts falling in love with her as a person (or think that he's in love with her) after she starts showing him some kindness, something he's never had from the world and anyone else. But even then, they'd barely know each other outside topics of music and singing, they both think the other is the angel in their life, filling the blanks in their hearts created by the loss of something else: for Phantom that's the loss of everything, the hole the size of the world, even the music, the thing he relies on most, can't be achieved without someone special and capable to do it for him, and that person just so happened to be Christine because she happens to have a capable throat and voice. And Christine just lose her father and she's devastated and needs some recognition and tutoring and guidance, and Phantom just so happened to be there for what she needs, filling the new blank. Phantom confuses the appreciation and need with love, and Christine confuses her feeling toward Phantom with grief-induced dependence. Christine needed something or someone to lean on when she lost her father, she just needs someone at that vulnerable moment, and Phantom just so happened to be the one being there for her. She needs him, but there is no foundation for that need to turn into love. She may think she loves him too, because of how much she loves music and he's almost like a symbol for love, but that's analogy, that's not really love. There is no real love between the two of them, as a real soul and person. With the hiding and masks, Christine doesn't even have a chance to know Phantom as a person. And for Phantom, staying in the dark for so long, Christine is the light in his world, so it's so natural for him to get attracted by the fire and light, and reaching out, trying to touch the light. But for Christine, her light is Raoul, Raoul is there under the light where she can see all the time, being there when she needs him to. Everyone is attracted by the light in their life, then why would Christine even choose the dark one that is Phantom instead of the one staying in the same light as herself is, the young beautiful man who has the state and wealth and promise of a good life ahead of her?

I believe in Christine and Raoul's love story now, which means Phantom becomes the villain of the story from this POV: he's trying to abduct the girl he thinks he loves, taking her away from the one she loves and chooses, taking away her choice, her freedom, making decision for her, just like the world making decision for him all along, he's doing the worst things to her the way he's treated, and that's no love. He thinks he loves Christine, but apart from teaching her singing and trying to help her career (by forcing her into the position that he wants her to be before she thinks she's ready for), forcing things into her life when she's not ready or doesn't want at the time. He's using Christine to achieve his own dream, in the disguise of helping Christine to succeed. If Christine doesn't have her voice, would Phantom still love her? If not, then it's so clear that what he really wants and cares isn't her as a person, not remotely related to her for a long time.

And this time I feel so much for Christine because, what a brave young girl she is. She's strong, she tries so hard, she learns what she wants to learn, she is a joy to be with as a friend, she's compassionate, she's passionate, she's kind and funny, and clever, and resistant, and curious, and really really brave, and she can see clearly. She seeks her happiness through freedom and choices made by her own hands. She walks the path that she knows she wants to walk. She doesn't need someone pave a red carpet for her, certainly not that someone do all the evil things in her name to make sure she gets what *he* wants (which isn't necessarily what she wants, even if it is, she surely can fight to get that herself.) She needs to fight for her career and life and love, and she makes decision and pursues all of it all by her own decision. But even though she's hurt and scared and disgusted by what phantom did, see phantom for the inner real monster that he is without judging his disfigured appearance, knows reasonably that phantom really is a violent and bad person, she still hears his side of the story, she still sees him, sees his pain and anger, the unfairness of the world and life to him, that all he ever wants is to be loved. So she hugs him, giving him what he could use (not all that he wants, but something he can use to think back on and remember), without being bothering by his disfigure. That's a hug of appreciation, regret, compassion, and sincere hope and wish that he can get better, be treated better, find someone love him the way he needed. She loves him, but it's more a love of kindness, like the way she would love anyone else, she's just continuing showing him her kindness and appreciation, because she has too many hearts to share and enough love to give, and there is a man in the world that doesn't have any, so she can give him some, warm his life a little. She can't fix him, she shouldn't need to, but she can still shine his life a little, giving him a little light that he never get to have before. Her love isn't romantic, has never been, and has no base to be one. It's just kindness and friendliness, and it's the best she can offer. So it's everything he takes. (What Phantom really really needs is therapy, but Christine shouldn't have to be his therapist. It's not Christine's duty to fix the broken man that Phantom is. And it's her right choice to walk away from the monster that Phantom gets turned into by the world.)

Even if phantom's feeling really is love -- Love isn't always the solution. Problem isn't automatically solved when there is love in the equation. Sometimes -- too many times -- love is the problem. I learned it recently, and I learned it again today.

And about Raoul. It's so interesting that I've never really paid enough attention to him -- which is just unfair. As if he's just a side character, a functional tool for the story, to be put there just to create the tension and dilemma between Christine and Phantom, as if it's a two-people story instead of three, as if his feelings don't matter, can't be seen -- And if I wasn't in the front row watching his facial expression and feeling his feelings, that probably is going to be the case. But just like other side character that turns out to be so abundant and realistic and emotionally full, Raoul becomes visible to me too. He overjoys when he reunites with Christine, he's so enthusiastic when he wants to invite Christine out for celebration that first night they meet again, he's making all those promises when they realize something dangerous is prominent and Christine got scared, he makes himself someone Christine can rely on and cry over his shoulder, he's clever and comes up with the plan from all those letters written by Phantom, he's brave following Christine into the basement to save her and confront Phantom, he's sacrificial and martyr to ask Christine to leave and save herself when he thinks Phantom's rope has got him and there's no hope for him to leave the cave but Christine might still have a chance, he's so hurt when he watches Christine hugging and kissing Phantom that he can't move his eyes away even as he's potentially still hanging on the rope and left to die (He's so beautiful and devastated in that scene, as if he can't imagine the girl he loves betrayed him to love another distorted man who's also a monster, but also he loves Christine too much that he can understand her and know it's not a betrayal, she's not choosing Phantom over him, she's saying goodbye to Phantom, but the natural human part of him is still hurt and will remember that there will forever be a small part of Christine deep down that misses Phantom one way or another, metaphorically or realistically, and he can't take that part away from Christine and he can't do anything about it. In that sense, it's as if Phantom still steals his girl from him, even just a little piece, even just a moment's time. So he's still hurting and aching, suffering quietly alone).

I fully didn't expect myself to relate to Raoul as much as I did this time and feel his hurt this time in the Christine-kissing-Phantom scene (don't get me wrong, I still can feel Christine's hurt in that scene, and a little of Phantom's stunned dumbness too, which mixing into a big pool of complicated feelings that's very difficult to unentangle). I trust his love toward Christine is real and pure, and that long time of acquittance since childhood and the reconnect after adulthood is enough to build a solid foundation for their love, they must find out they have more things in common, and that they want the same thing for their future. He knows Christine as a person, instead of just as a voice, a symbol of music; and Christine knows him too the same way, which is something really fundamental and necessary in any relationship in my eyes: honesty and trust and safety is just the absolute requirement. Christine doesn't feel safe around Phantom anymore (even though Phantom promised to never going to hurt her, but he did actually hurt her and scared her, not because of what he looks like, but from what he does), but she feels safe around Raoul, so that's all the reason she'll need to choose Raoul and leave with him. I'd forever ruminate the haunted look on Raoul's face in the scene when Christine kisses Phantom, when Phantom is too surprised and scared to even hold her back, and Christine has to move his hands to her waist to let him know it's okay to hold her back, this is she giving him her consent and permission, but as Phantom starts holding her, she's pulling back and leaving. The push and pull, the looks on Raoul's face, the hurt, the understanding, the desperation, the belief that he'd still die by Phantom's hand, all the complicated feelings from all three of them, resonating in my chest all at once, I think they just broke me.

I was thinking *Casablanca* too in that scene, thinking that's also a story of three people, one girl and two men, the girl loved both men, and both men love her, one is the right answer and one is the passion but wrong answer. The relationship is complicated, but the most difficult and unfair and dramatic part is -- the story is told from that wrong answer man's point of view, and that man has to be charming and easy to relate, and the reader and audience just has to love him and want her to choose him. And that's the man that charms the most and loves more, and it's always the man loves more making the final call and backing off, leaving the girl to go to the best choice for her life and future, choosing what's best for her, not what *he* wants, let her go, make her happy, give her a better life. It's like that very old fable folk story about two mothers fighting for one kid, and it's always the one loves harder that backs off.

If it's only that kissing scene, that haunted look on Raoul's face, the tearful smile on Christine's face, the pain on Phantom's face, I might not cry for the story. My feelings are on the brim of filling out, but I still managed to contain it. But then Christine runs back, leaving Raoul somewhere outside, somewhere safe, she comes back alone, coming back to see Phantom one last time, almost giving him hope, but she's just coming back to return his ring. There is real pain on her face this time, so much more raw and painful than when Raoul was in the same room. She needs this one moment of being with Phantom, alone, without an audience, especially without the audience of her fiancé. And she can finally let him see her real raw emotion and pain. She comes back alone because now, seeing Phantom let Raoul go, she can trust him not to hurt her even though they're alone. She finally doesn't fear him, but she's so sorry and so painful that she knows she'll have to hurt him, but she can't do anything about it. She's sorry, and she really doesn't want it to become this. This moment, this very last moment before she runs out again, with tears in her eyes, I think this moment, is the first moment that there is real love happening between them. Only happening when it's ending, and it's goodbye and farewell, and it's heartbroken. Farewell is so much more powerful than confession, than "I love you". (Oh God, this again.)

In that very scene, so many people were crying for Phantom's "I love you", only I know I was crying for the pained look on Christine's face. That girl really performed unbelievably perfectly. I wouldn't have it any other way.

I would like to think, the new me relating to Christine and Raoul, is because I'm coming to the light side now, I'm under the light and sunshine, knowing I'm loved, being the more wholesome version of myself. And I'm happy how this new me turns out.

I wonder who I'd relate to next time I rewatch this show, and if I'd feel something different again, feel like another new me again.
