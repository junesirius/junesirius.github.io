Heard it first in the World Con about writing, but then realize it's so very true to apply to the real world, too: Happy ending isn't the same as satisfying ending.

You can have a happy ending that's still not satisfying. And you can have a satisfying ending that's not necessarily happy.

Happiness isn't the same as satisfaction. And it's actually the satisfaction that we're looking for, but we mistake it for happiness, because many times they overlap, but they don't always.

Hope can make a far better satisfying ending than happy ending. And happy can mean so many different things to different people, so one single happy ending could very easily be viewed as sad ending, and therefore, far from satisfying. I'm thinking about *Stranger Things* all this time when I was typing down the last three paragraphs. Don't even want to think about *Supernatural*.

That panel about the endings is a really good panel. But I know I'd still struggle with endings when it's my turn.

*The Boys* has a satisfying ending. *Casablanca* is the ceiling and textbook level example of how to write a hopeful satisfying ending that doesn't have to be happy for every single character, but the ending is just right and characters get what they deserve and audience finishes the movie with a smile and a soothed heart. Hope is a really powerful thing.

<br>

When I say this can also apply to other aspects of life beyond life, I'm thinking about how do I evaluate my current mood. (Since we don't have an "ending" in the life, it's just a discussion of happy vs satisfying, then.) And I realize I'm not unhappy, but I'm not happy either. What I am feeling, that feeling is unsatisfaction. I'm unsatisfied. That's why I'm itching. Itching to move, itching to do something, itching to do this and that and run myself to ground. I'm unsatisfied. But am I pleased that I'm here? I sure am. I'm glad, I'm pleased, it could be so much worse but instead I'm in this very fine position. I should be content, but instead, I'm still hoping for more.

No wonder in the very old Zen lessons there are topics about how to control your greed, limit your want. It's "what you want" ultimately decide how happy you can be -- and once again, the "happy" here actually means "satisfied". People confuse those two so easily, so frequently.
