A row of guitars on the pawn shop wall, behind metal bars and doors and security locks

Different colors and shapes

All with so much personalities and passions

Who are those young musicians and dreamers who had to give up their dreams and love for music and pawn their beloved guitar to this pawn shop?

But they also still can't give up on the dream entirely, because, why pawn shop? Why not used item market? Second-hand market?

Pawn shop, where you can still get a chance to get it back, if you get enough luck, if you get enough money just in time, you'd have them back

Who's the poor young musician who got so desperate and hungry and no money for your next meal, and pawning your beloved guitar become the last resort for your hunger? But you still, still couldn't fully give up, still has the hope that you'll find the money in time, maybe tomorrow, maybe the day after tomorrow, and as you hand your beloved guitar (that was mocked by so many of your friends back then when you first got it for its weird shape but you love it so much because you think it represents you so perfectly, and it really does), you promised yourself and your baby that you'd come back for it. You prayed no one would get it before you can, because no one is supposed to like it the way you do, because no one should match with it as perfectly as you are to it. But there's always a chance that it would be sold to someone, be taken away from you permanently. Hurry, the owner of the pawn shop tell you kindly, things sold quickly here. And you nod, sad and in pain, but still determined. You'd get the money to get it back, just tomorrow. Tomorrow things will be better. Tomorrow you can find the food and job and you'll have money for it. Tomorrow you can pick up your dream again.

The tomorrow that comes doesn't get any better. You go back to the pawn shop and glance at your guitar. It's still on the wall, beside other twenty that just look as wary, as lonely, as passionate in a previous life before they end up here. You are relieved to find it there, still. And you urge yourself to try harder, to find the job you need, to find the money you need, to get food and still enough left.

Maybe you have slightly better luck the next day, maybe you don't. But even if you did, whatever you get isn't enough. You have to think for your meal the day after. You go back to the pawn shop, and your guitar is still there, hanging high on the wall, tied up by some leather string, like a cuff locking it to some cage. You promise yourself again.

And the next day, and the next day. And one day, you get too tired and you forget to go back to the pawn shop. The next day when you remember, you realize you already start forgetting the tune and notes that you once were so familiar with. You go back to the pawn shop and glance from outside the window. Your guitar is still there. There's a banner in the shop, they're having a sales right now, everything in the shop is twenty percent off.

Things get cheaper, and things get sold faster. Your guitar might get bought by someone walking in randomly real soon. You find your heart pounding, but it's from the old habit nerve, not even from fear again. You realize you don't feel the same kind of excitement when looking at your guitar as before. You realize you even stop calling it your beauty, your love, your baby. As if a lover that you're starting to grow strange, that the passion and flame is cooling down.

You allow yourself walk into the pawn shop, something you don't usually do. The owner of the shop recognize you and greet you. Wondering aloud if you're trying to pawn something else or to get something back. You say you're just looking around.

That's the truth. But really you're trying to squeeze your eyes and see the price tag on your guitar. You can't quite see it clearly from this distance away from the counter. The salesman comes to tell you after they finish helping the purchase of someone else who just got a big package from the shop. They see where you are squeezing and tell you the price. They ask if you want them to take it down the wall and let you have a try.

Your fingers itch at the suggestion. You haven't touched any guitar for so long. You want to. Your heart skip a beat thinking maybe you can touch your beloved guitar again, one more time, something you haven't done in so long.

You tell them no need with a smile. The price is still so much above what you have right now. You can't get it back. And you don't want to make it even worse for you walking out of the pawn shop. You'd already part with it once, knowing the feeling that you have to let go of it and someone will take it from your hand. You don't want to do it again.

You smile and tell them have a good day and walk out of the pawn shop.

The next time you glance from across the window is many days later. And many more days later. And many months and years later. At some point you finally realize there is no point. You don't have enough money for it still. And even if you do, you are no longer that young you who want to pursue the music as a life anymore. Somewhere down the road, the flame of that young passion is already extinguished.

And no one has tended to that guitar for long, the board must have dried, the strings must have become loose, everything would be in a bad condition for it to be the same as memory of it once were.

You're not the same anymore, and your guitar is not the same anymore. The life is not the same, the world is not the same. There is no point keep going back and keep holding on to it anymore. So you stop going back to that pawn shop, stop glancing from outside the window to check if that familiar shape is still on the wall, stop feeling sorry and guilty and pain and helpless and relieved all at the same time. You just decide, that day, that this dream of playing music for a life is really over for you.

That day you walk away from the pawn shop. And you realize, after so many years, this is the moment that you really lost that guitar. Not all those years ago when you pawned it, but this moment, when you give up getting it back. And it's the end of that dream and hope and promise you made to your young self.

You don't feel sad. Or maybe you do, just a little bit, but think, this is just part of growing up, this is just part of the life.

You still have dream, sometimes. In the dream, you are still that young you, still knows how to play guitar. Your fingers dance between the strings, the color of the guitar is always the same as you remembered, burning like flame, shape like music. Your real fingers twitch in your sleep, covered in callouses. You don't know if you still remember how to play, but in those dreams at night, your fingers still do on your behalf. You indulge yourself in those dreams just a little longer before waking up. It's still too early outside, but you have to work now, because you have a job and a life now, that you have worked so hard to earn, that cost your guitar and so many other things, but at least now you know you have a place to stay and a meal to eat. You get out of the bed, already forget about that last dream.
