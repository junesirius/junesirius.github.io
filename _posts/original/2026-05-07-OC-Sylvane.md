### Origin story and past experience

came to birth right after a huge disaster to the clan, deemed back luck (cause of the disaster) by her own people, discriminated and hated and excluded by her own people for no reason

half-elf from mother side, half-human from father side

human father is a forest ranger before he disappears, but was an old sailor before that, had been traveling around but now settled in the woods and cares for the forest, has adventure stories to tell. but passed away now because of short human life span (or died fighting the villainous human that's hurting the woods? but none of the elves know except for his mate and daughter) (or went missing and they don't know what happened to him).

elf mother born and grow in the woods, but always curious and rebellious, has a desire for adventure and loves sea, loves listen to the human's adventurous sailor story. the human has such shorter life than she has but so much more amazing life story to tell, amazed her and attracted her, that's what she wanted to do or want their offspring to experience, but she couldn't for herself because of objected by the clan. but still rebellious enough to choose to mate with him and give birth, but her life and the child (Sylvane)'s life got tough after her mate passed away of old age and her clan consider her a betrayal and the child is the proof and symbol of the bad luck that caused them disaster (e.g. earthquake or human's deforestation, the clan hates human, but she knows not all human are the same, like *Avatar*)

Growing up is tough for Sylvane, even though she's loved and protected by her mother and her father (when he's around, but he's not welcomed by the clan so he can't stay long, and he has fight to do, and he can't ask his mate to come with him, because if she leaves, she'll never be able to return to the clan and her parents/family), she knows and can feel the exclusion and hatred from all the other members of the clan. Most other elvish kids don't dare to approach her even if they want, because they're warned by their parents and family not to. So she kinda grows tough and get used to and enjoys solitude and loneliness.

She doesn't have many friends, so mostly she's friends with the woods, she listens to and talks with trees and animals. But she does have an elvish friend who doesn't care about what the rest of the clan says and willing to hang out with her. But she worries being with her would make her unpopular too, so she tries to not let others see them together, and urges her to go back to her home before anyone sees. The friend is very nice and friendly to her in the way no one else does, Sylvane thinks she's in love with her, but also thinks she's not romantically interested, just trying to be a good friend, so she never said anything and just hang out like a good friend. But suppressed emotion is hard, the more they spend time each other the harder it is to suppress, so in the end Sylvane decides to leave the clan and go travel the world, to see the sea that her mother longs to see so much but never really did (and send postcard when she's finally near the sea), to live the life that she heard her mom tells her about where her dad goes (and try to look for her dad; the reason why her mom can't leave is to wait in case her dad finally goes back to the clan, to protect him from the rest of the clan).

She had to hide her ears and identity when she's with humans, because otherwise there would be too many questions and some (many) humans would have evil thoughts toward her because that's what humans were taught about young beautiful elf girls and would try to drug her and distain her. But she's vigilant enough to protect herself and very good at combat, so those men never achieved their dirty attempt. But that's also how the ideation of men gets more and more disappointing in her mind and therefore she solemnly swear to herself that she'd never be with a human man or boy the way her mother with her father, and she'd always only keep her romantic love to the childhood elvish friend that she believed she'd never be reciprocated. But she's okay with it.

For her adventures, she helps sailors navigate storms by reading the sky. She listens more than she spoke. She journals down the interesting people she met and the stories she heard, and writes stories of her own too, sometimes when she's in good mood, even tell others her stories (but when she does, people/humans usually laugh at her and get drunk), she accepts that she'll never be fully understood and it's okay.

She wanders tide pools collecting strange shells and dead things washed ashore after tempests. She becomes a legend story in seaside town as the girl who could tell when bad weather (or bad news) was coming before anyone else senses it. But because of that, she's seem as bad luck again, as if she's the one bringing those bad things to them, and treated unfairly again. But it's nothing new to her, so she just moves on to the next new town where no one knows who she is.

Most people who come close to her, as she gradually learns, because they need something from her: guidance, comfort, healing, warnings, entertainment of the stories, company, and afterwards they return to their ordinary lives without her, but she's okay with that too. She thinks as long as she can offer something and get those brief moments of interaction, that's good enough for her.

On her way following the clues she gathered from the letters her mother showed her that were sent by her missing father, she starts learning more things she never thought she would. More mysterious legend that she thought were only childhood stories, but are real. She studies ruins, forgotten shrines, strange disappearances, old myths sailors whisper about after midnight. She helps people because she knows what it feels like to be frightened and unseen. She sleeps lightly, trusts slowly, and keeps detailed journals because she's afraid important things vanish when nobody records them. At night, she watches the stars compulsively, and seeks meaning from things that seem not to have any meaning at all, but she insists on believing they do, and she'll learn them one day.

There's one myth that always troubles her but she can't seem to ever decipher it, the myth about a missing star and the return of a black star. She found some old journals that may or may not scribbles by some drunk sailors, that contains strange annotations:

- constellations that moved impossibly
- references to places where the veil thins
- notes about stars disappearing from the sky one by one
- a prophecy: "if the black star returns, do not let her face it alone."

She's somehow convinced those are references to her and related to how and where she can find her missing father (or dead, as many has told her, and her mom also kinda already accepted it), but she still has hope.

She's been hurt so many times by men and women, human and elf, that she begins not trusting people easily, but she knows how to appear friendly and charming and empathetic, so she can still get along with them, but stops opening up easily.
