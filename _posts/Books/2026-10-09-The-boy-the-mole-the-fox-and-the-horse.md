I read the books again just now. Not the second time.

I'm surprised how much I cling to them, how much I love the books (the both of them), how much they comfort me, calm me, and how easily they can make me cry even when I thought I'm handling everything okay.

They're not even deep words. The lines are simple. Cheap, even, in the way that they almost look repetitive from having been said by so, so many books and works, even the phrasings aren't anything new, aren't anything fancy. Things about love, and home, and friends, and kindness. We have seen them everywhere. But we also haven't seen them enough. I got reminded so many times and I'd be startled to find out I almost forget them, the easiest things. They're the cliche things that we need them to be cliche, that we need to hear them often enough to never forget them. But the way the brains work, of course we'd still forget about them from time to time, that's why we need to go back to the book and read the random pages and get reminded again, usually when we're least expecting it.

It's almost always different things hitting me at different moments. I cry at different pages every time. The four characters in the book (five, if you count the bad slimy thing that happens in your head and lies to you, but we're only counting the good things here), it's so easy to see they are all just an aspect of your own head. I can see myself in all of them, and if I remember correctly, I read or watched it somewhere that Charlie said he can see himself in all of them too. The boy is the main self, the one that walks and observes, the one that's with the most POV of the life, the center of the story, the core of the sense of "self", the navigator of this ship. But also always so young and curious, have so many questions, so lost, so confused, so worried and afraid, so lonely, has so much love but so little for himself, need so much love and support and friend to keep going. And I can see every single little bit of these things in me. And I can see the artist Charlie must have such a small boy inside him, or else he wouldn't even have been able to draw or write or think any of that.

The mole is a cheerful dedicated little thing. Comedic. Cake-centered. He brings so much joy and joke, lightening the mood, adding the color, content about the life, live at the moment. He knows what he wants and he's happy with it. He's like the squirrel in the "Ice Age" for that pine for all these years over the series, until he gets it in the very last one. When I say "adding the color", I literally mean the number of colors on the page. Even though many times the mole's answer gets predictable, you'd know to whatever philosophical or difficult life questions, there's always an simple answer from the mole, and it's always the cake. But it still manages to become surprising and funny, and you'd laugh in the middle of deep pensive moments, you feel your heart lighten, because he's even more like a child than the boy. If the boy is like a grade-school child, the mole is like a kindergarten child, only living for the moment, but sometimes the deepest truth of the life, of the philosophical questions, does exist in kindergartener's answer. Of course he can get deep and touching and adult-like too, but for the most of the times, we really only just need his dedication for the cakes. When we forget what we're searching for, looking for, fighting for, maybe it's time to take some steps back and remember the mole inside of us, maybe all we ever really want and love is just a piece of cake (or many), and it's actually easy to get, and when we allow ourselves to get it, we'd be happy. We'd be reminded that happiness is actually simple, and don't forget that just because we're still trying to go somewhere else. Live the life as it is. Like the mole said, "carpe diem", it means "don't leave to tomorrow the cake you could eat today." I don't believe anyone wouldn't smile at the mole's answer. (And that pure child's joy seeing the mole's dream cake shop. Remember that kid inside your heart. Remember the mole in you.)

The fox is a quiet one, but he's still there. Charlie's own words in the introduction of the books already said that, the fox has been hurt by the life. But he's still there. He doesn't say much, but when he speaks, those are important things worth listening to. He watches the world warily in the way I'm far too familiar with. He's hurt in a way I'm far too familiar with. He doesn't need to say what kind of hurt and past you need to have to be like that, those who have seen it, know it far too well. And just by having the fox around, we see those parts inside us too. The hurt part, the trauma part, the adult part that has experienced too much and don't want to say, but still want to be seen. So the fox comes, circling the boy and the mole quietly, and he stays. As much as you want to stay pure and innocent and cheerful, as much as you like to have only the boy and the mole, the real world and the real life is still out there, so the pain and hurt is still there, and sometimes way too deep. As much as you want to will them to disappear, want to get over the pain and past, want to grow out of it, you know it's still a part of you, and you know the hurt has already been too deep and it becomes part of you. It becomes who you are, and how you would do certain things. You'd still lick your wound because it's never really recovered. And you become quiet, and too careful. Those who's been hurt badly, has the look of having been hurt badly. You can just see it. Just like you can just see the fox's hurt from the way he walks, he talks, he circles around, he studies the situation before approaching, he being so easily scared but pretends he's not, he wants but doesn't want to allow himself want, until he gets quietly accepted and he becomes comfortable to stay. You can just see everything right there, because the pain and past and trauma leaves a mark. But it doesn't mean you'd be broken forever, it doesn't mean all aspects of you are broken. You can be hurt and quiet like fox, but that's still only just one of many creatures and beings inside you. You are not the storm and the storm will pass, and in the same way, you are not the fox, it's just part of many things. You are not the pain and hurt and past. You are bigger than that.

The horse is the last thing to come, and the biggest and most beautiful thing they've ever seen. He's so devastatingly beautiful and wise, and comforting. You can rely on him on a stormy day. It's just too beautiful and touching that it has to make me tear up again. The horse is probably the most adult part of the whole journey up till now. The part you gradually grow to put as a mechanism to help you live throughout the day like a real responsible adult living the world, say the proper words, do the proper things, hold everything together. The one that holds everything together. The one that can allow the other little ones, the boy, the mole, the fox to lean to, to rely on. The one that has enough reasoning to help you stay sane, to remember you still have friends, you are loved, even in the middle of the worst storms. It's the mechanism and sensible reasoning mind that grow into place with so much time of being helpless but wishing for someone or something to rely on, and ultimately forming the shape inside your own mind, to be strong enough to give yourself the support that you would have seek from others. You should still have friends, like all of them have been trying to remind you, but with the analogy of all of them being a part of you, then the horse is the most strong one coming to be after the age and year and accumulation of the past and storm. He's there because the other three needs him, but he also needs the other three so he wouldn't need to always always keep everything together.
